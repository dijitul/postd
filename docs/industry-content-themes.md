**Version:** 1.0

**Purpose:** Content theme library for all 7 business categories. Provides evergreen post ideas, UK seasonal content calendars, CTA formulas, and local community angle suggestions. Used by the Weekly Evergreen source type and as context enrichment for all cascade source types.

## How to Use This Document

When generating evergreen content, the AI content engine selects from the relevant industry section based on the business's onboarding category. The seasonal calendar is used to prioritise which themes are most timely. Rotate through evergreen ideas to avoid repetition — track recently used themes in the `recent_topics` field.

## Category 1: Restaurant / Cafe

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **The story behind a signature dish** — Why does the business make it? Where did the idea come from? What makes theirs different? People love origin stories.

2. **A day in the kitchen before opening** — The prep, the mise en place, the quiet before the rush. Behind-the-scenes content from the kitchen is consistently high-performing for food businesses.

3. **Meet the team** — A genuine spotlight on a staff member. Not a formal bio — a fun fact, why they love working there, what they always order off-menu.

4. **The sourcing story** — Where do the ingredients come from? Is anything locally sourced, seasonal, or from a nearby supplier? Provenance content resonates strongly with UK food audiences.

5. **Our most asked question, answered** — "Do you do gluten-free?" / "Can we book for large groups?" / "Do you have parking?" Address it with warmth and clarity.

6. **A tip from the chef** — One genuinely useful cooking tip, herb pairing, or kitchen trick. Educational content builds authority and gets saved and shared.

7. **The seasonal specials process** — How do they decide what goes on the specials board? Take the audience behind the creative decision.

8. **Customer favourite spotlight** — "This dish has been on our menu since day one, and here's why it's staying." Community around a beloved item.

9. **Why we're not a chain** — The independent difference. What does being a local independent restaurant actually mean for the customer? Celebrate the things chains cannot do.

10. **A gentle re-introduction to the business** — For new followers who've recently discovered the page: who are you, where are you, what do you stand for? Told like a story, not an advert.

- Reference local landmarks in seasonal content ("as the {local park} starts to look beautiful this spring...")

## Category 2: Retail

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **The "what's just landed" reveal** — New stock arrival content. Show the unpacking, the display being set up, the first look. The process is as compelling as the product.

2. **Why we stock this brand/product** — The story behind a buying decision. A buyer who is passionate about what they stock is more persuasive than any ad.

3. **The gift guide** — "Looking for something for someone who has everything?" Specific, curated, genuine. Works any time of year, not just Christmas.

4. **Behind the buying process** — How do they decide what to stock? Trade shows, supplier relationships, customer requests? This is genuinely interesting to retail customers.

5. **The most popular item right now** — Social proof and trend-riding in one. "We can't keep this in stock" content works extremely well.

6. **How to style / use this product** — Educational content that creates desire through knowledge. Relevant to clothing, homewares, gifts, and beyond.

7. **The return story** — "This item sold out last year, and we couldn't resist bringing it back." Nostalgia and popularity combined.

8. **Customer styling / usage photo** — With permission, showcase how a customer has used or worn something. Real people, real use, real credibility.

9. **The independent retail difference** — What does shopping with a local independent retailer actually give you that a big-box chain does not? Advice, curation, service, connection.

10. **What's selling right now and why** — Trend commentary from the shop floor. Genuine retail insight from someone who watches buying patterns every day.

## Category 3: Trades

(Plumbers, Electricians, Builders, Decorators, Roofers, Landscapers, Joiners, and all related skilled trades)

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **The before and after** — The single most effective content type for trades. Show the problem, show the solution. Even a modest job looks impressive when framed well.

2. **A warning sign to look out for** — "If you see this in your home, here's what it might mean." Genuinely useful, positions the tradesperson as an expert who cares.

3. **A job we're particularly proud of** — Not just "here's what we did" but why this one was satisfying. The tricky problem, the creative solution, the happy customer.

4. **The most common mistake we see** — Honest, helpful, and a gentle case for using a professional. Not preachy — told with empathy for DIY enthusiasts who got in over their heads.

5. **A day on site** — What does the average day actually look like for a tradesperson? The early start, the materials, the problem-solving. People are fascinated by skilled work.

6. **How to find a good tradesperson** — Counter-intuitive: a tradesperson advising people how to find a good tradesperson. Shows confidence and puts the customer's interest first.

7. **The myth vs reality** — "People often think X, but actually..." Correct a common misconception about the trade. Education builds authority.

8. **Seasonal maintenance tip** — "This time of year, we recommend checking..." Genuinely useful seasonal advice that positions the business as proactive and expert.

9. **A customer situation resolved** — Without identifying details, describe a callout scenario. "We got a call last week from a customer in Mansfield whose..." Relatable, reassuring, professional.

10. **Why we love this trade** — Genuine passion for the craft. Why did they get into it? What is still satisfying about it after years of doing it?

### Local Community Angle Suggestions

## Category 4: Professional Services

(Accountants, Solicitors, Financial Advisers, Consultants, Architects, HR Professionals, Marketing Agencies, and similar)

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **The question we get asked most** — "The most common question we hear from new clients is..." Answer it with clarity and no jargon. Establishes expertise and provides immediate value.

2. **A case study (anonymised)** — "A client came to us with this problem. Here's what we did and what happened." The outcome story is more compelling than a services list.

3. **Debunking a myth** — "A common misconception in {industry} is..." Correcting wrong assumptions builds authority and shows the business is confident enough to educate.

4. **The "what to look for" checklist** — How should someone evaluate whether a {professional service provider} is right for them? Counter-intuitively, this builds trust and attracts the right clients.

5. **A change in law, regulation, or industry practice** — And what it means for small businesses or individuals. Timely, useful, positions the firm as on top of developments.

6. **Why we started the business** — The founding story. What gap did they see? What frustrated them about how things were being done? Genuine origin stories create human connection.

7. **What we wish clients knew before they came to us** — "If we could tell every new client one thing, it would be..." Empathy-forward content that respects the client's perspective.

8. **A local business spotlight** — Recognise a complementary local business. Shows community-mindedness and creates potential reciprocal goodwill.

9. **The process, demystified** — "Here's exactly what happens when you come to us, step by step." Reduces anxiety about engaging professional services. Very effective for B2B.

10. **Behind the credentials** — Not a boast — a genuine explanation of what a qualification or accreditation means and why it matters for the client. Educational and reassuring.

## Category 5: Health & Beauty

(Hair Salons, Barbers, Beauty Therapists, Nail Technicians, Spas, Aesthetic Clinics, Massage Therapists, and similar)

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **A treatment spotlight** — Not just what it is, but how it feels, what it does for the client, and who it is right for. Sensory, experience-led content.

2. **The transformation reveal** — Before and after content (with client permission) remains the most effective content format for hair and beauty. Show the work, celebrate the result.

3. **A tip from the professional** — One specific, actionable piece of advice that clients can use at home. "Here's what we recommend for maintaining colour between appointments." Saves the client money and builds loyalty.

4. **Meet the team** — A genuine spotlight on a therapist or stylist. What do they love about the job? What is their signature treatment?

5. **The product we can't live without** — Authentic recommendation of a product the team genuinely uses. Not an advert — an honest endorsement.

6. **Busting a beauty myth** — "You might have heard that X. Here's the truth." Correcting misconceptions with professional authority.

7. **The new treatment introduction** — When a new treatment or service is added to the menu, introduce it with context: why this, why now, who is it for, what can they expect?

8. **Client self-care tips for between visits** — "Here's how to make your {treatment} last as long as possible." Generous, expert, client-focused.

9. **The booking system tip** — When are the quieter times to book? When do the most popular slots fill up? Help clients navigate the diary. Transparency builds trust.

10. **A day in the salon / spa** — The atmosphere, the smells, the music, the conversations. Lifestyle content that makes the reader want to be there.

## Category 6: Automotive

(Garages, MOT Centres, Car Dealers, Car Valeting, Tyre Fitters, Auto Electricians, and similar)

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **The MOT warning sign** — "Here are five things that will fail your MOT that you can easily check yourself." Genuinely useful, builds trust, does not feel like a hard sell.

2. **A job we're proud of** — A repair that required real skill or creative problem-solving. Told from the technician's perspective. Trade passion content.

3. **Seasonal car care checklist** — "Before the winter sets in, here's what you should check on your car." People genuinely need this information and trust a local garage to give it straight.

4. **The honest answer to a common question** — "Does revving a cold engine damage it?" / "How often do you really need an oil change?" Genuine, no-jargon answers.

5. **The myth vs reality of car servicing** — "Main dealer vs independent garage: what's the truth?" An honest comparison that confidently makes the case for the independent.

6. **What goes into a service** — Demystify the process. What does the technician actually do during a service? Most car owners have no idea. Show them.

7. **A vehicle rescue story** — "We had a car come in last week that..." Relatable, humanising, demonstrates expertise.

8. **The product or part we trust** — Why do they use certain brands of oil, tyres, or parts? Professional endorsement from a mechanic carries real weight.

9. **EV and hybrid advice** — Growing relevance for all garages. What do they offer for hybrid or EV customers? Positions the garage as forward-thinking.

10. **The new driver / first car checklist** — A genuinely helpful guide for someone who has just passed their test. Broad reach, lots of sharing potential.

## Category 7: Fitness & Leisure

(Gyms, Personal Trainers, Yoga Studios, Dance Schools, Swimming Pools, Sports Clubs, Outdoor Pursuits, and similar)

### 10 Evergreen Post Ideas

1. **The beginner's fear, addressed** — "If you've been thinking about joining but feel nervous, this is for you." The single most effective barrier to entry for gyms and fitness studios is the fear of being judged. Speak to it directly.

2. **A member or client spotlight** — With permission, celebrate a member's progress or milestone. Real people, real results, genuine community.

3. **The class or session you might not know about** — Spotlight a less-known class or service. "Have you tried our...?" Introduction to something surprising.

4. **A training tip for the current season** — Specific, actionable, genuinely useful. Not generic motivational content — a real tip that applies this week.

5. **Why rest days matter** — Counter-intuitive content from a fitness business. Shows genuine care for members' wellbeing, not just workout volume. Builds trust.

6. **The nutrition angle** — A simple, non-dogmatic nutrition tip that complements the training. Not a diet plan — a practical piece of advice.

7. **Behind the scenes at a class/session** — What happens in a session that members might not see from the outside? The instructor's preparation, the warmup process, the progression.

8. **A challenge or goal for this week** — Community-building content. Set a small, achievable challenge and invite the audience to take part.

9. **The equipment explained** — What is that machine for, actually? Demystifying gym equipment makes the space feel more accessible.

10. **The "why we do this" post** — What is the real reason the business exists? What does the team believe about movement, health, and community? Manifesto content that attracts the right members.

## Universal Content Principles (All Industries)

These principles apply regardless of category and should inform every evergreen post:

**1. Specific beats generic, every time.**
"How to prepare your boiler for winter" beats "Winter is coming — stay warm!" A specific, useful piece of advice is worth ten vague motivational posts.

**2. The audience's interest, not the business's interest.**
Every post should begin from the question: "What does my audience actually want to know or see right now?" Not: "What do I want to promote?"

**3. Local references earn trust.**
Mentioning a specific town, street, local event, or regional moment makes content feel genuine and relevant in a way that generic content never can.

**4. Consistency beats virality.**
For small businesses, showing up regularly with decent content is more valuable than chasing occasional viral posts. The goal is a reliable presence that reinforces trust over time.

**5. Authenticity over production.**
A genuine behind-the-scenes photo beats a polished stock image. A real customer story beats a made-up scenario. Real is the competitive advantage that small businesses have over big brands.
