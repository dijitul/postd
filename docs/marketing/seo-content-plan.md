# postd.uk SEO content plan: October to December 2026

Prepared 25 September 2026. Covers keyword and SERP research, a four-pillar topic cluster model, a 13-week publishing plan (26 articles, 2 per week) and the technical SEO work that has to happen first.

---

## 0. Read this first: honest caveats

1. **No paid keyword tool data was used.** Volumes and difficulty below come from SERP inspection (who ranks, how strong they are, how many UK results show up, whether Google's own help pages or forums hold the top spots). Every volume is a **relative band and a guess**, labelled as such. Before committing budget, check the top 30 terms in Semrush, Ahrefs or Google Keyword Planner (UK, English) and adjust priorities.
2. **The domain is new to search and has no Search Console history.** The cannibalisation audit in section 4 is therefore a *design-time ownership map*, not a GSC page+query analysis. Once the site has 8 to 12 weeks of GSC data, re-run the audit properly using page+query dimensions.
3. **Expect 3 to 6 months before new articles rank.** Seasonal pieces in this plan (Christmas hours, Black Friday, Small Business Saturday) will mostly earn their rankings for the **2027** season. In 2026 they are distribution assets: share them via postd's own GBP, Facebook and email.
4. **Product claims must match what is live.** As of 25 September 2026 publishing is live on all four platforms (Google Business Profile, Facebook, LinkedIn Company Pages and X), so GBP-led articles can promise automatic posting to the Google profile today. Keep every claim to those four platforms.
5. **Pricing is under revision.** CTAs below say "from £[X]/month". Fill this in once pricing is final (working range £19 to £69).

## 3. Topic cluster architecture

Four pillars. Every cluster article links up to its pillar in the first 150 words and again in the conclusion. Every pillar links down to all its clusters in a "Read next" block and contextually in the body.

### Pillar 1: Google Business Profile posts (the GBP hub)
- URL: `/guides/google-business-profile-posts`
- Owns: "google business profile posts", "gbp posts", "google posts", "how to post on google business profile"
- Why: under-served, US-dominated SERP, postd's biggest differentiator, strongest local-business intent.

### Pillar 2: Automated and AI social media posting (the solution hub)
- URL: `/guides/automated-social-media-posting`
- Owns: "automated social media posting (uk)", "social media automation for small business", "auto post to social media"
- Why: closest match to what postd sells. Converts best, competes hardest.

### Pillar 3: Social media for local and trade businesses (the "what to post" hub)
- URL: `/guides/social-media-for-local-businesses`
- Owns: "social media for local businesses", "social media for tradesmen" (as secondary until a dedicated trades page is justified), "social media post ideas for small business uk"
- Children: trade landing pages under `/for/{trade}`, seasonal idea posts, platform choice.

### Pillar 4: Tools and costs (the comparison hub)
- URL: `/guides/social-media-tools-uk`
- Owns: "best social media scheduler uk", "social media scheduling tools uk", "cheap social media scheduler uk"
- Children: `/alternatives/{tool}` pages, cost breakdowns.

### URL scheme
- Pillars: `/guides/{slug}`
- Articles and templates: `/blog/{slug}`
- Trade landing pages: `/for/{trade}` (hybrid: guide content plus product CTA, indexable)
- Comparisons: `/alternatives/{tool}`

## 4. Cannibalisation ownership map (design-time)

No GSC data exists yet, so this is enforced by design. Rules:

1. **One primary keyword per URL.** The owner is listed in the table below. No other page may use that phrase in its title tag or H1.
2. **Word-level guards inside Pillar 1:** the pillar title must not contain "ideas", "examples", "templates", "how often", "hours" or "rejected". Each of those words belongs to one cluster page.
3. **Seasonal separation:** "Christmas hours" (GBP special hours, how-to) and "Christmas post ideas" (content ideas) are separate pages with separate intents. The hours page never lists post ideas beyond a short "post this alongside it" section that links out.
4. **Trade pages vs Pillar 3:** Pillar 3 covers principles across all local businesses and gives each trade two sentences plus a link. Depth lives only on `/for/{trade}`.
5. **Cost queries:** "social media management cost uk" belongs to the AI vs freelancer article. The Pillar 4 tools page covers software prices only and links there for service costs.
6. **Re-audit trigger:** at week 12 (or once 8 weeks of GSC data exists), export page+query for all queries containing "google business profile", "post", "social media for" and check for two URLs in the top 20 with split clicks.

## 7. Technical SEO prerequisites (blockers before week 1)

The site is a client-rendered Vite React SPA with no robots.txt, sitemap.xml, per-route meta tags or blog route. Google can render JavaScript, but rendering is deferred and unreliable for a new domain, other crawlers and AI answer engines often do not execute JS at all, and every route currently shares one title and description. **Articles must ship as static HTML.**

Priority order:

1. **Static rendering for all public content.** Options, in order of preference:
   - (a) A separate static site generator (for example Astro with Markdown content collections) built to `/blog`, `/guides`, `/for`, `/alternatives`, served by nginx directly on postd.uk. Keeps the React app untouched and gives the lightest pages.
   - (b) Prerender the React marketing routes at build time (for example `vite-react-ssg` or a prerender step) if the team wants one codebase.
   - Either way, the landing page `/` and a pricing page should also be prerendered.
2. **nginx routing and real 404s.** The SPA fallback currently answers any path with `index.html` and a 200, which creates soft 404s. Serve static content paths first, return a true 404 for unknown public URLs, and only fall back to the SPA for app routes.
3. **Per-route meta:** unique `<title>` (50 to 60 chars), meta description, self-referencing canonical, Open Graph and Twitter tags, `lang="en-GB"`.
4. **robots.txt:** allow public content, disallow app routes (`/dashboard`, `/posts`, `/inbox`, `/platforms`, `/settings`, `/billing`, `/admin`, `/onboarding`, `/auth/`), declare the sitemap. Also add `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` on app routes, since robots.txt blocks crawling but not indexing. Keep `api.postd.uk` out of the index (noindex header on all API responses).
5. **sitemap.xml** generated at build, containing only canonical, indexable, 200-status URLs with accurate `lastmod`.
6. **Canonical host:** pick apex `postd.uk`, 301 `www` to it, HTTPS only, no trailing-slash duplicates.
7. **Structured data:** `Organization` (with dijitul as founder/parent and `sameAs`), `SoftwareApplication` with `offers` in GBP once pricing is set, `Article` with `author` linked to an author page, `BreadcrumbList`. FAQ and HowTo markup no longer produce rich results for most sites, so use them only where they describe the page honestly, not for SERP features.
8. **Author and trust pages (E-E-A-T):** an author page for Olly (dijitul, Mansfield, years building local marketing), an About page stating who runs postd, and an editorial note on comparison pages disclosing that postd is one of the tools compared.
9. **Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools:** verify both on day one and submit the sitemap. Bing matters because it feeds ChatGPT search and Copilot answers.
10. **Core Web Vitals for article templates:** LCP under 2.5s, INP under 200ms, CLS under 0.1. No React hydration on article pages if avoidable, WebP or AVIF images with explicit dimensions, self-hosted fonts with `font-display: swap`.
11. **Article template:** breadcrumb, author box, "last reviewed" date, table of contents, pillar "Read next" block, sticky trial CTA on mobile. Footer built mobile-first: link columns collapse into tap-to-expand accordions on phones (`aria-expanded`, always open on desktop), contact links stay as large tap targets, brand block on top, footer credit linking to https://dijitul.uk.
12. **Analytics:** GA4 or Plausible with trial sign-up as a conversion, UTM-tagged CTAs per article so each piece's sign-ups can be attributed.
