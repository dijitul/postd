Managing TikTok comments as a small business comes down to three jobs: answer the comments that are really customer enquiries, let TikTok's filters catch the abuse and spam, and stop worrying about the rest. Questions about prices, areas and availability come first, ideally the same day. Jokes, emojis and "first!" can be left alone. TikTok's own keyword filters and Creator Care Mode do most of the clearing up if you set them once.

This guide covers what to reply to, the TikTok settings worth switching on, and a weekly routine that fits around a working day.

## Why do TikTok comments matter for a small business?

On TikTok, the comments are often where enquiries happen. Someone watching a Nottingham salon's balayage video will type "how much is this?" under it rather than visit the website. Someone watching a Mansfield landscaper lay a patio will ask "do you cover Ollerton?" in the comments, in public, where every other viewer can see whether you answered.

That public part is the point. A reply to one person is read by everyone who watches the video afterwards. A clear, friendly answer to "how much?" does the job of a price list. A question left hanging for a week tells every later viewer that nobody is minding the shop.

TikTok comments also behave differently from Facebook ones:

- **Videos keep getting views for weeks.** A video can pick up new viewers long after you posted it, so comments trickle in on old posts as well as new ones.
- **The audience is wider than your local customers.** A video that travels will attract comments from people who will never buy from you. Most of those need no reply.
- **Tone is more casual.** Short, friendly replies fit better than a formal "Thank you for your enquiry".

## Which TikTok comments should you reply to first?

Sort comments by how likely they are to turn into money or trouble. This is the order I would work in:

| Comment type | Example | What to do | How fast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying question | "How much for a full set?" "Do you do Sundays?" | Answer with a real figure or a clear next step | Same day if you can |
| Area or availability question | "Do you come out to Hucknall?" | Yes or no, then how to book | Same day |
| Complaint from a customer | "Still waiting for my refund" | Short public reply, then move it to a private channel | Within hours |
| Praise from a customer | "Best coffee in Derby" | Thank them by name if you know it | When you get to it |
| Genuine question about the video | "What filler is that?" | Answer if it is quick, it builds trust | When you get to it |
| General chat, jokes, emojis | "😂😂" | A like, or nothing | No reply needed |
| Spam, scams, "DM me to grow your account" | | Delete, and report if it is a scam | When you see it |
| Abuse or threats | | Delete, report, block. Keep a screenshot if it is serious | Straight away |

A few house rules make this quicker:

1. **Answer price questions with a price.** "From £45, depends on length, book on the link in our bio" is better than "DM us". Some people will not DM, and everyone else reading wanted the same answer.
2. **Take complaints out of public view, but do answer in public first.** "Sorry about that, Sam. We have sent you a message to sort it" shows other viewers you dealt with it.
3. **Never share personal details in a public reply.** No addresses, order numbers or phone numbers in comments.
4. **Do not argue.** If someone is rude about your work, either answer the fair point once, calmly, or leave it. Arguing in public never looks good for the business.
5. **Consider a video reply for a question lots of people ask.** TikTok lets you reply to a comment with a video, which turns a good question into your next post.

## What comment controls does TikTok give you?

TikTok has a set of comment settings in the app, under your profile's settings and privacy menu, in the comments section. [TikTok's help page on managing comments](https://support.tiktok.com/en/account-and-privacy/account-privacy-settings/manage-comments) describes the current options. Menu names change from time to time, so if something has moved, check that page.

| Setting | What it does | Worth using for a business? |
|---|---|---|
| Who can comment | Choose who can comment on your videos, and change it per video | Usually leave it open. Customers are not always followers |
| Filter spam and offensive comments | Holds comments TikTok thinks are spam or offensive | Yes, switch it on |
| Filter keywords | Holds comments containing words you choose until you approve them | Yes, with a short list (see below) |
| Filter all comments | Holds every comment until you approve it. TikTok says this is not available to everyone | Only for a video that has attracted a pile-on |
| Review filtered comments | Where held comments wait for you to approve or delete | Check it weekly |
| Creator Care Mode | Hides comments flagged as offensive, inappropriate or profane, and comments from people you have reported, deleted or disliked before | Yes, especially if you have had trolls |

[Creator Care Mode was announced by TikTok in July 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/new-tools-for-creators?lang=en). TikTok says it learns from what you report and delete, so it gets more useful the more you use it. On the same comment, you can also delete, report, pin or block, and you can block the account behind it.

### How to set up keyword filters without hiding customers

Keyword filters are the most useful setting and the easiest to get wrong. A filter holds any comment containing the word, so a broad word hides genuine questions.

1. **Start with abuse and slurs you have actually seen**, plus common scam phrases you keep getting.
2. **Add your own name variations only if people are abusing you by name.**
3. **Do not add ordinary words like "price", "cost", "scam" or "rip off".** A real customer asking "is this a scam or are these prices real?" is still a customer. You want to see that comment, and answer it well.
4. **Check the filtered comments folder once a week** and approve anything that should not have been caught. If one word keeps catching good comments, take it off the list.

## What can you safely ignore on TikTok?

More than you think. A business account does not need to reply to:

- emojis, "first", tags of friends ("@jess we need to go here")
- general chat that is not a question
- people far outside your area who are just enjoying the video
- debates between other commenters, unless they turn abusive
- obvious bait from accounts that exist to start arguments

A like on a friendly comment is enough. It shows someone is around without adding work.

What you should not ignore, even when it is tempting: a customer complaint. Leaving it means the only version anyone reads is theirs. ## How long does handling TikTok comments take?

It depends on volume, so here is a worked example rather than a rule.

Take a café in Derby that posts three TikToks a week. Assume each video picks up 30 comments over its life, so about 90 comments a week. Say a third of those need a reply (questions, praise from regulars, the odd complaint) and each reply takes 2 minutes including reading the thread. That is 30 replies, or an hour a week. Add 15 minutes for the filtered folder and removing spam: 1 hour 15 minutes a week.

If the owner's time is worth £30 to £50 an hour, that is roughly £38 to £63 a week, or about £160 to £270 a month, spent on comments alone. Most of that time is not the typing. It is opening the app, finding which video has new comments, and remembering who has already been answered.

Cutting that down is mostly about batching:

- **Check twice a day at set times**, say 12:30 and 17:30, rather than every time the phone buzzes.
- **Keep a note of your standard answers** (prices, areas, booking link, opening hours) so you paste rather than type.
- **Deal with the newest video first**, because that is where most new viewers are.

## Where to start

This week:

1. Switch on **Filter spam and offensive comments** and **Creator Care Mode**.
2. Add a short keyword list: the abuse and scam phrases you have actually had. Leave out ordinary words like "price".
3. Write down your five standard answers: prices, areas covered, how to book, opening hours, how to complain.
4. Pick two times a day to check comments, newest video first.
5. Once a week, check the filtered folder and approve anything genuine.
