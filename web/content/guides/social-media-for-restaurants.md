Social media for a restaurant or takeaway does two jobs: getting people through the door (or ordering) and answering the questions they send back. Put your hours, menu and best photos on Google Business Profile first, then post real dishes, specials and events on Instagram and Facebook two to four times a week. The part that catches most restaurants out is the replies: "do you have a table for six on Saturday?", "is the curry gluten free?", "are you open on Boxing Day?" arriving in DMs during service. Below: what to post where, ready-to-use examples, and how to handle the message flood.

We make two products that cover different halves of this, postd for the posts and Postd Office for the messages, and we explain at the end which one suits whom, including when neither is the right fit.

## Where should a restaurant be on social media?

| Platform | What it does for you | What to put there |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Where people check you before booking | Hours, menu, photos, reviews, weekly posts, offers and events |
| Instagram | Where people decide whether the food looks good | Dish photos, Reels of the kitchen, stories for specials |
| Facebook | Where regulars and local groups follow you | Specials, events, opening changes, community news |
| TikTok | Reach beyond your regulars | Short kitchen videos, if someone on the team enjoys it |
| X and LinkedIn | Rarely worth it for a restaurant | Skip unless you do corporate catering |

### Get Google right first

Before anything else, check your Google Business Profile.

1. **Hours, including special hours** for every bank holiday and Christmas change.
2. **Your menu.** Google's [menu editor](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9455840?hl=en-GB) lets food and drink businesses add sections and dishes, or build a menu from a photo or PDF. You can also add a link to your menu.
3. **Booking and ordering links**, so the buttons go to your own system.
4. **Photos** of real dishes, the dining room and the outside (so people recognise it when they arrive).
5. **Reviews.** Reply to every one, especially the bad ones, calmly and briefly.

Then post once or twice a week. For what to post, see our [40 ideas for Google Business Profile](/guides/what-to-post-on-google-business-profile).

## What should a restaurant post?

### Dishes, with real photos

The single most effective post. Photograph the dish on the pass, in natural light, before it goes out. No stock photos, no AI images of food you do not serve.

Example for a restaurant in Nottingham:

> New on the winter menu: braised beef shin, slow-cooked for eight hours, with horseradish mash and crispy kale. £18.50. Available from Wednesday.

### Specials and what is on tonight

Example for a takeaway in Leicester:

> Tonight's special: lamb karahi with fresh naan, £11.95. Ready for collection from 5pm, or order for delivery through the link in our bio. We deliver within 3 miles.

### The people

Your chef, your front of house, the kitchen porter who has been with you for ten years. People like to know who is cooking.

> Meet Arjun, our head chef since 2019. He grew up in Leicester, trained in Birmingham, and his dal is the reason half our regulars come back. Ask him about the pickles.

### Bookings and events

Christmas parties, Valentine's, Mother's Day, quiz nights, live music. Post the date early, with how to book.

> Mother's Day is Sunday 7 March 2027. Three courses for £32, children's menu £12. We have two sittings, 12pm and 3pm, and tables go quickly. Book through the link on our profile.

### Trust and practical information

- Your food hygiene rating (people look it up on the [FSA ratings site](https://ratings.food.gov.uk/) anyway).
- Allergen approach: "Tell us about allergies when you order, and we will talk you through the menu."
- Accessibility, parking, high chairs, dogs, private hire.

### Reviews

Quote a real Google review word for word, first name only.

> "Best Sunday lunch we have had in Derby, and the Yorkshire puddings were the size of my head." Thank you, Claire. Sunday lunch is served 12 till 5, booking recommended.

## How do you handle the DM flood?

This is the part most restaurant guides skip. Once your posts are working, the messages arrive: on Instagram, on Facebook, in comments under your posts. Typical questions:

- "Have you got a table for 4 tonight at 7?"
- "Are you open on New Year's Day?"
- "Is the katsu curry gluten free?"
- "Do you do vegan options?"
- "Can I book the back room for 20 people?"
- "My order was missing a side."

They arrive while you are in service, when nobody can reply. A message answered the next morning is often a booking lost to the place down the road. Our guide on [how fast to reply to Facebook and Instagram messages](/guides/how-fast-reply-to-facebook-messages) goes into what customers expect.

### A simple system

1. **Write your standard answers once.** Hours, booking link, parking, dogs, vegan options, private hire, delivery area. These cover most messages.
2. **Send bookings to one place.** Reply with your booking link, or confirm the details back in writing so nothing lives only in a DM.
3. **Never guess about allergens.** The Food Standards Agency's [guidance on allergen information for non-prepacked food](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/allergen-information-for-non-prepacked-foods-best-practice) covers what customers should be told. For an allergy question, answer from your own allergen records, or say a member of staff will confirm, and ask them to mention it when ordering.
4. **Complaints go to a person.** Apologise, move it to a private message, and put it right.
5. **Check messages at set times.** Before service, after service and first thing in the morning.

Example replies:

> Booking: Hi Sam, thanks for getting in touch. You can book a table for tonight here: [booking link]. If it shows full, reply and we will check for cancellations.

> Allergy: Hi Priya, thanks for asking. Our katsu sauce contains wheat, so it is not suitable for a gluten-free diet. Please mention it when you order and the team will talk you through what is safe.

## How much time does this take?

A worked example, with stated assumptions. A restaurant posting three times a week on Instagram and Facebook and once on Google: about 7 posts a week at 15 minutes each, so 1 hour 45 minutes. Add 15 messages and comments a day at 2 minutes each, which is 30 minutes a day, or 3 hours 30 minutes over a seven-day week. That is 5 hours 15 minutes a week. At £30 an hour, that is £157.50 a week, or about £8,190 a year.

Most of that is the messages, not the posts.
