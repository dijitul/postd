Post on your Google Business Profile at least once a week. Google does not publish a recommended frequency, and the best available evidence suggests posts do not move your ranking directly, but a weekly post keeps something current on your profile for the customers who are already looking at it. Post more often when you have an offer, an event or a seasonal rush. This is part of our [complete guide to Google Business Profile posts](/guides/google-business-profile-posts).

## Why is once a week the right starting point?

Once a week is frequent enough that your profile always has something recent on it, and realistic enough that a busy owner will actually keep it up. A rhythm you can sustain for a year beats a burst of daily posts that stops after a fortnight.

It also fits how posts behave. [Google's help centre](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB) says posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set. So a business that posts weekly always has a run of recent updates on show, while one that posts a few times a year spends long stretches with little or nothing there.

## Does Google say how often to post?

No. Google's help pages explain how to create, schedule and repeat posts, but we could not find any Google guidance recommending a number of posts per week or month. Any exact figure you read, including ours, is a judgement, not a Google rule.

## Do Google Business Profile posts help rankings?

The evidence says posts are not a meaningful direct ranking factor. They seem to help with persuading people rather than with where you appear.

| Source | Date | What it says | How much weight to give it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Google: how local results are ranked](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB) | Current page, checked September 2026 | Local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence. Google recommends complete information, keeping hours up to date, responding to reviews and adding photos. Posts are not mentioned | High. This is Google's own description, although it is deliberately general |
| [Whitespark Local Search Ranking Factors 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) | November 2025 | A survey of 47 local search specialists ranked "frequency of Google posts" 148th among local pack factors, and "quantity of Google posts" 168th | Medium. Expert opinion, not a measurement, but the most widely used survey in the field |
| [Sterling Sky test](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/do-google-posts-impact-ranking/) | July 2021 | One post a week for nine weeks on three inactive listings, tracking 441 keywords each, produced no measurable change in local pack rankings | Low to medium. A real test, but small and five years old |

Put together: if you are posting in the hope of climbing the map, you will probably be disappointed. The things Google does mention, such as accurate details, reviews, review replies and photos, are where ranking effort is better spent.

## So why post every week at all?

Because the people who find your profile read it before they decide whether to call you. That is where posts earn their keep.

Picture someone in Mansfield searching for an electrician. They see three profiles in the map. All three have decent reviews. One has a post from Tuesday about a consumer unit swap in Rainworth, another from last week about EICRs for landlords, and a clear Call now button. The other two have nothing recent. The first one looks open, busy and local, and that is often enough to get the call.

Weekly posting also gives you:

- **A place for time-sensitive information.** Offers, events, holiday hours, new services.
- **Somewhere to put your best reviews in front of people,** quoted word for word.
- **Something to measure.** The **Performance** section of your profile shows calls, website clicks, direction requests and the searches people used to find you, according to [Google's guide to Business Profile performance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en-GB). Watching those numbers over months tells you more about your own profile than any general rule.

## What happens if your profile goes quiet?

Nothing dramatic that Google has documented. There is no published penalty for not posting. What does happen is simple: after six months your posts are archived, so your profile ends up with no recent updates, and customers comparing you with a competitor who posts regularly see the difference.

You will read claims that profiles lose visibility after a set number of days without activity. We could not find a controlled, published study or any Google statement to back up a specific number, so we are not repeating one here. If you want to know whether it affects your business, compare your own Performance figures for a quiet month and a busy one.

## When should you post more often?

Post more than once a week when you have something time-sensitive that customers need to know about. The extra posts should add information, not just volume.

| Situation | What to post | Suggested extra posts |
|---|---|---|
| Running an offer | An offer post when it starts, an update a few days before it ends | 1 to 2 over the offer period |
| Hosting an event | An event post a week or two before, a reminder the day before | 1 to 2 |
| Seasonal rush | Boiler services in autumn, Christmas bookings for salons, party menus for cafés | 1 a week on top of your usual post, for the busy weeks |
| Changed opening hours | An update post, plus setting special hours in the profile itself | 1 |
| New service or new area covered | An update explaining what, where and for whom | 1 |
| Last-minute availability | Salons and other appointment businesses posting gaps in the diary | As and when |

For ideas to fill those extra slots, see [what to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/what-to-post-on-google-business-profile).

## Does the time of day you post matter?

Less than on other platforms. On Facebook or X, a post competes in a fast-moving feed and can be buried within hours. On Google Business Profile, a post sits on your profile until it is archived or replaced, and it is seen when someone looks you up, not when you publish. We found no Google guidance saying that posting time affects how posts are shown.

Timing still matters in one practical way: publish before the moment it is useful. A café's Friday lunch special should be live on Thursday or first thing Friday, not at 2pm Friday. A plumber's frozen pipes tip should go out before the forecast cold snap, not after.

## How do you make sure you never miss a week?

The businesses that post consistently are the ones that have taken the weekly decision out of it. Three ways to do that:

1. **Batch and schedule once a month.** Sit down for an hour, write four posts, and use Google's **Schedule this post** option to set a date for each. It is free and built into your profile.
2. **Use Repeats for things that genuinely recur.** A weekly late opening or a monthly offer can be set once with the **Repeats** drop-down. Save it for posts where saying the same thing again is accurate and useful.

Whichever you choose, pair it with a quick monthly check of your Performance figures and your latest reviews.
