Most of a plumber's new customers search Google first, so your Google Business Profile is the place to post, with a Facebook Page second. Post once or twice a week about real jobs, what your reviews say, your Gas Safe registration and whatever the season is doing to people's pipes and boilers. Below is a 12-month UK calendar, trust posts that answer the "are they legit?" worry, example posts you can copy, and a worked example of how long it all takes.

## Where do people actually look for a plumber?

When a boiler dies or a pipe bursts, people do not scroll Instagram. They search "plumber near me" or "boiler repair Mansfield" and look at the three businesses Google shows on the map. What they see there is your Google Business Profile: reviews, photos, opening hours and your recent posts.

That makes the order of priority fairly clear for most plumbers and heating engineers:

| Platform | What it does for a plumber | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | The first thing people see when they search. Posts show you are active and local. | Yes, always |
| Facebook Page | Local group recommendations, people checking you out after a friend mentions you. | Yes, second |
| Nextdoor and local Facebook groups | People asking "anyone know a good plumber?" | Worth answering when you can |
| Instagram | Nice for bathroom fitters with photogenic finished jobs. | Optional |
| TikTok | Some plumbers build a following, but it is a hobby-sized time commitment. | Only if you enjoy it |
| LinkedIn | Useful if you chase commercial or letting agent work. | Only for commercial |

If you only do one thing, keep your Google profile busy. Our [complete guide to Google Business Profile posts](/guides/google-business-profile-posts) covers the post types and rules.

## What should a plumber post?

The posts that work for plumbers answer the questions people have before they ring you: Do they cover my area? Are they qualified? Are they any good? What will it cost? Roughly, rotate between:

- **Recent jobs.** What you did, where (the town or area, never the address), and what the problem turned out to be.
- **Review quotes.** Word for word, first name only.
- **Trust posts.** Gas Safe, insurance, guarantees, how you work.
- **Seasonal tips.** Frozen pipes, boiler checks, holiday lets, hosepipe leaks.
- **Common questions.** "How long does a boiler service take?" is a post, not just a phone call.
- **Offers.** Sparingly, and with a clear end date.

For more ideas, see [40 things to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/what-to-post-on-google-business-profile).

## Trust posts that win plumbing jobs

People inviting a stranger into their house to work on gas want reassurance. Calm, factual posts about your credentials do that better than any slogan.

### Gas Safe Register

By law, anyone working on gas appliances must be on the Gas Safe Register. The Health and Safety Executive tells householders to [check an engineer is Gas Safe registered](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/gas-safe-register-check.htm), and customers can do it themselves on the [Gas Safe Register website](https://www.gassaferegister.co.uk/) using the licence number on your ID card. The back of the card lists what you are qualified to work on.

That makes a perfect post, because you are teaching people to check, which only helps the honest engineers:

> Every one of our engineers carries a Gas Safe ID card, and you are welcome to ask to see it before we start. The back of the card shows exactly which appliances we are qualified to work on. You can also check any engineer on the Gas Safe Register website. It takes a minute, and it is worth doing for anyone who touches your boiler, not just us.

### Landlord gas safety checks

Landlords must have gas appliances and flues checked every 12 months by a Gas Safe registered engineer, and give tenants a copy of the record within 28 days of the check, according to the [HSE's guidance for landlords](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/faqlandlord.htm). Landlords forget dates, so a reminder post brings in repeat work:

> Landlords in Sutton and Kirkby: your gas safety record runs for 12 months, and your tenants need a copy within 28 days of the check. If yours is due in the new year, book now and we will send the certificate to you and your agent the same day.

### Water regulations and WaterSafe

If you are a member of an approved contractor scheme listed on [WaterSafe](https://www.watersafe.org.uk/), the national register backed by the UK's water companies, say so. It is less well known than Gas Safe, so a short explainer works well.

### Insurance and guarantees

Say what you carry and what you guarantee, in plain terms: "public liability insurance up to £X", "12-month guarantee on parts and labour". Only state what is true and current, and update the post if it changes.

### Heat pumps and grants

If you install heat pumps, the [Boiler Upgrade Scheme](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/boiler-upgrade-scheme-regulations-approved-standards-grant-categories-and-grant-levels/notice-of-approved-grant-categories-and-values-for-the-boiler-upgrade-scheme) currently gives £7,500 towards an air source or ground source heat pump in England and Wales, with a higher grant for some homes moving off oil or LPG. Grants change, so link the official page in your post and check it before you publish.

## Example posts you can copy

Change the details to match your job. Leave your phone number out of the text on Google and let the Call now button do the work.

**Frozen pipe tip (January):**

> Cold one tonight. If a pipe freezes, turn off the water at the stopcock first, then open the cold taps nearby to take the pressure off. Never use a flame or a heat gun on it. Not sure where your stopcock is? It is usually under the kitchen sink. Find it now, while nothing is leaking. If you need us, tap Call now.

The Met Office has a fuller [frozen and burst pipe checklist](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/warnings-and-advice/seasonal-advice/your-home/frozen-or-burst-pipes) you can link to on Facebook.

**Condensate pipe (November):**

> If your combi boiler stops on a frosty morning and shows a fault code, the cause is often a frozen condensate pipe, the plastic pipe that runs outside to a drain. Pouring warm (not boiling) water along it often clears it. If it keeps happening, we can reroute or insulate it. Booking jobs in Mansfield and Ravenshead this week.

**Recent job:**

> Boiler swap in Bulwell today. The old one was 19 years old and had been patched up twice this year. New combi fitted, system flushed, magnetic filter added and registered for the manufacturer warranty. The customer has heating and hot water back the same evening.

**Common question:**

**Summer planning:**

> Thinking about a new bathroom? Most of ours take 7 to 10 working days from strip-out to grout, and we are booking September starts now. Tap Learn more to see recent ones we have done in Chesterfield.

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

Your Google reviews are the easiest material you have, because a customer has already written the persuasive bit. A few rules:

1. **Quote word for word.** Do not tidy up or trim in a way that changes the meaning. Since April 2025, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act has made fake or misleading reviews illegal in the UK.
2. **First name only**, plus the area if the customer mentioned it.
3. **Add the story.** What was the job? What had gone wrong before?
4. **Ask every customer, not just the happy ones.** Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans offering anything in return for reviews and bans asking only satisfied customers.

Example:

> "Came out on a Sunday when our heating packed in, found the fault in ten minutes and did not charge a fortune." Thanks, Lee in Hucknall. It was a failed diverter valve, a common one on boilers of that age. Parts were on the van, so the house was warm again by lunchtime.

## How long does it take to do this by hand?

Here is a worked example. Your numbers will differ, so swap in your own.

**Assumptions:** two posts a week on Google and two on Facebook (four posts a week). Each post takes 20 minutes: thinking of something, finding a photo, writing it, posting it. Your time is worth £40 an hour, a fair figure for a self-employed engineer once you count the jobs you could be quoting.

- 4 posts × 20 minutes = 80 minutes a week
- 80 minutes × 52 weeks ÷ 12 = about 347 minutes a month, or **5.8 hours**
- 5.8 hours × £40 = **about £231 a month** of your time

## Where should a plumber start?

1. **Claim and finish your Google Business Profile.** Service areas, opening hours, services list, at least ten real photos.
2. **Post one Gas Safe or trust post this week.** It is the post you will reuse most.
3. **Pick three ideas from the calendar** for the month you are in.
4. **Ask your last five customers for a review**, all of them, not just the ones you think liked you.
5. **Post every week**, by hand or with a tool. See [how often to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/how-often-post-google-business-profile) for why steady matters.
