A salon needs two things from social media: new clients finding you, and existing clients booking again. New clients mostly find you on Google, so keep your Google Business Profile busy with posts, and use Instagram to show your work and take booking requests. Below is a 12-month UK post calendar, trust posts, examples to copy, and how to deal with the flood of "do you have anything Saturday?" messages without living on your phone.

## Where do salon clients actually look?

There are two different journeys, and they use different platforms.

- **New clients** search "hairdresser near me" or "nail salon Leicester" on Google, compare the map listings, read reviews and look at photos. Your Google Business Profile does the work here.
- **Existing and nearly-clients** follow you on Instagram or Facebook, see a colour they like, and send a DM asking for a price or a slot.

| Platform | What it does for a salon | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | New clients searching nearby, reviews, Book button | Yes, always |
| Instagram | Showing your work, reels, booking DMs | Yes, for most salons |
| Facebook Page | Local word of mouth, older clients, last-minute gaps | Yes |
| TikTok | Transformations and tutorials can travel far, often outside your area | Optional |
| LinkedIn | Rarely relevant unless you sell training or products | Usually no |

## What should a salon post?

Rotate through these so you are never staring at a blank caption:

- **Your work.** Colours, cuts, nails, brows, with a short note on what was done.
- **Gaps and availability.** "Two slots tomorrow" posts fill chairs.
- **Aftercare tips.** Making colour last, what to avoid after a lash lift.
- **Meet the team.** Who specialises in what.
- **Reviews**, quoted word for word.
- **Trust and safety.** Patch tests, hygiene, qualifications.
- **Seasonal bookings.** Prom, weddings, Christmas.

## Trust posts for salons

Salon clients worry about different things from a plumber's customers, but they still want reassurance before trying somewhere new.

### Patch tests

The National Hair and Beauty Federation's [allergy alert testing guidance](https://www.nhbf.co.uk/documents/nhbf-faqs-allergy-alert-testing-protocols/factsheet-faqs-allergy-alert-testing-protocols-2025.pdf) says allergy alert tests should be done at least 48 hours before a colour service, and the result recorded on the client card. Some colour brands ask for longer, so follow your manufacturer's instructions. A friendly post explains why, and saves awkward conversations at the desk:

> New to colour with us? We will need a quick skin test at least 48 hours before your appointment. It takes five minutes, you can pop in any time we are open, and it is there to keep you safe. Book it with your colour appointment and we will remind you.

### Qualifications and memberships

If you hold NVQs or VTCT qualifications, are NHBF members, or a stylist has finished advanced training, say so in a "meet the team" post. Keep it about what the client gets: "Sam has just finished her advanced colour correction course, so if a box dye has gone wrong, she is the one to see."

### Aesthetics treatments

If you offer injectables, note that the government is working towards a licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures in England, and it is already illegal to give Botox or fillers for cosmetic reasons to anyone under 18. Check the [current government position](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/licensing-of-non-surgical-cosmetic-procedures/outcome/the-licensing-of-non-surgical-cosmetic-procedures-in-england-consultation-response) before you post anything about licences, and do not claim one before it exists.

### Hygiene and how you work

Clean tools, fresh towels, how you sterilise nail equipment. A short behind-the-scenes post shows it far better than "high standards" ever will.

## What about booking messages on Instagram and Facebook?

For most salons, this is the real headache. Posting is the easy half. The hard half is the stream of DMs and comments that comes back: "how much for balayage?", "anything Saturday?", "do you do kids?". They arrive while you have a client in the chair, sit in three different apps, and get forgotten.

A few habits help:

1. **Answer with a next step**, usually a link to your booking system or a clear price range.
2. **Keep a set of approved answers** for the questions you get every day: prices, patch tests, parking, cancellation policy.
3. **Have one place to read everything**, so the owner's phone and the salon tablet are not both half-handling it.
4. **Never leave messages over the weekend.** See [how fast to reply to Facebook and Instagram messages](/guides/how-fast-reply-to-facebook-messages).

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

1. **Quote word for word**, first name only.
2. **Credit the stylist** if the review names them.
3. **Pair it with a photo** of the actual look, with the client's permission.
4. **Ask every client**, not just the happy ones. Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans offering discounts for reviews and asking only satisfied customers.

Example:

> "Finally found someone who listens. Asked for a trim and got a trim!" Thanks, Hannah in Beeston. Chloe has been cutting hair for 12 years and still thinks listening is the most important part.

1. **Tidy your Google Business Profile**: services with prices, a Book button linked to your booking system, real photos of your work.
2. **Write your patch test post** and your "meet the team" posts once, then reuse them.
3. **Pick three ideas from the calendar** for this month.
4. **Write down your ten most common DM questions** and the answers. You will use them whichever tool you choose.
5. **Ask this week's clients for reviews.**
