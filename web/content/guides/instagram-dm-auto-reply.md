You can auto-reply to Instagram DMs in four ways: Instagram's own frequently asked questions in chat settings, saved replies you tap to insert, Meta Business Suite's automated responses (instant reply, away message, FAQ presets and keyword replies), and third-party tools. All the built-in options are free and need a professional account. The trick to not sounding like a robot is simple: automate the acknowledgement and the questions with one fixed answer, write it the way you would say it, and keep a person on anything involving price, availability or a complaint.

This guide walks through each option, the rules Meta sets, and wording you can copy for a salon, a café and a tradesperson.

## What do you need before you can auto-reply on Instagram?

A **professional account**, either Business or Creator. Personal accounts do not get the business messaging tools. Instagram's help centre explains [how to switch to a professional account](https://help.instagram.com/2358103564437429).

For Meta Business Suite's automations you also need your Instagram account connected to your Facebook Page and added to Meta Business Suite. Most businesses already have this if they have ever run an ad or linked the two accounts.

## Which Instagram auto-reply options are there?

| Option | Where you set it | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frequently asked questions | Instagram app, chat settings | Shows up to four questions people can tap when they start a chat, each with an optional automatic answer | Opening hours, booking link, prices from |
| Saved replies | Instagram app and Meta Business Suite | Ready-written answers you insert yourself | Fast typing, not automatic |
| Instant reply | Meta Business Suite, Inbox, Automations | Sends a message to the first DM someone sends you | Setting expectations |

| Third-party tools | ManyChat, Postd Office and others | Flows, comment-to-DM, AI drafted replies | Higher volume, campaigns |

Menu names move around in Meta's apps, so if something is not where this table says, search the app's settings for the feature name.

## How do you set up frequently asked questions on Instagram?

Instagram lets a professional account add up to four frequently asked questions, which appear when someone opens a chat with you. Each can have an automatic answer. According to [Instagram's help page](https://help.instagram.com/4153704678000867), the feature sits in your messaging settings in the app.

Pick the four questions you get most. For a salon in Nottingham, that is probably:

1. How do I book?
2. How much is a cut and colour?
3. Do you have parking?
4. Do I need a patch test?

Keep each answer short and useful. "We're on Mansfield Road with free parking behind the building after 6pm and all day Sunday" beats "Please see our website for parking information".

## How do you set an instant reply and away message?

In Meta Business Suite:

1. Go to **Inbox**, then **Automations**. On a computer this is the easiest place to do it.
2. Choose **Instant reply**, pick Instagram (and Facebook if you want the same reply on both), write the message and save.
3. Back in Automations, choose **Away message**, set a schedule (for example 6pm to 8am and all day Sunday) and write the message.

Meta's [automated responses page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/395965998733706) describes the instant reply as a custom message sent to the first message someone sends you, and the away message as the reply sent when you are away from the inbox.

## How do keyword replies work, and why do they fail?

A keyword reply sends a set message when a DM contains a word you choose, such as "price" or "book". Meta's help centre notes three things that trip people up:

- **Desktop only.** Custom keyword replies are set up in Meta Business Suite on a computer.
- **Exact matches.** The automatic reply only goes out when the keywords match exactly. "Prices", "pricing" and "how much" are three different keywords.
- **Presets win.** If a preset reply (such as an away message) and a keyword reply are both triggered, Meta sends the preset and not the keyword reply.

Keyword replies are useful for one thing done well, like sending your price list link to anyone who types "price". They are a poor substitute for reading the message.

## What rules does Meta set for automated replies?

Meta's [Messenger Platform and Instagram messaging policy](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy) applies to Instagram messaging as well as Messenger. The parts that matter for a small business:

- **Tell people it is automated.** Where the law requires it, automated chats must say that the person is talking to an automated service, at the start of a conversation and when switching from a person to a bot. Meta recommends doing it everywhere.
- **The 24-hour window.** Tools connected through Meta can send normal messages for 24 hours after the customer's last message. A separate Human Agent tag allows a person to reply manually within 7 days. Your instant reply goes out straight away, so this rarely matters for it, but it matters for slow follow-ups sent from a tool.
- **Bots must respond quickly.** Fully automated experiences must respond to the user within 30 seconds. This is aimed at chatbot developers, and the built-in Meta tools handle it for you.

## How do you write an auto-reply that does not sound like a robot?

Most auto-replies sound robotic for the same reasons: they are vague, they over-promise and they are written in "customer service" language nobody actually speaks.

Five rules:

1. **Say it is automatic.** One short line. It is honest, and people forgive a bit of stiffness from an automatic message.
2. **Say when a person will reply.** "Within a couple of hours" or "by 9am" beats "as soon as possible".
3. **Give the one thing they might need now.** A booking link, the emergency number, opening hours.
4. **Write like you talk.** UK English, contractions, no "valued customer".
5. **Do not answer what you do not know.** Never put prices, availability or delivery times in an automatic reply unless they really are fixed.

### Weak

> Thank you for contacting us! Your message is important to us. A member of our team will respond to your enquiry as soon as possible. Have a great day!

### Better: a salon

> Hi, thanks for your message. This is an automatic reply: we're with clients most of the day and check messages at 11, 2 and 5. You can book straight in here: [link]. If you're asking about a colour, let us know if you've had a patch test with us in the last 6 months.

### Better: a café in Derby

> Thanks for getting in touch. This is an automatic reply. We're open 8 till 4, Tuesday to Sunday. For tables today it's quickest to ring us (number on our profile). For cake orders and private hire, tell us the date and numbers and we'll reply by the end of the day.

### Better: a plumber in Mansfield

> Thanks for your message, this is an automatic reply as I'm on a job. I check messages at lunch and after 5. If water is leaking right now, turn off your stopcock and ring the number on my profile. For quotes, send a photo and your postcode and I'll get back to you today.

### Away message for the weekend

> We're closed for the weekend, this is an automatic reply. We'll answer first thing Monday. If it's an emergency, please ring the number on our profile.

## When should you not use an auto-reply?

- **Complaints.** An automatic "Thanks for your message!" to someone whose wedding cake collapsed makes things worse. Your instant reply will go out anyway, which is why it should be neutral and short.
- **Prices that change.** If a quote depends on the job, the auto-reply should ask for the details, not give a number.
- **Anything legal, medical or safety related.** Allergy questions at a café, patch tests at a salon, gas smells for a heating engineer. A person answers these, every time.

## What about AI auto-replies?

There are now tools that read the message and write a full answer, rather than sending fixed text. They can save a lot of time, but the risk is obvious: an AI that makes up a price or an opening time is answering on your behalf.

Before trusting any AI with your Instagram DMs, ask:

- Where does it get its answers? Your approved information, or anything it can find?
- What happens when it does not know? It should leave the message for a person.
- Can you check each reply before it goes, at least at first?
- Can you turn on automatic sending for only some questions, such as opening hours?
