Black Friday is Friday 27 November 2026, with Cyber Monday on 30 November. For a local business, the best Black Friday is short and honest: one clear offer or value-add, announced this weekend, posted four to six times across the week, and set up as an offer post on your Google Business Profile. You do not have to discount at all, and if you do use a "was" price it must be one you have genuinely charged. Below is a day-by-day post plan you can start today, copy-and-paste examples, ideas that do not cut your prices, and the UK rules in plain English.

## Should a small local business do Black Friday at all?

Only if you have something worth saying. Black Friday was built for big retailers with stock to shift. A plumber in Mansfield or a salon in Nottingham does not need to copy them, and customers do not expect it.

There are three sensible positions:

| Approach | Suits | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| A genuine discount | Shops with stock, cafés with gift cards, salons with retail products | Training customers to wait for sales; "was" price rules |
| A value-add, no discount | Trades, salons, gyms, professional services | Low, as long as the extra is real |
| Opt out, politely | Busy trades, anyone fully booked | None. A cheerful "we do fair prices all year" post still gets seen |

If you are fully booked into December, opting out is a perfectly good choice. A short post saying so, with a nudge to book for January, is often more useful than a discount you cannot honour.

On Google, publish one offer post on Monday 23 November covering the whole run, rather than seven separate updates. Then use updates for the rest. On Facebook, the full sequence is fine.

### Example posts, ready to adapt

Teaser (Friday 20 November), for a salon in Nottingham:

> We do not do huge Black Friday discounts, but we are doing something for our regulars next week. Keep an eye on this page on Monday.

Announcement (Monday 23 November), for a café in Derby:

> Black Friday at the café: buy a £25 gift card between now and Monday 30 November and we will add a free hot drink voucher on top. Gift cards are available at the counter or on our website. Perfect for stocking fillers, and they never go out of season.

Detail (Wednesday 25 November), for a plumber in Mansfield:

> Our Black Friday offer is not a discount, it is a free check. Book a boiler service before 30 November and we will also check your radiators and bleed them while we are there, no charge. It is the job most people forget until the first cold snap. Book using the button below.

It's live (Friday 27 November), for a gift shop in Leicester:

> It is Black Friday. 15% off all candles and diffusers, in the shop and online, until 5pm on Monday 30 November. Nothing else has changed, including our wrapping, which is still free.

Last chance (Monday 30 November):

> Last day. The gift card bonus ends at 5pm today. After that, gift cards are still available, just without the free drink.

Thank you (Tuesday 1 December):

> Thank you to everyone who popped in over the weekend. Next up is Small Business Saturday on 5 December, when we will have mince pies on the counter from 10am.

## How do you post a Black Friday offer on Google Business Profile?

Google Business Profile has a dedicated offer post type. According to [Google's help page on posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), an offer needs a title, dates and times, and you can add a description, photo, coupon code, link and terms and conditions. It shows a "View offer" button automatically.

1. Open your profile in Google Search or Maps, start a new post and choose the **Offer** type. (Button labels move around between the app and desktop, but the offer option sits with the other post types.)
2. **Title:** say what it is, not "Black Friday!!!". "Free radiator check with every boiler service" beats "Black Friday deal".
3. **Dates:** set the start and end date and time. Use the real end. If it ends at 5pm on Monday 30 November, say so.
4. **Description:** one or two sentences. What they get, who it is for, how to claim it.
5. **Coupon code and link:** add them if you have an online shop or booking page.
6. **Terms:** keep them short and honest. One per customer, while stocks last, not with other offers.
7. **Photo:** a real photo of the product, the shop or the job. Not a "SALE" graphic.

Two things to avoid. Leave your phone number and email out of the text: Google's [posts content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB) says it may remove posts with contact details in them, and the Call button already uses your profile's number. And avoid shouting: capitals, strings of exclamation marks and emoji-heavy titles look like spam next to a calm competitor.

Example offer post:

> Title: Gift card bonus: free hot drink with every £25 card
>
> Buy a £25 gift card at the counter or online and we will add a free hot drink voucher. Ideal for Secret Santa.
>
> Dates: 23 November 2026, 8am, to 30 November 2026, 5pm
>
> Terms: One drink voucher per £25 card. Voucher valid until 31 January 2027.

## What can you offer instead of a discount?

A discount is the easiest offer to copy and the hardest to undo. These value-adds keep your prices where they are and still give people a reason to act this week.

1. **A free extra with a booking.** A radiator bleed with a boiler service, a fringe trim with a colour, a free oven clean with an end of tenancy clean.
2. **A bonus on gift cards.** Buy £25, get a free coffee or £5 to spend in January. It brings money in now and visits in the quiet month.
3. **First pick of the diary.** Let regulars book January or spring slots before anyone else.
4. **A bundle.** Shampoo, conditioner and a treatment together, priced as a bundle. Do not invent a "worth £X" figure unless those are your real separate prices.
5. **Free delivery or free gift wrapping** for the week.
6. **A donation.** "£1 from every order this week goes to the Mansfield food bank." Say which charity and report back afterwards.
7. **An extended guarantee** for jobs booked this week, if you can stand behind it.
8. **The honest opt-out.** "No Black Friday here, just fair prices all year." It reads well and costs nothing.

Example value-add post for an electrician:

> We are not doing Black Friday discounts. Our prices are the same in November as they are in March. What we are doing: book an EICR this week and we will test your smoke alarms while we are there, free. Booking until Monday 30 November.

## What are the UK rules on Black Friday "was" prices?

This is where small businesses get caught out, usually without meaning to. The short version: a "was" price, a "save £X" or a "50% off" must reflect a genuine price you have actually charged, and you need to be able to show it.

### What counts as a genuine "was" price?

The Advertising Standards Authority's [advice on promotional savings claims](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/promotional-savings-claims.html) says a "was" price must reflect the genuine usual selling price. It looks at how recently and how long the higher price was charged, your pricing history and your sales. The higher price should have been charged for significantly longer than the sale price, and the ASA says it generally expects at least three months of pricing history. A manufacturer's RRP on its own does not prove what customers usually pay.

The Chartered Trading Standards Institute's [guidance for traders on pricing practices](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/guidance-for-traders-on-pricing-practices) (last reviewed April 2026) adds practical tests:

- The sale should run for the same time as, or less than, the period you charged the higher price.
- The higher price should be recent and the last price you charged, not one from many months ago.
- An end date on a limited-time offer should be clear and true. Do not move it unless something genuinely unforeseeable happens.
- "Up to 50% off" is only fair if a significant proportion of the items are actually at 50% off.

### What has changed recently?

Since 6 April 2025, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has let the Competition and Markets Authority fine businesses directly for consumer law breaches, up to 10% of global turnover ([CMA announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-to-boost-consumer-and-business-confidence-as-new-consumer-protection-regime-comes-into-force)). In its [November 2026 Black Friday warning](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/the-real-deal-black-friday-back-under-the-asa-s-microscope.html), the ASA said it is monitoring Black Friday price promotions and that it banned ads from John Lewis, Debenhams and Boots last year because they could not evidence genuine reductions.

The law on reference prices is still being argued at the top end. In 2026 the High Court ruled in the CMA's case against Emma Sleep and declined to set a fixed sales-ratio test, and the CMA removed its mattress pricing guidance ([Browne Jacobson summary](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/retail-law-roundup-july-2026/cma-v-emma-matrazen-reference-pricing-case-upate)). For a small business, the practical rule has not changed: only use a "was" price you can prove.

### The safe way to word it

| Risky | Safer |
|---|---|
| "Was £60, now £30" (price never charged) | "£30 this week, our usual price is £45" (if £45 really is usual) |
| "Up to 70% off" (one item at 70%) | "20% off all candles" |
| "Ends tonight!" (then extended) | "Ends 5pm Monday 30 November" (and it does) |
| "Worth £120" bundle | "Shampoo, conditioner and mask together for £40" |
| Countdown that resets | No countdown, or one that ends when you said |

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. If you run a large online sale with reference prices, take advice.
