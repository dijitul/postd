Electricians win most new work from Google, so post first on your Google Business Profile, then on a Facebook Page, and add LinkedIn if you chase commercial or letting agent work. Post weekly about finished jobs, your scheme registration (NICEIC, NAPIT or another), safety reminders and the seasonal jobs: EICRs for landlords, EV chargers, outside lighting when the clocks go back. Below is a 12-month calendar, trust posts, examples to copy and a worked example of the time involved.

## Where do customers look for an electrician?

Someone whose lights keep tripping searches "electrician near me" or "electrician Loughborough" and picks from the businesses Google shows on the map. Your Google Business Profile is that listing, and your posts sit on it. Landlords and letting agents also search Google, but they often keep a contact once they find a good one, so it pays to be visible when they first look.

| Platform | What it does for an electrician | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | First thing people see when they search. Posts prove you are active and local. | Yes, always |
| Facebook Page | Local group recommendations, word of mouth checks. | Yes, second |
| LinkedIn Company Page | Letting agents, facilities managers, builders who subcontract. | If you want commercial work |
| Instagram | Neat cable work and smart home installs look good. | Optional |
| TikTok | Fun, but a big time commitment for few local leads. | Only if you enjoy it |

## What should an electrician post?

Your posts should answer what a customer worries about: Are they qualified? Will it be safe and signed off? Do they cover my area? What will it cost? A good rotation:

- **Finished jobs.** Consumer unit swaps, rewires, garden lighting, EV chargers, with the town and a photo.
- **Trust posts.** Scheme registration, Part P, insurance, certificates.
- **Safety tips.** Overloaded extension leads, old fuse boxes, outdoor sockets.
- **Landlord reminders.** EICR due dates, remedial work.
- **Common questions.** "Do I need a new consumer unit?" "How long does a rewire take?"
- **Reviews**, quoted word for word.

## Trust posts that win electrical work

Most homeowners cannot judge good wiring, so they judge you on credentials and how you explain them. These are the posts that do the most work.

### NICEIC, NAPIT and Part P

In England and Wales, notifiable electrical work in homes, such as a new circuit or a consumer unit replacement, falls under Part P of the Building Regulations. An electrician registered with a competent person scheme can self-certify that work, and the scheme notifies building control. If the electrician is not registered, the work has to be signed off another way. The [Planning Portal explains this](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/electrics/building-regulations-checking-for-safety), and customers can check you on the [Electrical Competent Person register](https://www.electricalcompetentperson.co.uk/).

> Getting a new consumer unit or an extra circuit? That is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations. We are NICEIC registered, which means we can certify it ourselves and the scheme tells building control for you. You get an electrical installation certificate and a building regulations compliance certificate. You can check any electrician on the Electrical Competent Person register before they start.

Swap NICEIC for NAPIT or your own scheme, and only post it if it is true.

### EICRs for landlords

Under the electrical safety standards rules, landlords in England must have the electrics in rented homes inspected and tested at least every five years, give a copy of the report to their tenants, and carry out any required remedial work. The [government guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance), updated in November 2025 to cover social housing too, has the detail. Wales and Scotland have their own rules, so check before you post if you work there.

> Landlords in Nottingham and Beeston: your Electrical Installation Condition Report needs doing at least every five years, and your tenants need a copy. If yours was done when the rules came in, it may be due soon. We can test while the property is occupied and send the report to you and your agent.

### EV chargers

If you are an OZEV-approved installer, say so. The government's [EV chargepoint grant for renters and flat owners](https://www.gov.uk/electric-vehicle-chargepoint-grant-household/what-you-can-get) covers 75% of the cost of buying and installing a chargepoint, up to £500, for people with private off-street parking. Since 1 April 2026 the customer applies to OZEV directly after buying the charger, according to the government's [summary of the changes](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-electric-vehicle-chargepoint-grant-schemes-from-1-april-2026). Grant rules change, so link the official page and check it before you post.

> Renting, or living in a flat with your own parking space? You may be able to get 75% off the cost of a home EV charger, up to £500, through the government grant. We are OZEV-approved installers covering Leicester and Oadby. Tap Learn more for how the grant works.

### Insurance and certificates

State your public liability cover and what paperwork the customer gets: electrical installation certificate, minor works certificate, building regulations certificate. Customers like to know they are getting a paper trail.

> Daisy-chaining extension leads behind the TV? It is one of the most common things we see at Christmas. Each lead has a maximum load, and plugging a heater or kettle into one can overheat it. If you are always short of sockets, adding a double socket is usually a short, affordable job.

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

1. **Quote the review word for word**, first name only.
2. **Add what the job was.** A review saying "sorted it fast" is more convincing when you explain it was a tripping RCD caused by a water-damaged outdoor socket.
3. **Ask every customer for a review.** Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans incentives and bans asking only the happy ones.

Example:

> "Explained everything, tidied up after himself and the certificate was in my inbox that night." Thanks, Priya in West Bridgford. That was a consumer unit upgrade on a 1960s semi, with two extra circuits for the kitchen.

## Where should an electrician start?

1. **Fill in your Google Business Profile properly.** Services, service areas, hours and real photos.
2. **Write one scheme registration post** (NICEIC, NAPIT or yours) and pin the idea for reuse every few months.
3. **List your landlord customers' EICR dates** and post a reminder each quarter.
4. **Ask your last five customers for a review.**
