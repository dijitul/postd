A cafe's social media has one job: getting local people through the door this week. That means posting where they look when they are deciding where to go, which is Google first (search and Maps) and Facebook second, with Instagram if your food photographs well. Post two or three times a week about specials, new menu items, opening hours and the little practical details people want to know. Below is a 12-month UK calendar, trust posts, examples you can copy and a worked example of the time involved.

## Where do cafe customers look?

People choose a cafe in the moment: "coffee near me", "brunch Derby", "dog friendly cafe Matlock". They look at the map, compare photos and reviews, check you are open, and walk in. Your Google Business Profile carries most of that decision, and your posts show you are open, busy and worth the walk.

| Platform | What it does for a cafe | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Search and Maps, hours, photos, reviews, posts | Yes, always |
| Facebook Page | Regulars, local groups, events, changes to hours | Yes |
| Instagram | Food and interior photos, younger customers | If your food photographs well |
| TikTok | Can bring visitors from further away | Optional |
| LinkedIn | Only if you cater for offices or events | Rarely |

Our [complete guide to Google Business Profile posts](/guides/google-business-profile-posts) covers the post types and rules.

## What should a cafe post?

- **Today's or this week's special**, with a photo of the actual plate.
- **New menu items**, where the idea came from and what is in it.
- **Practical information**: dogs welcome, high chairs, step-free access, parking, laptop-friendly or not.
- **Opening hours**, especially changes and bank holidays.
- **Your suppliers**: the local bakery, roastery or farm.
- **Behind the scenes**: the morning bake, a new barista, a busy Saturday.
- **Reviews**, quoted word for word.
- **Events**: quiz nights, live music, a supper club.

## Trust posts for cafes

### Food hygiene rating

The Food Standards Agency's food hygiene rating scheme gives every food business a rating from 0 to 5. In Wales and Northern Ireland you must display the sticker; in England it is voluntary but encouraged, according to the [government's summary of the scheme](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-hygiene-rating-scheme/food-hygiene-rating-scheme). If you have a 5, it is a good post:

> We have just had our food hygiene inspection and kept our 5, the top rating. Thanks to Dan and the kitchen team, who clean down every afternoon whether anyone is watching or not. You can look up any food business's rating on the Food Standards Agency website.

### Allergens

Allergy information is a real worry for some customers, and posts that show you take it seriously build trust. Food you prepare and pack on site before sale, such as a wrapped sandwich, must carry a full ingredients label with the 14 allergens emphasised, under the rules known as Natasha's Law. The [Food Standards Agency's guidance for cafes](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-guidance/prepacked-for-direct-sale-ppds-allergen-labelling-changes-for-restaurants-cafes-and-pubs) explains what counts.

> Got an allergy? Every sandwich and salad we pack in the morning carries a full ingredients label with allergens in bold. For anything made to order, ask any of us and we will check the recipe with the kitchen. We would rather you asked twice than not at all.

Never claim a dish is "allergen-free" or "safe for coeliacs" unless you have the processes to back it.

### Suppliers and sourcing

"Our bread comes from the bakery two doors down" is trust. So is naming your coffee roaster. Only make claims you can stand behind.

## Example posts you can copy

**Today's special (Google update):**

> Today's soup is roasted tomato and red pepper with a cheese scone, £6.50 eat in or take away. Made this morning, gone by two most days.

**New menu item:**

> New from Monday: the Derbyshire oatcake breakfast, with bacon, cheese and a fried egg, wrapped in proper oatcakes from our local oatcake shop. We have had so many people asking for it that we gave in.

**Practical information:**

> Yes, dogs are welcome in the front room, and we keep a jar of dog biscuits on the counter. Water bowls are by the door. Paws on the floor, please, not the chairs.

**Opening hours change:**

> Heads up: we are closing at 2pm this Saturday for a private party. Back to normal hours on Sunday, 9 till 4.

**Christmas bookings:**

> Christmas lunch bookings are open for groups of 6 to 20, weekdays until 23 December. Two courses for £22 or three for £27. Tap Learn more for the menu.

**Behind the scenes:**

> 6.45am and the scones are in. Fifty plain, thirty fruit, and a tray of cheese for the soup. This is why the kitchen smells like it does when you walk in at eight.

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

Reviews of cafes are often short and specific, which makes them good posts:

1. **Quote word for word**, first name only.
2. **Add a photo of the thing they mentioned**: the cake, the room, the coffee.
3. **Say something back**: who made it, where it comes from.
4. **Ask every customer**, not just the regulars who love you. Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans offering free coffee or discounts for reviews.

Example:

> "Best flat white in Belper and they remembered my order on the second visit." Thanks, Mark. That is Ellie, who has a memory for orders that we find slightly alarming.
