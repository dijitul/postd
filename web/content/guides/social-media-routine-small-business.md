A workable weekly social media routine for a small business takes about 30 minutes: one 20-minute session to write and schedule the week's posts, plus two quick checks a day for reviews, comments and messages. Once a month, spend 15 minutes checking your Google profile and seeing what got a response. Once a quarter, plan the season ahead. If you hand the posts over, the routine shrinks to a few minutes of approving and the replies.

January is a good time to start one, because nobody remembers what you did not post in December.

## What does a 30-minute weekly routine look like?

Here is the whole week. The only fixed appointment is the planning session; the rest are checks that fit around work.

| When | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Monday, 7.30am | 20 minutes | Planning session: pick photos, write two or three posts, schedule them for the week |
| Every day, lunchtime | 2 to 3 minutes | Check new reviews, comments and messages; reply to anything simple |
| Every day, end of work | 2 to 3 minutes | Same again; anything that needs a proper answer gets one now |
| Friday | 1 minute | Favourite this week's two best job photos so Monday is easy |

That comes to about 20 minutes of planning and, depending on how busy your inbox is, 20 to 30 minutes of replies across the week. Replies are the part that varies most, and the part no routine can batch, because customers do not wait for Monday.

## How do you run the 20-minute planning session?

Same order every week, so it becomes automatic.

1. **Photos (4 minutes).** Open your camera roll and the photos you favourited on Friday. Pick one per post. Check backgrounds for house numbers, faces and paperwork.
2. **Reviews (2 minutes).** Any new Google reviews since last week? Reply to them, and pick the best one as a post. Our guide to [turning Google reviews into social media posts](/guides/turn-google-reviews-into-social-media-posts) covers quoting exactly and using first names.
3. **Pick the angles (2 minutes).** Two or three different types of post, so the week does not feel samey: a job, a review, a tip.
4. **Write (8 minutes).** Two or three sentences each. Shorter for Google (and no phone numbers: Google [may remove posts that include them](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB)), a bit warmer for Facebook, business-to-business for LinkedIn.
5. **Schedule (4 minutes).** Use the free schedulers in [Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), [Meta Business Suite](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1252240869631062) and [LinkedIn Pages](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548192/scheduled-posts-for-linkedin-pages). Spread the posts over different days.

Those timings assume one or two platforms. Add about five minutes for each extra platform you write a separate version for. Our breakdown of [how long social media takes](/guides/how-long-does-social-media-take) shows the full sums if you want to cost it.

## What goes in a week of posts?

A simple rotation means you never sit there wondering what to say. Cycle through these over a month:

| Week | Post 1 | Post 2 | Post 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Job of the week | Real review | Tip |
| 2 | Service spotlight | Behind the scenes | FAQ answered |
| 3 | Job of the week | Real review | Local: an area you cover or an event |
| 4 | Seasonal reminder | Before and after | What is new: a service, a member of staff, a page on your website |

If you only post once a week, take the first column. For ready-made wording, see our [Google Business Profile post templates](/guides/google-business-profile-post-templates). For how often each platform needs feeding, see [how often to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/how-often-post-google-business-profile).

## What should you do once a month?

Fifteen minutes on the first Monday of the month, after the weekly session.

- **Check your Google Business Profile details.** Hours, phone number, services, areas covered. Change anything that has drifted.
- **Look back at last month's posts.** Which ones got calls, messages, comments? Do more of those.
- **Add new photos to your profile,** not just to posts.
- **Check upcoming dates.** Bank holidays, school holidays, local events, anything that changes your hours.
- **Look at your website.** New services or prices not on the site yet? Fix them, because your posts and your site should say the same thing.

## What should you do once a quarter?

Half an hour, four times a year.

- **Plan the season.** What is your next three months like? A plumber in Mansfield thinks boiler services in autumn and frozen pipes in winter; a café in Derby thinks summer menus and Christmas bookings; a cleaner in Leicester thinks spring cleans and end-of-tenancy season.
- **Review your platforms.** Is each one earning its place? If LinkedIn never brings anything and you are not selling to businesses, it is fine to stop. Our guide to whether a small business needs a [LinkedIn Company Page](/guides/linkedin-company-page-small-business) helps decide, and [Facebook Page or Google Business Profile](/guides/facebook-page-vs-google-business-profile) compares the two that matter most to local firms.
- **Check who has access.** Remove former staff and old tools from your Facebook Page, Google profile and LinkedIn Page. Make sure you, the owner, are an admin on all of them.
- **Schedule ahead for busy patches and holidays.** See [how to keep posting over Christmas without working Christmas](/guides/schedule-social-media-over-christmas) for the approach; it works for any holiday.

## What does the routine look like for different businesses?

Three worked examples. The times are estimates for someone who has done the routine for a few weeks.

| | Plumber, Mansfield | Café, Derby | Cleaner, Leicester |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platforms | Google, Facebook | Facebook, Google | Facebook, Google |
| Posts a week | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| Planning session | Monday 7am in the van, 20 minutes | Sunday after close, 25 minutes | Friday 3pm, 20 minutes |
| Reply checks | Lunch and 5pm | Mid-morning lull and after close | Between jobs and evening |
| Best content | Finished jobs, reviews, seasonal tips | Today's bakes, the team, regulars' reviews | Before and afters, reviews, what is included |
| Monthly | Hours and service areas | Menu and opening hours | Prices and areas covered |

## How do you keep the routine going?

Most routines die in the third week. A few things help:

- **Put the session in your diary as a job,** with a reminder. It is easier to keep an appointment than a good intention.
- **Lower the bar on bad weeks.** One post is better than none. A photo and a sentence is a post.
- **Batch ahead before busy spells.** If you know a big job is coming, do two weeks in one session.
- **Do not chase daily posting.** It is the fastest way to burn out and stop altogether.
- **Keep a running list** in your phone of post ideas as they occur: questions customers ask, jobs you are proud of, things you wish people knew.

## The routine with none of the writing

If even 20 minutes a week will not happen, hand the posts over and keep the parts only you can do. A freelancer can write and schedule for you, or a tool can write and publish from your website and reviews.

Either way, the routine becomes:

| When | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Once a week | 5 to 10 minutes | Read the coming week's posts, edit anything wrong, add a job photo or two |
| Twice a day | 2 to 3 minutes | Replies to reviews, comments and messages |
| Once a month | 15 minutes | The same monthly checks as above |

That is the "or none" in the title: not quite none, because replies and facts still need you, but close.
