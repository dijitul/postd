If you have no time for social media, do less, but do it every week. Pick one or two platforms (Google Business Profile first if local people search for what you do), post once to three times a week, and do the whole week's posts in a single 30-minute sitting using the free schedulers. Take photos as you work so you are never starting from nothing. If even that does not happen, hand the writing over, to a person or a tool, and keep only the replies.

That is the plan in one paragraph. The rest of this guide is how to make it stick when you are on site from 7am and doing quotes at 9pm.

## Why does social media take so long?

Because the time does not go where people think. Pressing publish is quick. Working out what to say, writing it three ways, finding a decent photo and remembering to do it all at the right moment is what eats the evening.

In our step-by-step breakdown of [how long social media takes](/guides/how-long-does-social-media-take), three posts a week on three platforms works out at about 54 minutes per post, or roughly 11.7 hours a month, before you answer a single comment. That is a worked example with stated assumptions, but most owners who time themselves are surprised.

So "no time" usually means one of two things:

- **No ideas.** You open the app, cannot think of anything, close it.
- **No routine.** You post when you remember, which is never in a busy week.

Fix those two and the time needed drops sharply.

## What is the least you can do and still have it be worth it?

Here are three realistic levels. Pick the one you will actually keep doing, not the one that sounds best.

| Time a week | What you do | What you skip |
|---|---|---|
| 15 minutes | One Google Business Profile post. Reply to any new reviews | Every other platform |
| 30 minutes | One Google post and one Facebook post, scheduled in one sitting. Replies | LinkedIn, X, Instagram, video |
| 60 minutes | Two or three posts a week across Google, Facebook and, if you work with businesses, LinkedIn. Replies | Daily posting, trends, video |

For more on frequency, see [how often to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/how-often-post-google-business-profile). The short version: regular beats frequent.

## Which platform should you keep?

If you only keep one, keep the one people use when they are ready to buy.

- **Google Business Profile.** For trades and local services, this is where "plumber near me" ends up. Posts appear on your profile in Search and Maps. Keep it.
- **Facebook Page.** Strong for local community, recommendations and repeat customers, especially for cafés, salons, dog groomers and cleaners.
- **LinkedIn Company Page.** Worth it if your customers are other businesses: letting agents, facilities managers, offices.
- **X.** Fine if you already use it. If you do not, it is the first to drop.
- **Instagram and TikTok.** Great for visual businesses that can film. If you are a heating engineer with no time, they are not where to start.

## The 30-minute weekly session

This is the routine that makes "no time" manageable. Do it at the same time every week: Friday after the last job, Sunday evening, Monday at 7am in the van before the first call. Whatever you will stick to.

1. **Open your camera roll (5 minutes).** Pick two or three photos from this week's jobs. A finished bathroom, the team, a before and after, the new van.
2. **Check your reviews (2 minutes).** Any new ones? A good review is a post already written. Reply to it while you are there.
3. **Write the posts (15 minutes).** One idea per post. Two or three sentences is plenty: what you did, where, who it helps. Write the Google version without your phone number, because Google [may remove posts with phone numbers in the text](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB): use the Call now button instead.
4. **Schedule them (8 minutes).** Use Google Business Profile's own [scheduling](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), [Meta Business Suite](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1252240869631062) for Facebook and [LinkedIn's scheduler](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548192/scheduled-posts-for-linkedin-pages) for your Company Page. Spread them across the week.

Done. Close the apps until next week, apart from replies.

## How do you stop running out of ideas?

Keep a short list of post types you can always fall back on. Each one works for almost any local business, and you can rotate through them for months.

| Post type | Example (an electrician in Leicester) |
|---|---|
| Job of the week | "Full rewire finished in a Victorian terrace in Clarendon Park this week." |
| Review | Quote a real review exactly, with the customer's first name |
| Service spotlight | "Did you know we fit EV chargers? Here is what is involved." |
| Tip | "If your lights flicker when the kettle goes on, here is what it might mean." |
| FAQ | "Do I need an EICR to sell my house?" answered in three sentences |
| Seasonal | Christmas lights safety in December, garden lighting in spring |
| Behind the scenes | The team, the van, the new testing kit |
| Local | An area you cover, a local event you are supporting |

## How to capture content while you work

The 30-minute session only works if you have something to work with. Build two small habits:

- **Take a photo before you pack up.** Every job, even if most of them never get posted. Check there is nothing personal in the shot (house numbers, people, paperwork) and ask the customer if it is their home.
- **Voice-note the story.** On the drive back, 20 seconds: "Boiler in Kirkby, 15 years old, customer had no heating for three days, sorted same day." That is a post.

## What can you stop doing without guilt?

- **Posting every day.** It is not needed for a local business.
- **Chasing trends and trending audio.** Unless you run a business that suits it.
- **Stuffing posts with hashtags.** Two or three relevant ones at most, and none on Google.
- **Copying the same post everywhere.** It is better to post once on Google and once on Facebook than to paste one text into four places where it does not fit.
- **Feeling bad about a quiet week.** Just schedule ahead so the quiet weeks do not show.

## What about replies?

Replies are the part you cannot batch, because customers do not wait for Sunday. Two short checks a day, at lunch and at the end of the day, are enough for most small businesses. Turn on notifications for messages and reviews only, not likes.

Keep replies human. A plumber in Mansfield answering "can you come out tomorrow?" knows things no tool does.

## When should you hand it over?

If you have tried the weekly session for a month and it still does not happen, that is useful information, not a failure. The options are:

| Option | Roughly | Suits you if |
|---|---|---|
| Keep doing it yourself, less often | Your time | You will do 15 minutes a week |
| A freelancer | £150 to £1,200 a month, per published UK guides | You want a person, photos and maybe video |
| A tool that writes and publishes | From £15 a month | You want regular posts without writing them |

If you go with AI writing of any kind, read [what Google and Meta say about AI-written posts](/guides/ai-generated-social-media-posts) first. The short answer is that it is allowed, but you are responsible for what goes out.
