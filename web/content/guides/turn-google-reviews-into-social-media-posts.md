To turn a Google review into a social media post, copy the review word for word, use the customer's first name only, add a line of context about the job, and post it with your own photo rather than a screenshot. Ideally ask the customer first, because the reviewer owns the words they wrote. Never change what a review says, and never present a few hand-picked reviews as your overall rating.

Done that way, review posts are among the most useful posts a local business can publish: they are true, they are in your customers' words, and they cost you nothing to write.

## Why are reviews such good social media posts?

A review is the one thing on your social media that you did not write. When a café owner in Derby says the coffee is good, people shrug. When a customer called Priya says it, people believe it.

Reviews also solve the hardest part of posting regularly, which is having something to say. If you get two or three reviews a month, you have two or three posts a month already written.

## What does Google say about sharing reviews?

Google is relaxed about it, but it is worth knowing exactly what it says.

- **Google calls reviews testimonials.** Its [help page on managing reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050?hl=en-GB) says positive reviews provide helpful testimonials for your business, and shows you how to copy a link to any single review (Google Maps, your business, Reviews, More, Share review, Copy link).
- **The reviewer owns the words.** [Google's Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms?gl=GB&hl=en-GB) say your content remains yours, and use reviews you write as an example. The reviewer gives Google a licence to show the review; that licence is to Google, not to you.
- **There is no built-in "turn a review into a post" button.** When we checked in November 2026 we could not find one in Google Business Profile. You copy the text into a new post yourself, or use a tool that does it.
- **Your Google posts must follow Google's posts policy.** Google says [you are responsible for making sure your posted content complies with the law](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB), and may remove posts with phone numbers in the text, so keep contact details out and use the Call now button.

So Google is not the obstacle. The things to get right are UK advertising rules, consumer law on reviews and data protection.

## What UK rules apply to sharing reviews?

Three sets of rules matter. None of them stops you sharing reviews; all of them shape how.

### The advertising code (CAP Code)

Posts promoting your business count as marketing, and the UK advertising code covers testimonials. The ASA's advice on [testimonials and endorsements](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html) (updated September 2026) sets out the main rules:

| Rule | What it means for a review post |
|---|---|
| 3.47 | Hold evidence the testimonial is genuine, and contact details for the person. A real Google review linked to a real job is good evidence |
| 3.48 | The testimonial must relate to what you are advertising, and must not be taken out of context or edited in a misleading way |
| 3.49 | Claims inside a testimonial must not mislead. If a review says "cheapest in Leicester", you need to be able to back that up before you repeat it |
| 3.50 | No testimonial without permission. The ASA notes an exception for accurate quotes from a published source, but you still need evidence the words are genuine |

A public Google review is arguably a published source. Even so, asking takes one message, avoids any argument, and most happy customers are pleased to be asked.

### The ban on fake and misleading reviews

Since 6 April 2025, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act has banned fake reviews and misleading presentation of reviews. The CMA's [fake reviews guidance (CMA208)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208) explains what that covers. For a small business sharing reviews, the practical points are:

- only share real reviews, from real customers, exactly as written
- never write a review yourself, or ask staff, friends or family to
- do not hide or suppress negative reviews, or present a selection of good ones as if it were your overall picture
- if you quote a star rating or review count, keep it accurate and current

### Data protection

A reviewer's name is personal data under UK GDPR (see the ICO's guide to [what personal data is](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/personal-information-what-is-it/what-is-personal-information-a-guide/)). Using a first name only, and nothing that pins down where they live, is the simplest way to share the praise without sharing more about the person than you need.

This is a practical summary, not legal advice. If you are in a regulated trade, such as healthcare or financial services, check your own regulator's rules on testimonials too.

## Do you need to ask the customer first?

Our advice: ask when you can, and always quote exactly when you cannot. A short message is enough:

> Hi Sarah, thank you so much for the lovely review. Would you mind if we shared it on our Facebook and Google pages? We would only use your first name.

Keep the reply. If someone later asks you to take their review down from your social media, do it straight away and without fuss.

## Why use first names only?

Because it is enough. "Sarah" with the job described ("boiler replaced in Mansfield") is just as believable as a full name, and it protects the customer.

Things to leave out:

- surnames, unless the customer has said they are happy to be named
- profile photos from their Google account
- house names, street names or anything that identifies their home
- photos of their property, unless they have agreed

A general area is usually fine ("a customer in Mansfield"), but for a small village or a distinctive job, even that can identify someone. Use your judgement.

## How do you turn a review into a post, step by step?

1. **Pick the review.** Look for one that mentions something specific: a service, a member of staff, how quickly you came out, a problem you solved.
2. **Copy it exactly.** Paste it into a notes app. Do not fix spelling or grammar inside the quote.
3. **Shorten if needed, honestly.** Cut whole sentences and mark gaps with an ellipsis (...). Never cut in a way that changes the meaning, such as dropping "but the wait was long" from the middle of a sentence.
4. **Ask permission,** if you can reach them.
5. **Add a line of context.** What was the job? "Sarah had us out for an emergency boiler repair last week."
6. **Use your own photo,** not a screenshot: the job, the team, the shop front.
7. **Adapt it for each platform** (see below).
8. **Keep a note** of which review you used and when, so you do not repeat it too often.

## Should you use a screenshot or type the review?

| | Screenshot | Typed quote |
|---|---|---|
| Shows the reviewer's full name and photo | Yes, unless you crop or blur | No |
| Easy to read on a phone | Often not | Yes |
| Works with screen readers | No | Yes |
| Looks like proof | Yes | Add "from our Google reviews" |

If you want to show proof, link to the review using Google's Share review option, or simply say where the review came from.

## What does a good review post look like on each platform?

Here is one review for a café in Derby, written four ways. The review: *"Best flat white in Derby and the staff remembered my order on my second visit. Lovely little place."* Posted by a customer called Tom.

**Google Business Profile:**

> "Best flat white in Derby and the staff remembered my order on my second visit." Thank you, Tom! We roast in small batches every week and love getting to know our regulars. Pop in, we are open from 7.30am Monday to Saturday.

**Facebook Page:**

> This one made our week. Tom left us a review saying we make the best flat white in Derby, and that the team remembered his order on his second visit. That is exactly what we are here for. Thank you, Tom, see you tomorrow morning.

**LinkedIn Company Page:**

> A lovely review from Tom: "Best flat white in Derby and the staff remembered my order on my second visit." We also do coffee and pastries for office meetings across the city centre. If you are planning a team breakfast, we would be glad to help.

**X:**

> "Best flat white in Derby and the staff remembered my order on my second visit." Thanks, Tom. See you tomorrow.

The quote stays identical every time. Only the wrapper changes.

## How often should you post reviews?

Mix them in rather than posting nothing else. One review post in every four or five keeps your feed believable; a page that is all five-star quotes starts to look like an advert. ## Mistakes to avoid

- **Tidying up the review.** Fixing a typo inside quote marks changes their words. Leave it.
- **Combining two reviews into one quote.** That creates a review nobody wrote.
- **Quoting an old rating.** "Rated 4.9 from 200 reviews" needs to be true today.
- **Offering a discount for a review.** Google's [content rules](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) ban offering incentives, such as payment, discounts or free services, in exchange for a review. Sharing an incentivised review as a testimonial makes it worse.
- **Using a review for something it does not cover.** A review of your boiler service does not support a post about bathroom fitting.
- **Ignoring a takedown request.** If a customer asks you to remove their review from your page, do it.
