Meta Business Suite's scheduler is free, works well, and is enough for a business that only posts to Facebook and Instagram and is happy writing every post itself. It stops at Meta's own platforms, so it cannot post to your Google Business Profile, LinkedIn or X, and it does not know anything about your business. You pay for a tool like postd when you want posts written from your own website and reviews, or published to Google and LinkedIn as well.

We make postd, so we have an interest here. This guide is honest about the cases where free is the right answer, and there are plenty.

## What is the Meta Business Suite scheduler?

Meta Business Suite is Meta's free control panel for a Facebook Page and its linked Instagram account. You reach it at business.facebook.com or through the Meta Business Suite app. [Meta's help centre](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/205614130852988) describes it as the place to create and schedule Facebook and Instagram content and see it on a calendar.

For scheduling, the parts you will use are:

- **Create post**: write a post, add photos or video, choose your Facebook Page, your Instagram account or both, then pick a date and time instead of publishing now.
- **Planner**: a calendar of everything published and scheduled, including posts, stories and reels, where you can drag things around and fill gaps.
- **Insights**: reach and engagement for what you have posted.
- **Inbox**: Messenger, Instagram DMs and comments in one place, with simple [automated responses](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/395965998733706) such as instant replies, away messages and keyword replies.

All of this is free.

## What does Meta Business Suite do well?

Quite a lot, honestly.

- **It is free.** There is no paid plan to upgrade to for scheduling, and no per-user fee.
- **It is Meta's own tool.** New Facebook and Instagram formats tend to work here first. Third-party schedulers depend on what Meta lets them do through its API.
- **Instagram Reels, stories and carousels.** It handles the Instagram formats properly, which matters for a salon, a café or a dog groomer whose customers live on Instagram.
- **One post to both places.** You can write once and send it to your Facebook Page and Instagram account together, with tweaks for each.
- **The Planner is genuinely useful.** Sitting down on a Sunday and filling the week is easy.

If your business lives on Facebook and Instagram, and you like writing your own posts, you may never need anything else for scheduling.

## Where does the free scheduler stop?

There are four gaps, and whether they matter depends on your business.

### 1. It only posts to Facebook and Instagram

Meta Business Suite cannot publish to Google Business Profile, LinkedIn or X. For many local businesses, Google is where the customers are searching. A plumber in Mansfield gets far more calls from people finding him on Google Maps than from his Facebook Page. Google has its own free scheduler now (our [guide to Google Business Profile posts](/guides/google-business-profile-posts) covers it), but that means two tools, two logins and two sets of writing.

### 2. It does not write anything for you

You still have to come up with the idea and the words for every post. Meta has added some AI suggestions, but they do not know your services, your prices or what your customers said about you last week. The hard part of social media is rarely pressing "schedule". It is thinking of something worth saying on a Thursday night after a long day.

### 3. Scheduling has a time limit

Meta limits how far ahead you can schedule. Most 2026 guides, such as [SocialBee's](https://socialbee.com/blog/meta-planner-30-day-post-scheduling-restriction/), put it at 75 days, while older ones say 29 or 30. Either way, you cannot set up a year of posts and forget it. For most owners this is not a real problem, because you would not want to.

### 4. Nothing reminds you to keep going

A scheduler is only as good as the batch of posts you put into it. When you get busy, the queue runs dry and the Page goes quiet. That is not a fault in the tool, but it is the most common way free scheduling fails in practice.

## When is free enough?

Free is enough if most of these are true:

- Facebook and Instagram are the only places you post, or the only ones you care about.
- You enjoy writing posts, or someone in the business does.
- You can set aside an hour or so a week to plan and schedule.
- You already have plenty of photos and ideas.
- Google Business Profile is either not important to you or you are happy posting there separately.

A Nottingham salon that posts its work on Instagram every day, where the stylists take the photos as they go, is a good example. Meta Business Suite is the right tool, and paying for a scheduler would be wasted money.

## When is it worth paying?

It is worth paying when:

- **Google matters more than Facebook.** Trades, cleaners, accountants and most service businesses win work from Google searches.
- **You want LinkedIn too.** An accountant or a commercial cleaning company in Leicester will get more from a LinkedIn Company Page than from Instagram.
- **Nobody has time to write.** If posts only happen when the owner finds a spare evening, they mostly do not happen.
- **You want the posts to come from what you actually do.** postd works through your website one page at a time and quotes your real reviews, so posts stay specific.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and plenty of businesses should. A sensible split:

| Job | Tool |
|---|---|
| Instagram Reels, stories and photo posts | Meta Business Suite |
| Replying to Facebook and Instagram messages | Meta Business Suite inbox |
| Google Business Profile posts | postd |
| LinkedIn Company Page posts | postd |
| Facebook Page posts | One or the other, not both |

The only thing to watch is doubling up on Facebook. Decide which tool owns your Facebook Page posts, so customers do not see two similar posts on the same day.
