For most UK local businesses, Google Business Profile matters more, because it is what people see when they search for what you do near them, at the moment they are ready to call or visit. A Facebook Page is the better tool for keeping in touch with existing customers, local groups and word of mouth. If you can only do one well, start with Google. If you can manage both, the extra work is smaller than it looks, because the same ideas work on each with a little rewording.

We should say up front that we make postd, which publishes to both (plus LinkedIn and X), so we have no reason to push you towards one. What follows is how we see the difference after working with local businesses in Mansfield and across the East Midlands.

## What is the difference between them?

They do different jobs. Google Business Profile is where people find you. Facebook is where people who already know you keep up with you.

| | Google Business Profile | Facebook Page |
|---|---|---|
| Where it shows | Google Search and Maps | Facebook feed, groups, search within Facebook |
| When people see it | When they search for you or what you do | When they follow you, or someone shares or tags you |
| What they want | Hours, reviews, photos, a phone number, directions | News, personality, events, offers, community |
| Reviews | Google reviews, which feed into local ranking | Recommendations on your Page |
| Messaging | Chat was switched off in July 2024 | Messenger |
| Post types | Updates, offers, events | Text, photos, video, Reels, events, stories |
| Tone | Short, factual, with a button | Chattier, more personal |
| Cost | Free | Free (ads optional) |

## Why does Google Business Profile usually come first?

Because of intent. Someone typing "emergency plumber Mansfield" or "hairdresser near me" has a need right now. Your Google profile is the first thing they see, often before your website.

Google explains how it ranks local results on its [local ranking help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB): **relevance** (how well your profile matches the search), **distance** (how far you are from the searcher) and **prominence** (how well known you are, including your reviews and ratings). It says complete, accurate information and more reviews can help.

Note what that list does not include: posts. Google does not say posts improve your ranking. What posts do is help the person looking at your profile choose you over the next one, by showing recent work, real reviews and current offers. We go into the evidence in [do Google Business Profile posts help SEO?](/guides/do-google-business-profile-posts-help-seo)

The essentials on Google, in order:

1. Correct name, category, address or service area, phone and hours.
2. Special hours for bank holidays and Christmas.
3. Photos of your real work, team and premises.
4. Asking happy customers for reviews, and replying to every one.
5. A post a week. See [how often to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/how-often-post-google-business-profile).

## When does Facebook matter more?

Facebook can be the more important of the two for some businesses:

- **Cafés, pubs and restaurants**, where regulars want today's specials, events and opening changes.
- **Salons, barbers and groomers**, where last-minute gaps and before-and-after photos fill the diary.
- **Community-rooted businesses** whose customers live in local Facebook groups: villages, estates, school-gate networks.
- **Events, classes and clubs**, where Facebook events and reminders are part of how people plan.
- **Anyone whose customers message to book.** Facebook still has Messenger. Google does not: chat on Business Profiles [ended on 31 July 2024](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14919062).

And it is a poor fit for some. A commercial cleaner selling to offices, or an accountant serving limited companies, may find a LinkedIn Company Page more useful than Facebook, because that is where business buyers check out suppliers.

## Which should a plumber, café or salon prioritise?

| Business | Priority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Plumber, electrician, roofer | Google first, Facebook second | Most jobs start with a search |
| Café or coffee shop | Both, roughly equal | Google for new visitors, Facebook for regulars |
| Hair or beauty salon | Both, Facebook slightly ahead for bookings | Gaps and offers spread through followers |
| Restaurant or takeaway | Both, plus Instagram | Menus and hours on Google, specials on social |
| Domestic cleaner | Google first | People search "cleaner near me" |
| Commercial cleaner, accountant | Google first, then LinkedIn | Business buyers search, then check you out |
| Dog groomer | Both | Google for new clients, Facebook for photos and gaps |

## How do you write the same post for both?

Take one idea and write it twice. Here is a finished job for a plumber in Mansfield.

Google version, short with a button:

> New combi boiler fitted in Forest Town today, replacing a 20-year-old back boiler. Gas Safe registered, 10-year manufacturer warranty, and the old boiler taken away. Get a free quote using the button below.

Facebook version, chattier:

> Today's job in Forest Town: out with a 20-year-old back boiler, in with a new combi. The customer's been nursing the old one through two winters, so they are very pleased to have hot water on demand. Thanks for having us, Sue, and for the endless cups of tea.

Note the differences: no phone number in the Google text (Google's [posts content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB) says it may remove posts with contact details), first names and a bit of warmth on Facebook, and the same real photo on both.

And a café in Derby:

> Google: Soup of the day is leek and potato, with a cheese scone, £6.50. Eat in or takeaway, open until 4pm.
>
> Facebook: It is properly cold out, so the leek and potato soup is back, with Maria's cheese scones. We have made double today. Dogs welcome in the front room as always.

## How much time does running both take?

A worked example. One post a week on each platform. Writing the idea once and rewording it for the second platform takes about 20 minutes, plus 5 minutes finding a photo. Call it 25 minutes a week, or just under 22 hours a year. At £40 an hour, that is about £870 of an owner's time a year.

Add replying to reviews and messages (which we would always keep in your own hands, or at least approved by you) and you are at roughly an hour a week.
