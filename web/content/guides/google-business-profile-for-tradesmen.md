For a tradesman, a Google Business Profile is the free listing that appears when someone nearby searches "plumber near me" or "electrician Mansfield". To win jobs from it, set it up as a service-area business with your address hidden, list the towns you cover, choose the most specific category for your trade, add your services, ask every customer for a review, reply to all of them, and post once a week about real jobs. The setup takes an afternoon. The weekly routine takes about 15 minutes.

## Why does Google Business Profile matter so much for trades?

Because it is where people look first when something goes wrong. Someone with a leaking radiator in Sutton-in-Ashfield is not browsing Facebook. They search Google, see three businesses on a map, and ring one of them. The profile is the shop window for a business that does not have a shop.

It is also free. There is no paid version of a Business Profile, and Google's [help page on local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB) says: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

That help page also tells you what does matter. Google ranks local results on **relevance** (how well your profile matches the search), **distance** (how far you are from the searcher) and **prominence** (how well known you are, including your reviews). Everything in this guide is aimed at those three.

## How should a tradesman set up a Google Business Profile?

### 1. Hide your address and set service areas

Most trades work from home or a yard and go to the customer. Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en-GB) use exactly this example: a plumber running a business from a home address should clear the address from the profile.

Then set your service areas. According to [Google's service area help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en-GB):

- You can add **up to 20 service areas**, by town, city or postcode.
- The whole area should not usually be **more than about two hours' driving** from your base.
- You **cannot set a radius**, such as "25 miles from Mansfield".

To set them: go to your profile, select **Edit profile**, then **Location**, then **Edit** next to Service area. Add your towns and select **Save**. Google says approved edits can take up to 48 hours to show.

Be honest about where you actually go. A roofer in Mansfield who lists Sheffield, Leicester and Lincoln but never takes jobs there will waste their own time on calls they turn down.

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Do not show a home address | Clear the address field, keep it for verification only |
| No PO boxes or virtual offices | Google says these are not acceptable as a business address |
| One profile per business without a shopfront | Do not create a second profile for a second town |
| Up to 20 service areas | List the towns you genuinely cover |
| No radius | Use town names or postcodes |

If you also have a trade counter or showroom that is staffed during your opening hours, you can show that address and set a service area as well. Google calls these hybrid businesses.

### 2. Use your real business name

Your name on Google should match your van, your website and your invoices. Google's guidelines say "including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted", and list service descriptions and location details among the things that do not belong there.

So "Smith Plumbing & Heating" is fine if that is the name on the van. "Smith Plumbing Emergency Plumber Mansfield 24/7" breaks Google's guidelines and puts the profile at risk of suspension. Put the trade in your category and the towns in your service areas instead.

### 3. Choose the most specific category

Your primary category tells Google what you are. Google's [category guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669?hl=en-GB) says to choose a specific category rather than a broad one, and to add "a few" additional categories for other things you do, without adding one for every service.

How that works in practice:

| Trade | Primary category to look for | Additional categories, only if you really do the work |
|---|---|---|
| Plumber who fits boilers | Plumber | A heating category |
| Electrician | Electrician | An electrical installation or EV charger category |
| Roofer | Roofing contractor | A guttering category |
| Builder | Builder | A bricklaying or extension category |

Google's category list changes and the exact names vary, so type your trade into the category box and pick the closest match that appears. You cannot invent your own.

### 4. Add your services, hours and description

Add each service you offer in the **Services** section, in plain words customers use: "Boiler service", "Combi boiler installation", "Leak repair", "EICR for landlords". Set your normal hours accurately and add special hours for bank holidays. Our guide to [Christmas hours on Google Business Profile](/guides/google-business-profile-christmas-hours) covers the 2026 dates.

Write a short description that says what you do, where, and what makes you trustworthy: years trading, qualifications, guarantees. Google's ranking help says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local search results.

### 5. Add real photos

Google's ranking page recommends adding photos and videos. For a trade, that means:

- you and your team, in workwear, by the van
- finished jobs, from where the customer would stand
- before and after pairs, with the customer's permission
- the van with your name on it, which proves you are real

Google's [photo guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en-GB) ask for images that are in focus, well lit and without heavy filters. A phone photo is fine.

## How do you get more Google reviews as a tradesman?

Reviews feed prominence, and they are what customers read before ringing. Google's ranking help says "positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out."

The routine that works:

1. **Get your review link.** Google's [help page on reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en-GB) explains how to create a link or QR code customers can use.
2. **Ask every customer, at the end of the job.** In person, then send the link by text the same day while they are pleased.
3. **Do not offer anything in return.** Google treats free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews as fake engagement, which it says is "strictly prohibited". Ask everyone, not just the customers you know are happy. Google's own help notes that a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy.
4. **Reply to every review.** Thank people by first name and mention the job. Reply calmly to bad ones, stick to facts, and offer to put it right.

A review that says "Dan fixed our boiler in Rainworth the same day" helps more than one that says "great service", because it mentions the job and the area. You cannot write it for them, but you can remind them what you did: "If you have a minute to say what we fixed, it really helps."

## What should tradesmen post on Google Business Profile?

Posts are short updates on your profile. The best evidence says they are not a big ranking factor on their own, which we cover in [how often to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/how-often-post-google-business-profile). What they do is persuade the person comparing you with two other plumbers that you are busy, local and careful.

Five post types that suit trades:

| Post | Example |
|---|---|
| Finished job | "New consumer unit fitted in Ravenshead today. The old fuse box had no RCD protection..." |
| Review quote | A real review, word for word, with the customer's first name |
| Seasonal reminder | Boiler services in autumn, frozen pipe prevention in winter, gutters after leaf fall |
| Tip | "Where your stopcock is and why you should check it before Christmas" |
| Credentials | Gas Safe, NICEIC, NAPIT, TrustMark, insurance, guarantees, only if they are genuinely yours |

Never write your phone number into a post. Google says it may remove posts with unverified contact information, and the Call now button uses the number on your profile anyway. ## A 15-minute weekly routine for tradesmen

Doing this on a Friday afternoon keeps the profile ticking over without eating into the working week.

| Step | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 minutes | Pick your best job photo from the week |
| 2 | 6 minutes | Write one post: what, where, why it mattered, Call now button |
| 3 | 3 minutes | Reply to any new reviews |
| 4 | 2 minutes | Text your review link to this week's finished customers |
| 5 | 2 minutes | Glance at **Performance** for calls and direction requests |

The habit that makes this easy is taking photos as you go. Before you start the job and once it is tidied up. Without photos, step 1 becomes twenty minutes of scrolling.

### What the routine costs in time

A worked example: 15 minutes a week is 13 hours a year. If you value your time at £40 an hour, that is about £520 a year of your evenings. Add Facebook and the figure roughly doubles. That is cheap for the work it can bring in, but it is also the bit most trades stop doing by February. ## What should you not do?

1. **Do not create fake or duplicate profiles** for other towns. One business, one profile, unless you have a genuine staffed premises somewhere else.
2. **Do not stuff your name with keywords.** It risks suspension.
3. **Do not buy reviews or swap them with other trades.** It breaks Google's rules and UK law, which has banned fake consumer reviews since April 2025.
4. **Do not leave the profile untouched for months.** Google archives posts older than six months unless a date range is set, according to its [help centre](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), so a quiet profile eventually shows no updates at all.
5. **Do not ignore bad reviews.** A calm, factual reply reads well to the next customer.
