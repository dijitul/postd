Accountants and bookkeepers win local clients from Google searches and referrals, so post on your Google Business Profile for people searching nearby, on LinkedIn for business owners and referrers, and on Facebook for sole traders and landlords. The tax calendar writes most of your content for you: Self Assessment on 31 January, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax quarterly updates, payments on account and the tax year end. Below is a 2027 calendar with the official dates, trust posts, examples to copy and a worked example of the time involved.

## Where do clients look for an accountant?

A sole trader who has left their tax return late searches "accountant near me" or "self assessment accountant Nottingham" in January, compares the Google listings and rings the one that looks approachable with good reviews. A limited company director is more likely to ask another business owner, then check you on LinkedIn and your website.

| Platform | What it does for an accountant | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Local search, reviews, deadline posts | Yes, always |
| LinkedIn Company Page | Business owners, referrers, recruiting | Yes for most practices |
| Facebook Page | Sole traders, landlords, local groups | Useful for personal tax clients |
| Instagram and TikTok | Some accountants build big audiences, rarely local | Optional |
| X | Tax news and debate, not many local clients | Optional |

If you are deciding where to put the effort first, see [Facebook Page or Google Business Profile](/guides/facebook-page-vs-google-business-profile).

## What should an accountant post?

- **Deadline reminders**, with the official date and a link to GOV.UK.
- **Plain-English explainers**: payments on account, MTD, what counts as an allowable expense.
- **What to do if you have missed a deadline.**
- **Client capacity**: "taking on new sole trader clients for 2026 to 2027".
- **Fixed fees**, if you publish them.
- **Meet the team**: who does what.
- **Reviews**, quoted word for word.
- **Trust posts**: professional body membership, software certifications.

## Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: what to post

MTD for Income Tax is the biggest change many sole traders and landlords will face, and the questions are already coming in. According to [HMRC's eligibility guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-youre-eligible-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax):

| Qualifying income | Based on the tax year | Must use MTD from |
|---|---|---|
| Over £50,000 | 2024 to 2025 | 6 April 2026 |
| Over £30,000 | 2025 to 2026 | 6 April 2027 |
| Over £20,000 | 2026 to 2027 | 6 April 2028 |

Qualifying income is self-employment and property income added together, before expenses, so turnover rather than profit. Employment income, dividends and pensions do not count, according to HMRC's guide to [working out qualifying income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/work-out-your-qualifying-income-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax). That catches people out, so it is a good post on its own:

> Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: does it apply to you from April 2027? HMRC looks at your self-employment and property income added together, before expenses, on your 2025 to 2026 return. If that is over £30,000, you will need compatible software and quarterly updates from 6 April 2027. A landlord with £18,000 rent and a sole trader income of £14,000 is over the line, even if the profit is small. Check your own position with HMRC's eligibility checker, or ask us.

Keep MTD posts factual and link the HMRC pages. Exemptions exist, for example for people who are digitally excluded, so do not tell anyone they definitely must sign up.

## Trust posts for accountants

### Professional bodies and qualifications

If you are a member of ICAEW, ACCA, CIMA, AAT, CIOT, ATT or another body, say so and say what it means for clients: you follow a code of ethics, keep up with training and are regulated. Only use titles you are entitled to, such as "chartered accountant" or "chartered tax adviser", and follow your body's rules on how to describe your firm.

### Anti-money laundering supervision

Accountancy service providers must be supervised for anti-money laundering, either by a professional body or by HMRC, according to [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-accountancy-service-provider-registration). It is not exciting, but a short post explains why you ask new clients for ID, which saves awkward emails.

> Why do we ask new clients for ID? Accountants are required by law to check who our clients are, under the money laundering regulations. It is the same for every practice, and it takes five minutes with a passport or driving licence and a recent bill.

### Companies House identity checks

Directors must now verify their identity with Companies House. If you help clients with it, say so, and link [the GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/verify-your-identity-for-companies-house) rather than quoting deadlines from memory, since they depend on each company's confirmation statement date.

### Software certifications

If you are certified in Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent or other MTD-compatible software, say so. Clients choosing software for MTD will look for help.

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

Accountancy reviews tend to say the same thing in different words: "made it easy", "explained everything", "saved me stress". Use the specific ones:

1. **Quote word for word**, first name only, and only with nothing confidential in it. Never mention a client's tax position, even if they did.
2. **Add what kind of client they are**: "a sole trader", "a landlord with two properties".
3. **Ask every client after their return is filed**, not just the happy ones. Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans incentives and selective asking.
