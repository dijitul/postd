A social media inbox puts the direct messages and comments from Facebook, Instagram and TikTok on one screen, so you answer everything from one place instead of opening three apps and hoping you did not miss one. For a small business it is mostly about two things: replying faster, and nothing slipping through. The free option is Meta Business Suite, which covers Facebook and Instagram but not TikTok. Paid inboxes add TikTok comments, team sharing, AI drafted replies and better spam handling.

This guide covers why messages go missing in the first place, every realistic option from the native apps to paid tools, how much time one inbox saves (with the sums shown), and what to look for before you pay for anything. We make one of these tools, Postd Office, so I will say where it fits and where something else is the better choice.

## Why do small businesses miss social media messages?

Because the messages arrive in too many places, and each app hides them in its own way.

Take a hair salon in Nottingham. In a normal week a booking enquiry might arrive as:

- a Facebook Messenger message to the Page
- a comment under a Facebook post ("Do you have anything Saturday?")
- an Instagram DM, which may land in **Requests** rather than the main inbox if the person does not follow you
- a comment on an Instagram Reel
- a comment on a TikTok video

That is five places, across three apps, each with its own notifications. Some of them sit behind a profile switch, because the owner's personal Instagram and the salon's account live in the same app. Comments are the worst: an enquiry left under a post from three weeks ago is easy to miss completely.

None of this is a discipline problem. The apps were built for people chatting with friends, and a business account is bolted on.

## What actually goes wrong when messages are scattered?

Three things, in my experience of running social accounts for local businesses:

1. **Slow replies.** You see the message, mean to answer it after the next client, and it is tomorrow before you do. By then they have booked elsewhere. Speed matters more than most owners think, because someone messaging a local business is often messaging two or three at once.
2. **Missed replies.** The message never registers at all: a comment on an old post, an Instagram request, a TikTok comment on a video you posted weeks ago.
3. **Double replies or contradictions.** Two people in the team answer the same customer, one says £45, the other says £50. Or nobody answers, because each thought the other had.

There is also a cost that is easy to ignore: the bad stuff. Spam links, scam "page violation" messages pretending to be Meta, and the occasional abusive comment all land in the same places as real customers. Without a way to filter them, you either spend time on them or leave them sitting under your posts for customers to see.

### Option 1: the native apps

Facebook, Instagram and TikTok each have their own inbox and notifications. If you get three or four messages a week, this is fine. Turn notifications on for the business accounts, check Instagram's message requests once a day, and look at comments on recent posts when you post something new.

It stops working when you have a team (everyone needs logins), when volume grows, or when you are on the tools all day and only see your phone at lunch.

### Option 2: Meta Business Suite

Meta Business Suite is free and its [Inbox](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/294426838452244) puts your Facebook Page messages, Facebook comments, Instagram DMs and Instagram comments in one list. You can use it on a computer or in the Meta Business Suite phone app. For a business that only uses Facebook and Instagram, this is the first thing to try, and for some it will be all they need.

It also has [automated responses](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/395965998733706): an instant reply to someone's first message, an away message, suggested replies for up to five frequently asked questions, and custom keyword replies (desktop only, and the keywords must match exactly). Meta also offers [inbox suggestions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/991413128263758), replies it proposes that you accept or ignore.

What it does not do:

- **TikTok.** Meta's tools only cover Meta's platforms.
- **Answer from your own approved information.** Instant replies and FAQ replies are fixed text. They cannot answer "Do you do balayage on Saturdays?" unless you wrote that exact reply in advance.
- **Give everyone simple access.** Team members are added through Meta's business portfolio permissions, which needs each person to have their own Facebook account and confuses most people the first time.

For a business on Facebook and Instagram only, with one or two people answering, those gaps may not matter.

### Option 3: general social media management tools

Tools like Agorapulse and Sprout Social include a shared inbox alongside scheduling, reports and listening. They are well made and they cover more networks than anything else here. They are priced per user:

- Agorapulse lists Standard at $79 a user a month billed annually, or $99 billed monthly ([Agorapulse pricing](https://www.agorapulse.com/pricing/), checked November 2026). That is about £60 to £75 a user at current rates.
- Sprout Social lists Essentials at $79 a seat a month billed annually and Standard at $199 a seat ([Sprout Social pricing](https://sproutsocial.com/pricing/), checked November 2026), about £60 and £150 at current rates.

If you have a marketing person who also needs scheduling, analytics and several brands, one of these may well be the right choice. For a plumber and a part-time office manager, three users on a per-seat plan adds up quickly.

### Option 4: chat automation tools

ManyChat and tools like it are built to run automated conversations, typically "comment PRICE on this Reel and we will DM you the link". They are very good at that. ManyChat's [pricing page](https://manychat.com/pricing) lists Pro from $29 a month billed annually (about £22 at current rates), priced by how many contacts you talk to each month. These tools are less suited to the ordinary, messy questions a local business gets ("Can you come out to Ravenshead on Thursday? It's leaking under the sink").

## How much time does one inbox save?

Here is a worked example. Change the numbers to fit your own week.

**Assumptions** for a café in Derby with an owner and one manager:

- They check Facebook, Instagram and TikTok separately, four times a working day.
- Each round of checks takes about 9 minutes: opening each app, switching to the business profile, looking at messages, requests and recent comments, and remembering where they got to. Call it 3 minutes an app.
- With one inbox, each check takes about 4 minutes, because everything new is in one list.
- They work 6 days a week.
- Their time is worth £35 an hour.

**Checking three apps:** 4 checks x 9 minutes x 6 days = 216 minutes, or 3.6 hours a week.

**One inbox:** 4 checks x 4 minutes x 6 days = 96 minutes, or 1.6 hours a week.

**Saved:** 2 hours a week. At £35 an hour that is £70 a week, or about £303 a month (£70 x 52 ÷ 12).

That is the time saved on checking alone. It does not count the time saved writing replies if the tool drafts them, and it does not count the bigger number: work you win because a reply went out in ten minutes rather than the next day.

A rough way to put a value on that, again with stated assumptions: if the café gets 8 enquiries a week through social media (table bookings, cake orders, private hire), one of those is missed or answered too late, and a third of missed enquiries would have become a £60 order, that is about £20 a week, or £87 a month, walking out of the door. Your numbers will be different. A plumber's job is worth more; a café's cappuccino less. The point is to do the sum with your own figures.

## What should you look for in a social media inbox?

Use this as a checklist before paying for anything.

1. **The platforms you actually use.** Messages and comments, on each one. Check TikTok carefully: many tools cover TikTok comments, but TikTok DMs are a different matter (see below).
2. **Comments as well as DMs.** A lot of enquiries from local customers are public comments. An inbox that only shows DMs misses half the work.
3. **Team access without sharing passwords.** Everyone should have their own login, with the owner able to remove someone in one click. Nobody should need the Facebook password.
4. **Who answered what.** An activity log, or at least a clear mark on each conversation, so two people do not answer the same message.
5. **Replies you can trust.** If it drafts replies with AI, where does the information come from? It should only use answers you have approved, and it should leave anything it is unsure about for a person.
6. **Spam and abuse handling.** Hide abusive comments quickly, flag scams, and keep junk out of the main queue.
7. **Urgent things first.** A complaint or "my boiler is leaking" should not sit behind ten "😍" comments.
8. **Pricing that fits.** Per-user pricing punishes small teams who want everyone to help. Check what three people would cost, not one.
9. **Where your data lives.** For a UK business, check where messages are stored, for how long, and whether there is a GDPR data processing agreement. Your customers' messages are personal data.
10. **A way out.** Monthly billing, cancel any time, or at least a money-back period.

## Can one inbox handle TikTok direct messages?

Mostly not, for UK businesses, and it is worth knowing why before you choose a tool.

TikTok comments are available to third-party tools through TikTok's developer platform, so many inboxes show them. TikTok direct messages go through TikTok's Business Messaging API, which several tools that use it say is not available to business accounts registered in the UK, the European Economic Area or Switzerland. [Trengo](https://trengo.com/products/tiktok) and [respond.io](https://respond.io/integrations/tiktok-business-messaging) both say this is a TikTok rule that applies to every software provider. I could not find TikTok's own public page stating it, so treat it as the position reported by those tools, and check again if TikTok changes it.

In practice: for a UK business, TikTok DMs are still answered in the TikTok app. If TikTok is where most of your enquiries come from, factor that in.

## Should a social inbox reply automatically?

Some automation is useful. An instant reply that says "Thanks, we will get back to you within a couple of hours, or ring us on the number on our page if it's urgent" is better than silence. An away message on Sunday is sensible.

Where it goes wrong is when software answers a question it does not really know the answer to. A bot that tells a customer your boiler service is £70 when it is £85, or says you are open on Boxing Day when you are not, causes more trouble than a slow reply. Meta's own [messaging policy](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy) also recommends telling people when they are talking to an automated service, and requires it where the law does.

A sensible rule: automate the acknowledgement and the questions with one fixed answer (opening hours, address, parking), and keep a person in charge of anything involving price, availability or a complaint.
