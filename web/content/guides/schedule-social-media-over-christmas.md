To keep posting over Christmas without working Christmas, write every post for 21 December to 4 January in one sitting before you break up, then schedule them with the tools each platform gives you for free: **Schedule this post** in Google Business Profile, the planner in Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram, and the clock icon on a LinkedIn Page. Or use a tool that does all of them at once. Then set an away message for incoming messages and stop looking at your phone.

## Why bother posting over Christmas at all?

Because your customers are still searching. People check opening hours on Christmas Eve, look for an emergency plumber on Boxing Day, book January hair appointments between Christmas and New Year, and plan their "new year, new kitchen" projects on the sofa. A profile that went quiet on 18 December looks like a business that might not be there in January.

You do not need to post much. You need the right few posts to go out at the right time, without you lifting a finger on the day.

## What should you schedule for Christmas 2026?

Christmas Day 2026 is a Friday, so Boxing Day falls on a Saturday and the bank holiday moves to **Monday 28 December**. New Year's Day is Friday 1 January 2027, and Scotland has an extra substitute holiday on Monday 4 January ([gov.uk bank holidays](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays)).

A light plan for a local business:

| Date to publish | Post | Platforms |
|---|---|---|
| Mon 21 Dec | Christmas and New Year opening hours | Google, Facebook, Instagram |
| Wed 23 Dec | Last-minute availability, or "last chance before the break" | Google, Facebook |
| Thu 24 Dec | A short thank you to customers this year | Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn |
| Sun 27 Dec | Something light: a team photo, your favourite job of the year | Facebook, Instagram |
| Tue 29 Dec | January booking reminder | Google, Facebook, LinkedIn |
| Thu 31 Dec | New Year's Eve hours, happy new year | Google, Facebook |
| Mon 4 Jan | "We are back", with what is booking now | Google, Facebook, LinkedIn |

Change the hours to suit you. For the hours themselves, also set **special hours** in your Google profile, not just a post. Our guide to [Christmas hours on Google Business Profile](/guides/google-business-profile-christmas-hours) has the steps.

For ideas to fill the slots, see [Christmas social media post ideas](/guides/christmas-social-media-post-ideas), and for ready-made Google wording, the [Google Business Profile post templates](/guides/google-business-profile-post-templates).

## How do you schedule posts on Google Business Profile?

Google added scheduling to Business Profile posts, and it is free. Following [Google's instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB):

1. Go to your Business Profile (search **my business** on Google while signed in) and select **Posts**, then **Add post**.
2. Choose an update, offer or event and write the post.
3. Add a photo and a button, such as Call now or Book.
4. Turn on **Schedule this post** and choose the date and time.
5. Select **Post**.

The **Repeats** drop-down sends a post out weekly, monthly or on a custom schedule. That suits a genuine weekly fixture, not a Christmas plan, where every post should say something different.

Keep phone numbers out of the post text. Google may remove posts with unverified contact details, as explained in [why Google posts get rejected](/guides/google-business-profile-post-rejected). A rejected post on 24 December is a nuisance you will not want to fix from the sofa.

## How do you schedule Facebook and Instagram posts?

Meta Business Suite is Meta's free tool for Facebook Pages and linked Instagram accounts. You can write one post, choose to publish it to Facebook, Instagram or both, and schedule it for later. Meta's help centre covers the steps for [scheduling Facebook Page posts](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1252240869631062) and for [saving, scheduling and rescheduling in Meta Business Suite](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2223502627919449?locale=en_GB).

In outline:

1. Open Meta Business Suite and select **Create post**.
2. Choose where it goes: your Facebook Page, your Instagram account or both.
3. Write the post and add photos or video.
4. Choose to schedule it rather than publish now, and pick the date and time.
5. Check it appears in the **Planner** or under **Content**, then **Scheduled**.

Two things worth knowing. First, Meta's own [troubleshooting page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/816464273497113?locale=en_GB) notes that a scheduled post may sometimes fail to publish at its scheduled time, so glance at the planner once over the holiday. Second, third-party guides disagree about how far ahead Business Suite lets you schedule. For a two-week Christmas plan it does not matter, but check in the planner itself before planning months ahead.

Whether Meta's free tools are enough for the rest of the year is a separate question, covered in [Meta Business Suite scheduler: is the free option enough?](/guides/meta-business-suite-vs-postd)

## How do you schedule LinkedIn Company Page posts?

If your business has a LinkedIn Company Page, LinkedIn's [help page on scheduling Page posts](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1419179) says:

1. Open your Page as a super admin or content admin.
2. Select **Page posts** in the left menu and start a post.
3. Select the clock icon at the bottom right to open **Schedule post**.
4. Choose a date and time, then select **Next**.
5. Finish the post and confirm the schedule.

LinkedIn's limits:

- Posts can be scheduled from **an hour from now up to three months ahead**.
- **Events, multiple photos, reshares, polls, jobs and service posts cannot be scheduled.** Stick to text with a single image.
- Only super admins and content admins can schedule.

LinkedIn goes very quiet between Christmas and New Year for most businesses. One thank-you post on Christmas Eve and one "back and booking" post in the first week of January is plenty.

### How long does doing it yourself take?

A worked example. Say writing a post and adapting it for each platform takes 15 minutes, and scheduling each version takes 3 minutes. Seven posts, each going to an average of two and a half platforms:

- Writing: 7 posts x 15 minutes = 1 hour 45 minutes
- Scheduling: about 18 scheduled versions x 3 minutes = 54 minutes
- **Total: about 2 hours 40 minutes**

At £40 an hour for your time, that is roughly £105 for the fortnight. Fine if you have a quiet afternoon in mid-December. Most trades and salons do not, which is why the Christmas posts so often never happen.

## What about the messages that come in?

Scheduling only covers what goes out. Over Christmas, people still send Facebook messages and Instagram DMs asking if you are open, whether you can fit them in, or for a quote. Leaving them unanswered for ten days feels rude, even when you are rightly on holiday.

Two simple options:

- **Set an away message** on Facebook and Instagram that says when you are back and what to do in an emergency. Our guide to [Christmas away messages for Facebook and Instagram](/guides/christmas-auto-reply-social-media) covers what to set and what to say.
- **Agree a five-minute daily check** with one person, or decide you will not check at all and say so in the away message.

## A pre-Christmas checklist

Do this in one sitting before you break up:

1. Decide your hours for every date from 24 December to 4 January.
2. Set them as **special hours** in Google Business Profile.
3. Update your website and Facebook Page hours to match.
4. Write the seven posts from the plan above.
5. Schedule them: Google in Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram in Meta Business Suite, LinkedIn on your Page.
6. Check each one shows as scheduled, with the right date and time.
7. Set an away message for Facebook and Instagram.
8. Put one reminder in your phone for 29 December to glance at the planner.
