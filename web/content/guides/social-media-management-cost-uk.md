In the UK, social media management costs roughly £150 to £1,200 a month from a freelancer, £400 to £2,000 a month from a small agency, and from £1,500 a month upwards from a larger agency, according to published 2026 price guides. Doing it yourself costs nothing in cash but, in our worked example, around 15 hours a month. Tools sit in between: free schedulers, general schedulers from a few pounds a month, and tools that write and publish posts from about £15 a month.

We make one of those tools (postd), so this guide says plainly where each option is the better choice, including the ones that are not us.

## How much does a social media freelancer cost in the UK?

Freelancers are usually the cheapest way to have a real person handle your social media. Prices vary a lot with experience and with how much they do: posting only, or also designing graphics, shooting video and answering comments.

| Source (2026) | Hourly | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| [Bee Viral's UK pricing guide](https://www.beeviral.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-social-media-management-cost-in-the-uk-2026-honest-guide), April 2026 | £15 to £50 | £150 to £800 on a retainer |
| [Whito's UK social media management costs](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-social-media-management-costs/), 2026: junior freelancer | £15 to £30 | £250 to £500 |
| Whito: experienced freelancer | £30 to £60 | £500 to £1,200 |
| Whito: virtual assistant | £10 to £20 | Not given |

Both sources are compiled from published rates and platforms rather than a formal survey, and Bee Viral is an agency selling its own service. Use them as a guide to what is normal, then get two or three quotes for the same scope.

What a freelancer usually covers, according to Bee Viral: scheduling and posting, basic strategy and basic reporting. Content creation is sometimes included and replying to comments rarely is.

## How much does a social media agency cost in the UK?

Agencies cost more because you get a team: someone to plan, someone to design, someone to report, often an account manager.

### What do you get at each price?

Whito breaks the market into tiers, which is a useful way to check whether a quote is fair for what is on offer:

| Package | Typical price | What is usually in it |
|---|---|---|
| Posting only | £250 to £500 a month | Scheduling on 1 to 2 platforms, template or stock graphics, a basic monthly report |
| Standard managed | £500 to £1,500 a month | Custom graphics, 2 to 3 platforms, a content calendar with approvals, basic comment replies, monthly report |
| Full service | £1,500 to £3,000 a month | Strategy, short video, 3 to 5 platforms, active community management, detailed reporting |
| Campaign-led | £3,000 plus | A dedicated team, professional production, paid social, monitoring |

Source: [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/uk-social-media-management-costs/). If a quote at the posting-only price promises full-service work, ask exactly what you will get each month.

## What does doing it yourself really cost?

Doing it yourself has no invoice, but it has a cost: your hours. In our step-by-step breakdown of [how long social media takes](/guides/how-long-does-social-media-take), a Mansfield plumbing firm posting three times a week on Google, Facebook and LinkedIn spends about 11.7 hours a month on posts and 3.6 hours on replies, around 15.3 hours in all.

| Your time is worth | Monthly cost of 15.3 hours |
|---|---|
| £30 an hour | about £459 |
| £40 an hour | about £612 |
| £50 an hour | about £765 |

Those are worked examples with stated assumptions (54 minutes per post across three platforms, 10 minutes a day on replies), not a survey. The hidden costs on top are harder to price:

- **The gaps.** Most owners stop posting in their busiest weeks, which is when they are most visible to new customers.
- **The 10pm sessions.** Time taken from evenings and weekends is not billed but it is not free.
- **Mistakes.** A phone number in a Google post can [get it removed](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB); a rushed post with a wrong price is a conversation you then have to have.

None of that makes doing it yourself wrong. If you enjoy it and do it well, it is the best value there is.

## Do quotes include VAT?

Check every quote. Most agencies are VAT registered and quote plus 20%. A sole trader whose taxable turnover is under the [£90,000 VAT registration threshold](https://www.gov.uk/how-vat-works/vat-thresholds) does not have to charge VAT. If you are VAT registered yourself, you can usually reclaim it, so it matters less; if you are not, a £500 agency quote is really £600.

Our own prices are plus VAT too: £15 a month on Local is £18 with VAT.

## Which option is right for your business?

| If this is you | The better choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| You enjoy it, have an hour a week and post regularly | Do it yourself with the free schedulers | Costs nothing but time you are happy to give |
| You can write but struggle to post at the right time | A scheduler such as Buffer | Cheap, and you batch the work once a week |
| You never know what to write and keep stopping | A tool that writes and publishes | Takes the writing off you for £15 to £59 a month |
| You need video, Instagram reels or TikTok | A freelancer or agency | Someone has to film and edit, and tools that write text do not |
| You want strategy, paid ads and reporting meetings | A regional agency | You are paying for a team and their judgement |
| You have several branches and a marketing budget | A mid-size agency, or a tool plus an in-house person | Scale and consistency across locations |

A common, sensible mix for a local trade is a tool for the regular posts, plus a freelancer for a few days a year to shoot good photos of real jobs.

## What to ask before you hire anyone

1. **How many posts a month, on which platforms?** Get a number, not "regular content".
2. **Who writes them, and where do the ideas come from?** Your jobs and reviews, or a generic content library?
3. **Do I approve posts before they go out?**
4. **Does the fee include replying to comments and messages?**
5. **Is ad spend included or extra?**
6. **What is the minimum contract and notice period?**
7. **Do I keep full ownership of my accounts?** You should be the admin; they should have access, not the other way round.
8. **Is the price plus VAT?**
