To add Christmas opening hours to your Google Business Profile, open your profile, select **Edit profile**, then **Hours**, and add each date under **Special hours**. Set them by early December, because Google says changes can take up to three days to appear. In 2026 the dates to cover are Christmas Eve (Thursday 24 December), Christmas Day (Friday 25 December), Boxing Day (Saturday 26 December), the substitute bank holiday on Monday 28 December, New Year's Eve and New Year's Day (Friday 1 January 2027). Scotland also has a substitute holiday on Monday 4 January 2027.

## Which UK bank holidays fall over Christmas 2026?

Christmas Day 2026 is a Friday, which pushes Boxing Day on to a Saturday. When a bank holiday lands on a weekend, a substitute weekday is given instead. These are the dates from [gov.uk's bank holiday list](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays):

| Date | Day | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 December 2026 | Thursday | Christmas Eve (not a bank holiday) | Everywhere |
| 25 December 2026 | Friday | Christmas Day, bank holiday | England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland |
| 26 December 2026 | Saturday | Boxing Day (the holiday itself moves to the Monday) | Everywhere |
| 28 December 2026 | Monday | Boxing Day substitute bank holiday | England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland |
| 31 December 2026 | Thursday | New Year's Eve (not a bank holiday) | Everywhere |
| 1 January 2027 | Friday | New Year's Day, bank holiday | England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland |
| 2 January 2027 | Saturday | 2nd January (the holiday moves to the Monday) | Scotland |
| 4 January 2027 | Monday | 2nd January substitute bank holiday | Scotland only |

The one that catches people out is **Monday 28 December**. A lot of customers will assume you are shut that day, and a lot of businesses will forget to tell Google either way. If you are open on the 28th, say so. If you are shut, say that too.

Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve are not bank holidays, but many businesses close early on both, and customers search for them more than almost any other day. Treat them as dates that need special hours.

## What are special hours on Google Business Profile?

Special hours are one-off opening times for particular dates. They sit on top of your normal weekly hours without changing them. According to [Google's help page on special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en-GB), they are meant for holidays, events and short temporary changes, and once the date passes, your regular hours show again on their own.

That matters at Christmas because the alternative, changing your normal hours, means remembering to change them back in January. Special hours remove that job.

Two rules from Google before you start:

- **Special hours only show if your opening hours are set to "Open with main hours".** If you chose "Open with no main hours", fix that first ([Google's guide to editing hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300403?hl=en-GB) explains how).
- **A single set of special hours cannot run past 24 hours.** If you are open overnight, for example a takeaway open until 2am on New Year's Day, split it across two dates.

## How do you add Christmas hours from Google Search?

The quickest route is Google Search on a computer or phone, signed in to the Google account that manages your profile. These steps follow [Google's instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en-GB):

1. Search Google for **my business**, or for your business name and town. Google's [help on finding your profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en-GB) suggests both. Your profile appears with the management tools at the top.
2. Select **Edit profile**, then **Hours**.
3. In the **Opening hours** section, next to **Special hours**, select **Edit** (the pencil icon).
4. Choose the date:
   - To use a date Google suggests, such as Christmas Day, select **Review**.
   - To add a date Google has not suggested, select **Add a date** and enter it.
   - To change a date you have already set, select it.
5. Set the hours for that date:
   - If you are open, untick **Closed** and enter your opening and closing times.
   - If you are closed all day, leave **Closed** ticked.
   - If you open twice in one day, for example a café doing 8am to 12pm and then a private party in the evening, select **More**, then **Add hours +**.
6. Select **Save**.

Repeat steps 4 and 5 for each date. If Monday 28 December or Monday 4 January does not appear as a suggestion, add it yourself with **Add a date**.

To delete a special date you no longer need, select the bin icon next to a closed date, or **More** then **Remove day** for a date you set as open.

## How do you add Christmas hours in the Google Maps app?

On a phone, the Google Maps app works too. Google's [help on finding your profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en-GB) says to open Google Maps and tap **Business** at the bottom right. If you manage more than one business, tap the down arrow at the top and choose the right one.

From there, the editing steps are the same as above: **Edit profile**, then **Hours**, then **Special hours**. Google changes the app's layout from time to time, so if a label looks slightly different on your phone, the Search route on a computer is the most dependable way to do it.

## Can you set special hours for lots of locations at once?

Yes. Google's special hours help page includes a section on setting special hours through a spreadsheet, for businesses that manage many locations in the Business Profile Manager. If you run two or three shops, it is usually just as quick to do each profile by hand. If you run twenty, the spreadsheet is worth it.

## What about being closed for the whole of Christmas?

Plenty of trades take the full fortnight off. Google's guidance is specific here: if you are closed for **seven or more days in a row**, or for a period you do not know the end of, mark the business as **temporarily closed** instead of adding special hours for every day.

The catch is remembering to switch it back. Put a reminder in your phone for the day before you reopen. A profile still showing "Temporarily closed" in the second week of January quietly costs you calls.

If you are closed for six days or fewer, for example 24 December to 29 December, special hours for each day are the right tool.

## Should you confirm hours even if you are open as normal?

Yes. Google says to confirm your hours for official holidays even when they match your regular hours, so customers know the information is accurate. Leaving a bank holiday blank leaves customers guessing whether your normal hours really apply.

A cleaner in Leicester who works through the 28th, or a corner café in Derby open as usual on Christmas Eve, gains from saying so. "Open as normal" is useful information when most of the competition is shut.

### What if you only take emergency calls?

Opening hours describe when you are open to customers. If you shut the van up on Christmas Day but answer the phone for genuine emergencies, there is no clean way to show that in special hours. Two honest options:

- Mark the day **Closed**, and say in a Google post that you are taking emergency calls only, with the Call now button attached.
- Set short hours, such as 9am to 12pm, if you will genuinely answer and attend during that window.

Do not set 24 hours if you will not pick up at 3am. A customer with a burst pipe who rings an "open" business and gets voicemail will remember it, and might leave a review saying so.

## Should you also post your Christmas hours?

Yes. Special hours fix what Google shows in the hours section, but plenty of people never tap through to the full hours. A short update post puts the dates in front of them, and you can schedule it in advance with **Schedule this post**, as covered in our [complete guide to Google Business Profile posts](/guides/google-business-profile-posts).

Google also notes that when you update your hours, your profile may show a message about the change, and on mobile an update post about it can appear in the **Updates** tab. You can edit that post to add detail, or delete it if you would rather write your own.

An example to adapt, with no phone number in the text (Google may remove posts with unverified contact details, according to its [posts content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB)):

> Our Christmas and New Year hours: open until 1pm on Christmas Eve, closed 25 to 28 December, back as normal on Tuesday 29 December. Open until 1pm on New Year's Eve and closed on New Year's Day. Book your January boiler service now and skip the queue when the cold snap comes. Tap Call now.

Post it in the first week of December and again around 20 December, when people start checking. If you are looking for more festive ideas for the profile, [what to post on Google Business Profile](/guides/what-to-post-on-google-business-profile) has a seasonal section.

## Common mistakes with Christmas hours

1. **Forgetting Monday 28 December.** It is the substitute bank holiday in 2026, and the date most likely to be wrong.
2. **Changing normal hours instead of adding special hours,** then forgetting to change them back in January.
3. **Leaving it until Christmas Eve.** Changes can take up to three days to appear, according to [Google's help centre](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en-GB).
4. **Hours on your website, Facebook Page and Google that disagree.** Customers notice. Change all three on the same day.
5. **Marking the business temporarily closed and not reopening it.** Set a phone reminder.
6. **Forgetting Scotland's extra day.** Scottish businesses have a substitute holiday on Monday 4 January 2027 for the 2nd January holiday.
