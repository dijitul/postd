Google rejects a Business Profile post when it thinks the post breaks one of its content policies, and the post then shows as **Not approved** and does not appear on Search or Maps. The most common cause for UK small businesses is a phone number, email address or social media handle in the text. Other causes are restricted products such as alcohol, misleading claims, repeated posts, bad links, offensive wording and, for hotels, any offer at all. Select the question mark next to the status to see which policy Google applied, fix that one thing, and publish again or appeal.

## What does "Not approved" mean on a Google post?

Every post you publish has a status. [Google's help page on posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB) lists three:

| Status | What Google says it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Live | The post is public and shows on Search and Maps | Nothing |
| Pending | It is uploading, processing, or your profile is unverified | Wait a few hours. If it stays pending, check your profile is verified |
| Not approved | The post breaks a content policy and does not show on Search or Maps | Check the reason, fix it, republish or appeal |

The useful detail is in the same help page: "The question mark next to the status indicates which content policy the post violates." Start there. It saves you guessing.

## What are the most common reasons a post is rejected?

These are the rules in Google's [Business Profile posts content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB), its [guidance on posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7662907?hl=en-GB) and the wider [prohibited and restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) that posts must also follow.

| Cause | What Google says | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Phone number, email address or social handle in the text | Google "may remove posts with unverified contact information" | Remove it and add a Call now button |
| Promoting alcohol, gambling, tobacco, guns, health and medical devices, pharmaceuticals, financial services or adult services | Calls to action and promotional offers for restricted goods are not allowed | Post about your business, not the restricted product |
| Hotel offers | Hotels cannot create offer posts, or any post mentioning deals, promotions or discounts | Use update posts for news only |
| Links to unsafe sites | Links to malware, viruses, phishing or pornographic material are not allowed | Link only to your own website or a trusted booking page |
| The same post again and again | "Posting the same content multiple times" is listed as repetitive content | Change the wording, angle and photo each time |
| Misleading claims | False, inaccurate or deceptive information is prohibited | Only claim what you can prove |
| Made-up reviews or testimonials | Content not based on a real experience is "fake engagement" | Quote real reviews word for word |
| Offensive language or images | Profanity, harassment and hate speech are prohibited | Rewrite it calmly |
| Sexually suggestive content | Posts should be family-friendly | Choose a different photo |
| Gibberish or extra characters | Google warns against "Misspelled words, extra characters, text that adds no value" | Write normal sentences |
| Off-topic commentary | General, political or social commentary and personal rants are not allowed | Keep it about your business |

### Phone numbers and other contact details

This is the one that catches most trades. Writing "Ring Dave on 07..." at the end of a post feels natural, and Google may remove the post for it. Google's help page puts it plainly: posts with a phone number in the description "might get rejected if they cannot be verified as being connected to the business."

The fix is the **Call now** button, which uses the verified number on your profile. If you have a second number, such as a mobile for out-of-hours calls, Google suggests adding it to your profile through **Edit profile** rather than writing it into posts.

The same goes for email addresses and social handles. "Find us on Insta @..." can get a post removed. Link to your website with **Learn more** and put your social links there.

### Restricted products: pubs, bars, vape shops and financial services

Google's [restricted content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) covers alcohol, gambling, tobacco, guns, health and medical devices, regulated pharmaceuticals, adult services and financial services. Posts cannot include calls to action or promotional offers for those goods. Google's guidance on posts adds that you can post about your business, but "do not include content about the products."

In practice:

- A pub in Nottingham posting "2 for 1 cocktails all weekend" is likely to be rejected. "Quiz night every Thursday from 8pm, teams of up to six" is fine.
- A café that happens to have a bottle of wine in the background of a photo of Sunday lunch is generally fine. Google excludes incidental depictions, such as a menu.
- A mortgage broker or financial adviser should be careful with anything that reads as a sales offer for a financial product. Explainers and team posts are safer.

### Offers from hotels

Google's [posts policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB) says hotels cannot create offer posts or any post that mentions or links to deals, promotions, special offers or discounts. The stated reason is to avoid confusion with prices shown elsewhere on the hotel's listing. A B&B in the Peak District can post about a new breakfast menu, not a winter rate.

### Repeated posts

Google's content rules list "posting the same content multiple times" as repetitive content. If you reuse a post word for word, or use **Repeats** for something that is not genuinely recurring, you risk rejection and customers stop reading it anyway. Change the job, the area, the photo and the opening line. ### Misleading or unprovable claims

Google prohibits false, inaccurate or deceptive information. "Mansfield's number one plumber" is hard to prove. "Gas Safe registered, with 120 Google reviews" is a fact you can check. Prices, guarantees and qualifications should all be true on the day you post.

### Reviews you did not get

Quoting a real review word for word is fine and works well. Inventing one, or editing one until it says something the customer did not, is not. Google treats content not based on a real experience as fake engagement, and since 6 April 2025 the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act has banned fake consumer reviews in the UK ([CMS summary of the CMA guidance](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews)).

### Photos and videos

Images in posts must follow the same prohibited and restricted content rules, plus Google's [photo and video guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6103862?hl=en-GB). An image of a product Google restricts can trigger a rejection even when the text is fine. So can a photo that is blurry, heavily filtered or not of your business.

## How do you fix a rejected post?

1. **Find the reason.** Open the post in your profile and select the question mark next to **Not approved**.
2. **Fix that one thing.** Remove the phone number, swap the photo, rewrite the claim.
3. **Check the rest against the table above.** Posts sometimes break two rules at once.
4. **Edit or recreate the post.** Google lets you edit a post through its **More** menu, then **Edit**, or delete it and publish a fresh version.
5. **Wait for the status to change.** Give it a few hours before deciding it has failed again.

If the second version is also rejected and you cannot see why, strip it right back: two plain sentences, your own photo, one button. If that goes through, add details back one at a time to find the problem.

## How do you appeal a rejected post?

If you are sure your post follows the rules, you can appeal. Google's [appeals help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551?hl=en-GB) lists "Restricted media (post, photo, video)" as something you can appeal, and businesses in the UK and EEA use the **appeals tool** for it:

1. Open the appeals tool from Google's appeals help page and confirm you are signed in to the right Google account.
2. Select your profile, then **Continue**.
3. Select the decision you want to appeal, then **Continue**. The tool shows the date, the reason, a link to the policy and whether the decision can be appealed.
4. Optionally upload evidence and explain why the post follows the policy.
5. Tick the confirmation box and select **Submit appeal**.

Things to know before you do:

- **You can only appeal once** for the same issue, so make your case properly the first time.
- **Decisions can take up to five working days.**
- **The status shows in the appeals tool** as Submitted, Approved, Not approved, Can't be appealed or Eligible for appeal.
- **If your whole profile is suspended,** that has to be reinstated before you can appeal decisions about content.

Honestly, for most rejected posts it is quicker to fix and republish than to appeal. Appeals are worth it when the post was important (an event with a date, say) and you genuinely cannot see what was wrong.

## Why is my post stuck on Pending?

Google says a pending post is still uploading or processing, or your profile is unverified. A brand new profile that has not finished verification cannot publish live posts. Check your profile shows as verified. If it does and a post has been pending for more than a day, try deleting it and publishing again.

## A pre-flight checklist before you publish

Run through this before every post and most rejections never happen:

- No phone numbers, email addresses or social handles in the text
- No offers on alcohol, gambling, tobacco or other restricted products
- No deals at all if you are a hotel
- Links only to your own website or a trusted booking page
- Every claim is true and checkable
- Any review is real and quoted word for word
- Different wording and photo from your last few posts
- Your own photo, clear and family-friendly
- Normal sentences, no strings of emoji or capitals
