Automated social media posting means software publishes posts to your business accounts on a schedule, so you do not have to log in every time. For a UK small business it can take care of the regular posts on Google Business Profile, Facebook, LinkedIn and X, but replies, complaints and anything sensitive should stay with a person. Costs run from free (the platforms' own schedulers), to a few pounds or a few tens of pounds a month for tools, to hundreds of pounds a month for a freelancer or agency.

## What is the difference between scheduling, automation and AI writing?

These three terms get used as if they mean the same thing. They do not, and the difference decides how much of your time a tool actually saves.

| Term | What it does | What you still do |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling | Publishes a post you have written at a time you choose | Think of the idea, write it, pick the photo, load it in |
| Automation | Publishes posts without you pressing the button each time, from a queue, a repeat rule or a feed | Keep the queue topped up |
| AI writing | Drafts post text from a prompt or from information about your business | Give it something to work from, check it, paste it in or approve it |
| Writing and publishing combined | Works out what to post, writes it and publishes it on a schedule | Check the posts, or let them go out automatically |

A free scheduler saves you the fiddly part: being at your phone at the right moment. It does not save the part most owners actually struggle with, which is deciding what to say every week. That is the gap AI writing is aimed at, and it is why "automation" in 2026 usually means a mix of the two.

## What can you safely automate?

You can safely automate posts that are regular, factual and based on things that are true about your business. For most local businesses that means the bulk of what goes on social media.

Good candidates:

- **Service spotlights.** One service at a time, what it involves, who it is for.
- **Review quotes.** Real reviews, quoted exactly, with the customer's first name.
- **Tips and FAQs.** The questions customers ask you on the phone, answered once in public.
- **Seasonal reminders.** Boiler services in autumn, frozen pipes in winter, Christmas booking deadlines.
- **Behind the scenes.** The team, the van, the workshop, the kitchen.
- **News from your website.** A new service page, a new blog post, a new area covered.

These work well automated because they do not depend on what happened this morning. A tip about bleeding radiators is as true on Tuesday as it was on Monday.

## What should you keep human?

Keep anything human where a wrong word costs you a customer or your reputation.

| Keep human | Why |
|---|---|
| Replies to Google reviews | Google's own guidance on [local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB) recommends responding to reviews. A canned reply to a detailed complaint reads worse than no reply |
| Complaints and disputes | These need judgement, facts about the job and sometimes a phone call |
| Direct messages and comments | Customers expect a person. A slow human reply beats a fast wrong one |
| Quotes and prices for specific jobs | A post that promises a price you cannot honour is a problem you then have to fix |
| Anything touching local tragedy or bad news | Pause scheduled posts when something serious happens in your area. A cheerful offer post on the wrong day looks careless |
| Posts about named customers | Get permission first, especially for photos of their home |

The rule of thumb: automate the posts you publish, keep the conversations you have.

## How does automated posting work on each platform?

Each platform has its own free scheduling tool and its own way of letting approved third-party apps publish on your behalf. The table sets out the key facts for the four text-friendly platforms most useful to UK local businesses, checked in September 2026.

| Platform | Free built-in scheduling | Length limit | Things to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Yes. "Schedule this post" and a "Repeats" option ([Google help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB)) | 1,500 characters | Google may remove posts with phone numbers, email addresses or social handles in the text ([policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB)). Posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set |
| Facebook Pages | Yes, through Meta Business Suite ([Meta help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1252240869631062)) | Long posts allowed | A Page is separate from your personal profile. Tools post to the Page |
| LinkedIn Company Pages | Yes. Page admins can schedule from one hour to three months ahead ([LinkedIn help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548192/scheduled-posts-for-linkedin-pages)) | 3,000 characters ([LinkedIn help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a528176)) | Events, polls, reshares and multi-photo posts cannot be scheduled natively |
| X | Options differ by account type, so check X's own help pages | 280 characters on a standard account; links count as 23 characters ([X developer docs](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters)) | Short, frequent and conversational suits X best |

When a third-party tool publishes for you, you connect your account through the platform's own sign-in screen and grant permission. You should never need to hand over your password. If a tool asks for your Facebook or Google password directly, do not use it.

## Why should each platform get its own version of a post?

The same message works differently on each platform, so copying one post everywhere wastes most of its value. Google is read by people ready to buy, Facebook by locals scrolling in the evening, LinkedIn by other businesses, and X in short bursts.

Here is one piece of news, a plumber adding landlord gas safety certificates, written four ways.

**Google Business Profile** (with a Call now button):

> Landlords: we now do annual gas safety certificates (CP12) across Mansfield and Ashfield. Boiler, hob and fire checked, certificate emailed the same day. Book before your current one runs out.

**Facebook Page:**

> Quick one for any landlords who follow us. We have started doing gas safety certificates, so if your tenant's certificate is due, we can sort it. We check every gas appliance in the property and email you the certificate the same day, no chasing. Drop us a message or book through the link on our page.

**LinkedIn Company Page:**

> We have added landlord gas safety certificates to our services for letting agents and portfolio landlords in Mansfield and Ashfield. Same-day certificates, one point of contact for multiple properties, and reminders before each renewal is due. If you manage lettings locally and want a reliable engineer on call, we would be glad to talk.

**X:**

> Now doing landlord gas safety certificates across Mansfield and Ashfield. Same-day certificate by email. Book via the link in our bio.

Notice what changes: the Google version has no phone number, because Google may remove it; the LinkedIn version speaks to letting agents rather than homeowners; the X version fits in 280 characters with room to spare.

## What does automated social media posting cost in the UK?

The honest answer is anything from nothing to several thousand pounds a month, depending on how much of the work you want done for you.

| Option | Typical cost | What you get | What you still do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Doing it yourself | Your time | Full control | Everything |
| The platforms' own schedulers | Free | Posts go out at the time you set | Write every post, load each platform separately |
| A general scheduler, such as Buffer | Buffer's paid plans start at $5 per channel per month, billed in US dollars; its free plan covers three channels and excludes Google Business Profile ([Buffer pricing](https://buffer.com/pricing), checked September 2026) | One place to queue posts for several platforms | Write every post, with optional AI help |
| A tool that writes and publishes | From about £19 a month | Posts written from your business information and published on a schedule | Approve or edit, or let it run |
| A freelancer | £150 to £800 a month, or £15 to £50 an hour | A person planning, writing and often designing | Brief them, answer questions, approve |
| A regional agency | £400 to £1,500 a month | A team, strategy, reporting, often paid ads | Meetings, approvals, a contract |
| A national agency | From £1,500 a month, often much more | As above, at a larger scale | As above |

Freelancer and agency figures are from [Bee Viral's UK pricing guide](https://www.beeviral.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-social-media-management-cost-in-the-uk-2026-honest-guide), published April 2026. They are an agency's own survey of the market, so treat them as a guide, not a quote.

### What doing it yourself really costs

Doing it yourself is only free if your time is. Suppose you spend 90 minutes a week on social media: thinking of ideas, writing, taking a photo, posting to three platforms. That is roughly six hours a month. If your time is worth £40 an hour to your business, the true cost is about £240 a month, and that is before counting the weeks you skip because you are busy, which are usually the weeks you most need the work.

The point is not that doing it yourself is wrong. Plenty of owners enjoy it and do it well. The point is to compare tools and freelancers against what your hours are really worth, not against zero.

## How do approval workflows keep you in control?

An approval workflow means posts are written and scheduled, but nothing goes out until you say yes. It is the middle ground between doing everything yourself and handing over the keys.

A good approval setup lets you:

- see each post before it is published, on your phone
- edit the wording, or change the scheduled time
- reject a post outright
- switch to fully automatic publishing when you are happy, and back again when you are not

The sensible way to start with any automated tool is with approval switched on. For the first few weeks, read everything. Look for facts that are out of date, services you no longer offer, prices that have changed and a tone that does not sound like you. Most problems come from the information the tool was given, so fix the source (usually your website) and the posts improve.

Once a month of posts has gone out without you needing to change much, autopilot is a reasonable choice. Keep glancing at what goes out.

## What are the risks of automated posting?

The main risks are generic content, wrong information and breaking a platform's rules. None of them is a reason to avoid automation; all of them are reasons to choose carefully.

### Generic posts

The biggest risk is posts that could belong to any business. "Looking for a reliable plumber? We offer quality service at great prices!" tells a customer nothing and makes you look like everyone else. Posts built from your own jobs, services and reviews avoid this, because they contain details no other business has.

### Wrong or out-of-date facts

A tool can only be as accurate as what it reads. If your website still lists a service you stopped offering in 2023, an automated post may promote it. Tidy up your website before you automate, and keep approval on until you trust the output.

### Platform rules

Automation does not change the rules; it just means breaking them faster. On Google Business Profile, the most common mistake is putting a phone number, email address or social handle in the text, which Google says may get the post removed. Google's [content rules](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) also prohibit repetitive content, misleading claims and content not based on a real experience.

### Fake or edited reviews

Since 6 April 2025, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act has banned fake consumer reviews in the UK ([CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews)). If a tool quotes reviews in your posts, they must be real reviews, quoted accurately.

### Account safety

Connect accounts only through the platform's own sign-in and permission screens. Give access to a Facebook Page or LinkedIn Company Page, not your personal login. Remove access from tools you stop using.

### Hashtag clutter

Automated posts often come stuffed with hashtags nobody searches for. On a local business post, two or three relevant hashtags at most is plenty, and on Google Business Profile they add nothing a customer needs.

## A checklist for choosing an automated posting tool

Ask these questions before you pay for anything:

1. **Which platforms does it publish to?** Check that Google Business Profile is included if local customers matter to you, and whether it is on every plan or only the dearer ones.
2. **Who writes the posts?** You, you with AI help, or the tool on its own?
3. **Where does the content come from?** A blank prompt, a library of generic posts, or your own website and reviews?
4. **Does it write a separate version for each platform,** or copy the same text everywhere?
5. **Can you approve posts first, and switch approval off later?**
6. **Does it write in UK English?** "Colour", "organise" and £ signs matter to UK customers.
7. **When does it post?** At sensible times in your time zone, or at 3am because the servers are in California?
8. **How is it priced?** Per channel, per user, per post or flat? Is there a free trial, and does it need a card?
9. **How do you connect accounts?** Through official sign-in screens, never by sharing passwords.
10. **How easy is it to leave?** Monthly billing, no long contract, and disconnecting your accounts in one click.
