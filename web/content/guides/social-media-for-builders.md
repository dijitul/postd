Builders and roofers win local jobs from Google searches and word of mouth, so the useful social media is a busy Google Business Profile and a Facebook Page, not dance videos. Post once or twice a week: progress photos, finished jobs with the town named, reviews, your memberships (TrustMark, FMB, NFRC or others) and plain answers about planning and building regulations. Roofers have the weather to post about too. Below is a 12-month UK calendar, trust posts, examples to copy and a worked example of the time involved.

## Where do people look for a builder or roofer?

There are two kinds of customer. Someone with a leaking roof after a storm searches "roofer near me" and rings one of the first three listings that look trustworthy. Someone planning an extension asks friends, searches "builders Grantham", and then spends weeks checking you out: reviews, photos of past work, memberships, how long you have been around.

Both end up on your Google Business Profile. The planner also looks at your Facebook Page and website.

| Platform | What it does for a builder | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | Search and Maps, reviews, photos of your work | Yes, always |
| Facebook Page | Local recommendations, progress photos, checking you out | Yes |
| Instagram | Before and after photos, good for kitchens and extensions | Optional |
| LinkedIn | Developers, architects, commercial clients | Useful if you do commercial or subcontract work |
| TikTok | Some builders do brilliantly, but it is a separate hobby | Only if you enjoy it |

## What should builders and roofers post?

- **Progress updates** on a current job, week by week.
- **Finished jobs** with the town and a few numbers ("single-storey rear extension, 4 by 5 metres, 11 weeks").
- **Before and after** photos, with permission.
- **Reviews**, quoted word for word.
- **Trust posts**: memberships, insurance, guarantees, how you handle deposits.
- **Plain-English explainers**: planning permission, building control, party walls.
- **Seasonal and weather posts**, especially for roofers.
- **Availability**: "booking spring starts now".

## Trust posts that win building work

Big jobs mean big deposits, and customers have heard the cowboy builder stories. These posts answer that worry directly.

### TrustMark

[TrustMark](https://www.trustmark.org.uk/) is the government-endorsed quality scheme for work in and around the home. Registered firms are vetted on trading practices, customer service and technical competence, and customers can search for them by postcode. If you are registered, explain what it means:

> We are TrustMark registered, the government-endorsed scheme for work in and around the home. That means we have been checked on how we trade, how we treat customers and the quality of our work, and you can look us up on the TrustMark website before you sign anything.

### Federation of Master Builders

FMB members are vetted and independently inspected before they can join, and customers can confirm membership with the [FMB's Find a Builder and member check](https://www.fmb.org.uk/find-a-builder.html). Mention it alongside your guarantee or warranty if you offer one through the scheme. Only describe the warranty terms you actually provide.

### Roofers: NFRC

The [National Federation of Roofing Contractors](https://www.nfrc.co.uk/) vets members with an office and site audit before they join. If you are a member, or registered with a competent person scheme for roofing, say so and link the official search.

### Planning and building regulations

Customers are often confused about the difference. A plain explainer is a trust post because it shows you know the rules:

> Planning permission and building regulations are two different things. Planning is about whether you can build it (size, look, impact on neighbours), and many single-storey rear extensions fall under permitted development. Building regulations are about how it is built (foundations, insulation, drainage, fire safety), and they apply to almost every extension. We handle building control on every job. Check the Planning Portal for your own project.

Link the [Planning Portal's extension guidance](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/extensions) on Facebook, and always tell customers to check their own case, because permitted development depends on the house and the area.

### Deposits and payments

How you take deposits and stage payments is a trust signal. "We never ask for more than X% upfront, and you pay the rest at agreed stages" is reassuring, if it is true.

## Posting about storms without scaring people

The Met Office names storms each season. The [2026 to 2027 list](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/about-us/news-and-media/media-centre/weather-and-climate-news/2026/from-family-tributes-to-shaun-the-sheep-official-storm-names-for-202627-revealed) was published in September 2026. Named storms bring searches for roofers, and a calm, useful post does more good than "is YOUR roof safe?".

> After last night's wind, a few people in Newark have messaged about loose tiles and lifted flashing. If you can see damage from the ground, take photos for your insurer before anything is moved. Do not go up a ladder in the wet or wind. We are prioritising homes with water coming in, then working through the rest in order. Tap Call now if you need us.

Rules for storm posts:

1. **Practical first**: what to check, what not to do, what to tell the insurer.
2. **No exaggeration** about danger or prices.
3. **Be honest about wait times.**
4. **Never pretend to be official** or imply you work for the council or an insurer.

## Example posts you can copy

**Progress update:**

> Week 4 on the extension in Bingham: walls up to plate height, steels in, roof timbers going on Monday. Weather has been kind. The customer has their kitchen back next month.

**Finished job:**

> Finished: single-storey rear extension in Southwell. 4 by 5 metres, bi-fold doors, vaulted ceiling with two rooflights. Eleven weeks from groundworks to handover, and building control signed it off first time.

**Roofer, autumn:**

> September is the month to get your roof looked at before the storms. We check slipped tiles, the ridge, flashing round the chimney and gutters. Most checks take under an hour. Booking Mansfield and Sutton this month.

**Common question:**

> Do I need a party wall agreement? If you are building on or near a shared wall with a neighbour, you probably need to serve a party wall notice before you start. We explain it at the quote stage and can point you to a surveyor.

The government's [party wall guidance](https://www.gov.uk/party-walls-building-works) is the page to link if you post this on Facebook.

**Diary availability:**

> Our diary for spring 2027 is now open. We are booking extensions and loft conversions from April. If you are still waiting on plans, get in touch anyway and we can price from drawings.

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

Builders' reviews are often long and detailed. That is perfect material:

1. **Pick the best sentence**, quoted word for word. Do not trim it in a way that changes the meaning.
2. **First name and town** only.
3. **Pair it with a photo** of that job.
4. **Ask every customer at handover.** Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans incentives and bans asking only the happy ones.

> "They kept the site tidy every single day, which with two kids at home was everything." Thanks, Sarah in Kegworth. Sweeping up at five o'clock is not glamorous, but it is the part people remember.
