For a typical UK small business posting three times a week on three platforms, social media takes about two and three quarter hours a week for the posts, plus another hour or so for comments and messages. That is around 15 hours a month, or about £600 of your time if you value an hour at £40. A scheduler saves only the posting step (about an hour a month); a tool that also writes the posts saves most of the rest.

Those numbers are a worked example with every assumption shown below, not a survey. Swap in your own times and you will get your own answer in five minutes.

## What does doing social media by hand actually involve?

Every post, on every platform, goes through the same five steps. Most owners only count the last one, which is why social media always seems to take longer than it should.

1. **Coming up with the idea.** What is worth saying this week? A job you finished, a service people forget you offer, a seasonal reminder, a review.
2. **Writing it.** Getting the words right, checking the spelling, making it sound like you and not like a brochure.
3. **Adapting it for each platform.** Google Business Profile has a 1,500 character limit and Google [may remove posts with phone numbers in the text](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB). X allows 280 characters on a standard account. LinkedIn readers are other businesses. A copy and paste rarely fits all three.
4. **Finding a photo.** Taking one on the job, finding a decent one on your phone, cropping it, checking there is no customer's house number or child in the background.
5. **Posting it.** Logging in to each platform, uploading the photo, pasting the text, adding a button or link, pressing publish.

Then, separately, there is the bit that comes back: comments, messages and reviews that need a reply.

## How long does each step take?

Here are the assumptions for our example: a plumbing and heating firm in Mansfield, posting three times a week on Google Business Profile, Facebook and LinkedIn. These are reasonable times for someone who is competent but not a professional copywriter. Yours may be quicker or slower.

| Step | Time per post | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Idea | 10 minutes | Longer on a week with nothing obvious to say |
| Write the first version | 15 minutes | For Facebook, usually the longest version |
| Adapt for the other two platforms | 10 minutes | 5 minutes each: shorten for Google, reframe for LinkedIn |
| Photo | 10 minutes | Find, crop, check the background |
| Post to three platforms | 9 minutes | About 3 minutes each to log in, upload and publish |
| **Total per post** | **54 minutes** | For one idea published in three places |

And for the replies: 10 minutes a day, five days a week, checking and answering comments and messages across the three platforms.

## What does that add up to in a week and a month?

| | Per week | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Posts (3 a week at 54 minutes) | 162 minutes (2 hours 42 minutes) | about 11.7 hours |
| Replies (10 minutes, 5 days) | 50 minutes | about 3.6 hours |
| **Total** | **212 minutes (about 3.5 hours)** | **about 15.3 hours** |

Monthly figures use 52 weeks divided by 12 months, so 4.33 weeks a month. For the posts: 162 minutes × 4.33 = 702 minutes, about 11.7 hours. For the replies: 50 × 4.33 = 217 minutes, about 3.6 hours.

If that looks high, it is because nobody does it in one sitting. Ten minutes here and fifteen there, in the van, at the kitchen table, at 10pm. It adds up without anyone noticing.

### What if you only post once a week?

At one post a week on the same three platforms, the posting work drops to 54 minutes a week, or about 3.9 hours a month. Replies stay roughly the same, because customers message you whether or not you posted.

## What is that time worth in pounds?

Doing it yourself costs nothing on paper, but the hours are not free. They come out of quoting, invoicing, family time or sleep. Here is the monthly time from the example, priced at three realistic hourly values for a UK owner.

| Your time is worth | Posts (11.7 hours) | Replies (3.6 hours) | Total a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| £30 an hour | £351 | £108 | £459 |
| £40 an hour | £468 | £144 | £612 |
| £50 an hour | £585 | £180 | £765 |

How do you pick a number? A simple way is to ask what an hour of paid work brings in after materials. If you charge £60 an hour on the tools, £40 is a cautious figure. For a salon owner in Nottingham, it might be what a chair earns in an hour. The point is not precision; it is to stop treating the time as zero.

## How much time does a scheduling tool save?

A scheduler saves one step: posting. You still think of the idea, write it, adapt it and find the photo. What changes is that you load everything in one sitting and it goes out on its own.

In our example, posting three versions takes 9 minutes per post. Doing it in one tool, in a batch, might take 4 minutes. That saves 5 minutes a post, 15 minutes a week, about 65 minutes a month.

| | Hand posting | With a scheduler |
|---|---|---|
| Idea, writing, adapting, photo | 45 minutes a post | 45 minutes a post |
| Posting | 9 minutes a post | about 4 minutes a post |
| **Time saved a month** | | **about 1.1 hours (about £43 at £40 an hour)** |

The free schedulers built into [Google Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), [Meta Business Suite](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1252240869631062) and [LinkedIn Pages](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548192/scheduled-posts-for-linkedin-pages) save even less time, because you still load each platform separately. Their real benefit is that you can do the work on a quiet Sunday and not think about it during a busy Tuesday.

That is worth having. It is just not where most of the time goes. Some schedulers let you send the same text to every platform at once, which saves the 10 minutes of adapting, but then your Google post may be too long, your LinkedIn post may speak to homeowners rather than letting agents, and the posts look copied.

## How much time does a tool that writes and publishes save?

A writing-and-publishing tool takes on the idea, the writing, the adapting and the posting. If it can use photos from your website or Google profile, it takes most of the photo work too. What is left is reading what it wrote.

Assumptions: you spend 2 minutes reading and, now and then, editing each platform's version (6 minutes per post across three platforms), plus 10 minutes a week sending in a few new photos from jobs.

| | Hand posting | Writing-and-publishing tool |
|---|---|---|
| Per post | 54 minutes | 6 minutes to review |
| Photos | included above | 10 minutes a week |
| **Per week (3 posts)** | **162 minutes** | **28 minutes** |
| **Time saved a month** | | **about 9.7 hours (about £387 at £40 an hour)** |

The sum: 162 minus 28 is 134 minutes a week. 134 × 4.33 = 580 minutes, about 9.7 hours a month.

If you switch to automatic publishing once you trust the posts, the review time falls further, though we would always keep glancing at what goes out.

## What none of these tools saves

The 3.6 hours a month of replies in our example does not go away with a scheduler or a writing tool. Comments, messages and reviews need someone who knows the job, the customer and the price. A tool can help you draft, but a person should decide what gets said.

The honest summary of where the time goes:

| Job | Hand | Scheduler | Writes and publishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideas | You | You | Tool |
| Writing and adapting | You | You | Tool, you check |
| Photos | You | You | Your own photos reused, you add new ones |
| Posting | You | Tool | Tool |
| Replies | You | You | You |

## How to work out your own number

Spend one normal week timing yourself. It is quicker than it sounds:

1. Note the start and end time each time you open a social media app for the business.
2. Split it roughly into "making posts" and "replying".
3. Multiply each by 4.33 for a monthly figure.
4. Multiply by what an hour of your time is worth.

If the answer is under an hour a week and you enjoy it, keep doing it yourself. If it is three or four hours and you dread it, or you keep stopping and starting, that is the time worth getting back.

## Where to start

Time one week honestly. If most of it is posting, try the free schedulers first and do your posts in one batch. If most of it is staring at a blank screen, a scheduler will not help much and you need something that writes.
