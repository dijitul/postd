Yes, it is fine to use AI to write your business's social media posts. When we checked on 20 November 2026, none of Google, Meta, LinkedIn or X banned AI-written text, and none required text posts to be labelled as AI. What they do care about is accuracy, spam and realistic AI images, video and audio. You stay responsible for every word that goes out under your name, so read AI posts before they are published.

Below is what each platform actually says, linked to its own pages, and what the UK advertising rules add.

## What do the platforms say about AI-written posts?

| Platform | AI-written text | AI images, video and audio | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | Allowed if it is helpful and accurate. Google says it is critical to fact-check AI content before publishing | Google Merchant Center has its own rules for AI product images; Business Profile has no AI-specific rule we could find | [Google Search guidance on generative AI](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) |
| Meta (Facebook, Instagram) | No rule against it; labelling rules do not cover text | "AI info" labels when Meta detects AI signals or you disclose. Disclosure required for photorealistic video and realistic-sounding audio, with possible penalties | [Meta, February 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/) and [April 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/) |
| LinkedIn | Welcome when it reflects a real person's perspective. You are responsible; say so if you relied heavily on AI | Content Credentials icon on images and video that carry C2PA data | [LinkedIn: content created with the help of AI](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1481496), [Content Credentials](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6282984) |
| X | No rule against it. Automated posting must not be spam or duplicated | May label or remove synthetic or manipulated media that could deceive and cause harm | [X synthetic and manipulated media policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/manipulated-media), [X automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation) |

Rules change. If a decision depends on one of these, open the source and check the current wording.

## What does Google say about AI content?

Google's [guidance on using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) is written for websites, but it is the clearest statement of Google's thinking:

- AI content is not banned. It must meet the same standards as anything else, Google's Search Essentials and spam policies.
- It is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing."
- Using AI to churn out many pages without adding value may break Google's scaled content abuse policy.
- Google suggests adding information on how your content was created, where readers would find that useful.

For Google Business Profile posts, we could find no rule about AI at all. The [posts content policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7213077?hl=en-GB) applies to every post however it was written: you are responsible for complying with the law, and Google may remove posts with phone numbers in the text. If posts are being taken down, our guide to [why Google Business Profile posts get rejected](/guides/google-business-profile-post-rejected) covers the usual causes.

## What does Meta say about AI on Facebook and Instagram?

Meta's labelling rules are about pictures, video and sound, not words.

In [February 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/), Meta said it would require people to use its disclosure tool when they post organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and that it may apply penalties if they do not. In [April 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/) it said it would add "AI info" labels to video, audio and images when it detects industry-standard AI signals or when people disclose them, with the label moved into the post menu for content that was only edited with AI. Its help centre page, [how to identify AI content on Meta products](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/), explains how the labels appear today.

Neither announcement sets any rule for AI-written captions or post text.

What this means for a salon in Nottingham or a plumber in Mansfield:

- AI-written captions: no label needed.
- An AI-generated illustration (a cartoon boiler, a seasonal graphic): Meta may label it automatically. That is fine.
- A realistic AI video or voiceover: use Meta's AI label when you post it.

## What does LinkedIn say?

LinkedIn's help page on [content created with the help of AI](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1481496) is the most direct of the four. It says AI-assisted content is welcome when it reflects a real person's perspective, experience or expertise, that you are responsible for everything you post, and that you should check, edit and approve AI-assisted content before posting. It suggests telling people if you relied heavily on AI.

It also describes a "Seems like AI slop" feedback option members can use on posts. LinkedIn says a related "Seems like AI" tip in your post analytics does not mean a post was removed or that a policy decision was made. The message is clear enough: generic AI filler is unwelcome, AI-assisted posts with real substance are fine.

Separately, LinkedIn [says automated comments are prohibited](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/authentic-content-and-conversations) (March 2026). That is about commenting on other people's posts with bots, not about publishing your own Company Page posts through an approved tool.

For images and video, LinkedIn shows a [Content Credentials](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6282984) icon when the file carries C2PA data, which can show whether AI was used. LinkedIn says it cannot yet identify and label all AI content.

## What does X say?

X's [synthetic and manipulated media policy](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/manipulated-media) says you may not share synthetic, manipulated or out-of-context media that may deceive or confuse people and lead to harm, and that X may label such posts. It is aimed at media, not ordinary text. X's [automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation) say you are responsible for what apps do with your account and that automated posts must not be spam or duplicate or substantially similar posts.

We could not load X's help pages directly when we checked (they blocked our request), so this summary is based on the published policy text as indexed in November 2026. Check the pages yourself before relying on the detail.

## What do UK advertising rules say about AI?

Most business posts count as marketing, so the UK advertising code applies. The ASA and CAP have been clear that there are no AI-specific rules, and that [existing rules apply however content is made](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html) (CAP, May 2025).

Their advice on disclosure comes down to one question: would your audience be misled if you did not say AI was used? And it adds a warning that disclosure is very unlikely to fix a message that is misleading in the first place. Their example is an AI image exaggerating a cosmetic's results: labelling it as AI does not make it acceptable.

For a local business, the translation is simple:

| Using AI to | OK? |
|---|---|
| Draft a post about a service you really offer | Yes, check the facts |
| Tidy the wording of a post you wrote | Yes |
| Make an illustration for a seasonal post | Yes, Meta may label it |
| Show a "finished job" you did not do | No, it misleads, label or not |
| Write a customer testimonial | No. Fake reviews are illegal in the UK |
| Invent a price, offer or guarantee | No. Only post what you will honour |

On reviews in particular: quoting real reviews is fine and useful, writing them is not. Our guide to [turning Google reviews into social media posts](/guides/turn-google-reviews-into-social-media-posts) covers how to do it within the rules.

## What goes wrong with AI-written posts?

The platforms are not the main risk. The posts are.

- **Made-up facts.** General AI tools fill gaps confidently. Ask one for a post about your bakery and it may invent a sourdough you do not sell or an opening time you do not keep.
- **Generic wording.** "Looking for a reliable plumber? We pride ourselves on quality service!" could be any business in the country. Customers skim straight past it.
- **US spelling and tone.** "Color", "neighborhood" and "awesome deals" on a Leicester cleaner's page give the game away.
- **Hashtag soup.** Fifteen hashtags nobody searches for, some of them unrelated to your business.
- **Repetition.** The same post with slightly different words every week. X's rules prohibit substantially similar automated posts, and Google treats repetitive content as spam.
- **Wrong tone at the wrong time.** A cheerful pre-written offer going out on a day of bad local news.

Every one of these is fixable by giving AI your real information and reading what it writes.

## How to use AI for your posts safely

1. **Feed it your facts.** Your website, your services, your area, your real reviews. The more specific the input, the less it invents.
2. **Read every post before it goes out,** at least for the first month.
3. **Check names, prices, dates and claims.** If you would not say it to a customer's face, delete it.
4. **Write for each platform.** Short for X, practical for Google, a bit warmer for Facebook, business-to-business for LinkedIn.
5. **Use your own photos for your work.** Keep AI images for illustrations, and label realistic AI video or audio on Meta.
6. **Keep hashtags to a few that relate to your business.**
7. **Pause scheduled posts when something serious happens locally.**
8. **Keep replies to customers human,** or at least human-approved.
