To hide a comment on a Facebook business Page, tap the three dots next to the comment and choose **Hide comment**. The comment disappears for everyone except the person who wrote it and their friends, so they are not told and usually do not notice. To stop comments appearing in the first place, add blocked words in your Page settings, switch on the profanity filter, and use Instagram's **Hidden Words** and TikTok's **comment filters** for the same job on those apps. As a rule: hide spam, scams and abuse, delete anything dangerous or illegal, and reply to genuine complaints.

This guide covers each platform step by step, the filters worth setting once, and how to decide between hide, delete, ban and reply.

## Hide, delete, restrict or reply: which should you use?

| Action | What happens | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Hide | Comment disappears for everyone except the commenter (and on Facebook, their friends) | Spam, scam links, trolling, swearing |
| Delete | Comment is removed for everyone, and the commenter may notice | Threats, personal details (someone's address or phone number), illegal content |
| Ban, block or restrict | Stops that person commenting, or limits who sees their comments | Repeat offenders, fake accounts |
| Filter | Comments with chosen words are hidden automatically | The same nonsense turning up every week |
| Reply | You answer in public | Genuine complaints and questions |

The mistake I see most from local businesses is hiding fair criticism. If a Leicester cleaner's customer comments "Paid for a deep clean and the oven wasn't touched", hiding it rarely makes the problem go away: they will see it is hidden from their friends' replies, or post it somewhere else. A calm public reply ("Sorry to hear that, Sarah. I've sent you a message so we can sort it this week") looks better to everyone else reading.

## How do you hide a comment on a Facebook business Page?

From the post on your Page, following [Facebook's help page on hiding comments](https://www.facebook.com/help/297845860255949):

1. Find the comment.
2. Tap or hover on the three dots next to it.
3. Choose **Hide comment**.

To bring it back, go to the same menu and choose **Unhide**.

According to Facebook's help centre, a hidden comment is still visible to the person who wrote it and their friends. That is the point: it stops the comment spreading without starting a row.

### How do you delete a comment or ban someone?

The same three-dot menu has **Delete** and, for repeat problems, options to ban or restrict the person from your Page. Delete is permanent and the commenter can tell it has gone, so keep it for things that genuinely should not exist anywhere: threats, someone's personal details, illegal content.

## How do you stop certain words appearing in Facebook comments?

Set this once and it quietly does a lot of work.

1. Go to your Page and open **Settings**.
2. Find the section for **followers and public content** (Meta moves things around, so if you cannot see it, search settings for "hidden words" or "profanity").
3. Turn on hiding comments containing certain words, and add your list.

[Facebook's help page on blocking words](https://www.facebook.com/help/131671940241729) says you can add words, phrases and emoji in any language, that common misspellings and variations are hidden too, and that you can upload a list as a CSV file. Comments with those words are hidden from everyone except the commenter and their friends.

What to put on the list:

- Common swear words and slurs
- Words used by the scams that hit your comments (crypto, "investment", "DM me", "check my profile", a dodgy domain you keep seeing)
- Competitors' names if they keep touting for work under your posts (use with care)

Two more Facebook settings worth knowing:

- **Profanity filter.** A separate switch in the same part of settings that hides comments with commonly reported swearing. See [Facebook's profanity filter page](https://www.facebook.com/help/1017549069082358).
- **Moderation Assist.** Facebook's rule-based tool for Pages and professional mode can hide comments that match conditions you set, such as comments containing links or coming from very new accounts. It is described on Meta's [comment moderation page for creators](https://creators.facebook.com/comment-moderation-and-support).

Facebook's keyword and profanity filters are set at Page level, so Meta says they also apply to [comments on your ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1129470964230971).

## How do you hide, delete and filter comments on Instagram?

### Hidden Words: the automatic filter

Instagram's Hidden Words setting hides comments (and message requests) that contain offensive words, phrases or emoji, plus any custom words you add. It is explained in [Instagram's help page on hiding comments or message requests](https://help.instagram.com/700284123459336). To set it up:

1. Go to your profile and open **Settings**.
2. Find **Hidden Words** (under privacy or "how others can interact with you", depending on your app version).
3. Switch on hiding offensive comments, and add your own words and phrases.

Use the same list you made for Facebook.

### Deleting a comment

You can delete any comment on your own posts. Instagram's help centre explains [how to delete a comment](https://help.instagram.com/289098941190483): tap the comment, then the delete option (on iPhone, swipe left on it). Instagram has also said you can [manage up to 25 comments at once](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/continuing-our-work-to-fight-online-bullying) to delete them, or block or restrict the accounts, which helps when a post attracts a pile-on.

### Restrict and Limits

- **Restrict** an account and their new comments on your posts are only visible to them unless you approve them. It is the Instagram version of hiding, for one person.
- **Limits** temporarily hides comments and message requests from accounts that do not follow you or only followed you recently. It is useful if a post goes viral for the wrong reasons.

### Turning comments off

For a single post, you can [turn comments off](https://help.instagram.com/1766818986917552). Do this sparingly: comments are often where enquiries arrive.

## How do you hide and filter comments on TikTok?

TikTok does not have a simple "hide this one comment" button in the way Facebook does. Instead, its filters hold comments back until you approve them. [TikTok's help page on comments](https://support.tiktok.com/en/account-and-privacy/account-privacy-settings/comments) describes:

- **Filter all comments:** every comment is hidden until you approve it (TikTok notes this is not available to everyone yet).
- **Filter unwanted comments:** hides comments that look like spam or offensive content until you approve them.
- **Filter keywords:** add your own words, and comments containing them stay hidden unless you approve them.
- **Creator Care Mode:** hides inappropriate or offensive comments, and comments similar to ones you have previously reported, deleted or disliked.
- **Review filtered comments:** where you approve or delete what the filters caught.

To find them, go to your profile, open **Settings and privacy**, then **Privacy**, then **Comments**.

You can also delete comments, and TikTok's help page says you can select up to 100 at once and delete them together.

## Which words should a small business filter?

Start with a list of 20 to 30 and add to it as things turn up. A sensible starting list for a local business:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Scam bait | crypto, bitcoin, forex, investment, "DM me", "check my page", "earn from home" |
| Fake Meta warnings | "page violation", "account disabled", "verify your page" |
| Swearing and slurs | Your own list; the built-in filters cover the common ones |
| Spammy offers | "followers", "promote your page", "cheap likes" |
| Personal attacks | Any staff names you need to protect, if someone has been targeting a team member |

Be careful with words that legitimate customers use. A café in Derby filtering "free" would hide "Are you free for a party of 10 on Saturday?".

## How often should you check hidden comments?

Once a week is enough for most businesses. Filters are blunt and will occasionally catch a real customer. Facebook lets you see hidden comments on each post, Instagram has a **View hidden comments** option under a post, and TikTok collects them under **Review filtered comments**.

## How much time does comment moderation take?

A worked example, with stated assumptions, for a restaurant in Nottingham that posts on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok:

- 150 comments a week across the three apps, of which about 20 are spam or scams.
- Opening each app, finding new comments on recent and older posts and dealing with them: about 15 minutes a day per app, so 45 minutes a day, 7 days a week. That is 5.25 hours a week.
- With filters set up and everything in one inbox: about 15 minutes a day for all three, 1.75 hours a week.
- The owner's or manager's time is worth £30 an hour.

Saved: 3.5 hours a week x £30 = £105 a week, about £455 a month (£105 x 52 ÷ 12). The filters on their own are free; most of the remaining saving comes from not hunting through three apps.
