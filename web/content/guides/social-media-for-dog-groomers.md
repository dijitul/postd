Dog groomers fill the diary from three places: Google, when a new owner searches for a groomer nearby; Facebook, where local dog owners recommend each other; and Instagram, where your grooms look their best. Post two or three times a week about real grooms, your qualifications, seasonal coat care and gaps in the diary, and have a plan for the booking messages that follow. Below is a 12-month UK calendar, trust posts, examples to copy, and how to stop DMs eating your evenings.

## Where do dog owners look for a groomer?

A new puppy owner searches "dog groomer near me" or "dog grooming Worksop", compares the listings, and reads reviews looking for one thing: will my dog be safe and happy here? Existing customers mostly rebook by message.

| Platform | What it does for a groomer | Worth it? |
|---|---|---|
| Google Business Profile | New customers searching nearby, reviews, photos | Yes, always |
| Facebook Page | Local dog owner groups, recommendations, gaps in the diary | Yes |
| Instagram | Before and after photos, booking DMs | Yes, for most groomers |
| TikTok | Grooming videos can do well, but views are often from far away | Optional |
| LinkedIn | Not relevant for most groomers | No |

Our [complete guide to Google Business Profile posts](/guides/google-business-profile-posts) covers the post types and rules.

## What should a dog groomer post?

- **Before and after photos**, with the breed and what you did.
- **Gaps in the diary**, as soon as they appear.
- **Coat care tips** by breed and season.
- **Puppy introductions**: first visits, what to expect.
- **Trust posts**: qualifications, insurance, how you handle nervous dogs.
- **Reviews**, quoted word for word.
- **Your set-up**: the salon, the drying room, the van if you are mobile.

## Trust posts for dog groomers

Owners are handing over a family member. What they want to know is that you are trained, careful and kind.

### Qualifications

There is no compulsory licence or qualification for dog grooming in England, which is exactly why posts about your training matter. If you hold a City & Guilds [Diploma in Dog Grooming](https://www.cityandguilds.com/qualifications-and-apprenticeships/land-based-services/animal-management/7863-diploma-in-dog-grooming), have done an apprenticeship, or are a member of the British Dog Groomers' Association (part of the [Pet Industry Federation](https://www.petfederation.co.uk/)), say so and explain what it covers.

> A few people have asked about our training. Both of us hold the City & Guilds Diploma in Dog Grooming, which covers handling, health checks, breed styles and safe use of equipment. There is no legal requirement for groomers to be qualified in the UK, so it is always worth asking.

Only claim the qualifications you actually hold.

### Insurance and first aid

If you carry public liability and care, custody and control insurance, or have done a pet first aid course, say so. Owners rarely ask, but they notice.

### Nervous and older dogs

A post about how you handle anxious dogs is one of the most reassuring things you can publish: shorter sessions, no cage drying, breaks, the same groomer each time. Only describe what you actually do.

## Example posts you can copy

**Before and after (Google and Instagram):**

> Biscuit the cockapoo came in matted after a muddy autumn. We clipped him short to get the coat healthy again, rather than spending two hours brushing out tangles, which would have been miserable for him. Teddy bear face kept. Booking regular six-week visits now so we can grow him back out.

**Gap in the diary:**

> Cancellation tomorrow at 10am, suitable for a small or medium dog. Tap Book to grab it, or message us on Facebook.

**Seasonal tip:**

> Fireworks season is starting. If your dog is due a groom in early November, book a morning slot so they are home before dark. We keep the radio on and the dryers low for nervous dogs, and you can stay for the first visit if it helps.

**Coat care:**

> Please think twice before shaving your husky. A double coat may not grow back the same after a close shave, which is why most groomers advise against it. What helps in summer is a proper deshed to remove the loose undercoat. Booking deshed appointments in Retford through June.

**Christmas deadline:**

> Our last grooms before Christmas are on 23 December, and Saturdays are full. If your dog needs to look smart for the family, book this week. Gift vouchers available too.

## What about booking messages?

The posts are only half of it. Every good post brings back a pile of DMs and comments: "how much for a cockapoo?", "do you have anything next week?", "can you do nails only?". They arrive while you are mid-groom with wet hands.

What helps:

1. **A standard reply for prices**: by size or breed, with what is included.
2. **A booking link or a clear next step** in every reply.
3. **One inbox** instead of checking Instagram, Facebook Messenger and comments separately.
4. **An away message** for evenings and days off, so people know when you will reply.

## How do you turn reviews into posts?

Dog owners write warm, detailed reviews, often naming the dog. Use them:

1. **Quote word for word**, owner's first name and the dog's name.
2. **Add the after photo** of that dog, with permission.
3. **Say what you did**: the style, the problem you solved.
4. **Ask every customer**, not just the regulars. Google's [content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en-GB) bans offering discounts for reviews and asking only happy customers.

> "Molly usually shakes at the groomers but she trotted in here like she owned the place." Thanks, Jo. Molly is a rescue whippet who came to us nervous last spring. Short sessions and the same groomer every time made the difference.
