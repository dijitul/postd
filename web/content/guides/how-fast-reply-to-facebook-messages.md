Aim to reply to Facebook and Instagram messages within an hour during your opening hours, and never later than the same working day. If you want Meta's **Very Responsive** badge on your Facebook Page, Meta's help centre says you need a response rate of at least 90% and a response time under 15 minutes. Outside your hours, an away message that says when you will be back is enough. What matters most is that a customer is not left wondering whether you saw their message.

There is no reliable, independent study I can point you to that says "reply in X minutes and you win Y% more work", so this guide does not invent one. Instead it covers Meta's actual rules, a worked example you can run with your own numbers, and the practical ways a busy owner can reply faster.

## Why does reply speed matter for a local business?

Because people messaging a local business are usually asking more than one.

Someone in Leicester whose end-of-tenancy clean is due on Friday will message three cleaning companies on Facebook at once. Someone wanting a Saturday cut and colour in Nottingham will DM two or three salons. The first business to reply with a clear, useful answer usually gets the job, not because the customer did careful research, but because they want it sorted.

That means speed is not about good manners. It is about being in the conversation before the decision is made.

## What is a realistic reply time target?

It depends on what you sell and how urgent your customers' problems are. These are sensible targets for a small business, based on my experience running pages for local firms rather than any official standard.

| Business | Typical message | Sensible target during opening hours |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency trades (plumber, locksmith, electrician) | "Water coming through the ceiling, can anyone come today?" | Minutes. If you cannot, the instant reply should give your phone number |
| Other trades and builders | "Could you quote for a new bathroom?" | Within a few hours, same day |
| Salons, barbers, beauty | "Have you got anything Saturday morning?" | Within an hour |
| Cafés, restaurants, takeaways | "Can I book a table for 8 tonight?" | Within an hour, faster near service |
| Cleaners, dog groomers, tutors | "Do you cover Wigston? What do you charge?" | Within a few hours |
| Accountants, solicitors, other professional services | "Can you help with my Self Assessment?" | Same working day |

The pattern: the more urgent the problem and the more alternatives the customer has, the faster you need to be.

## What does Facebook's Very Responsive badge need?

Facebook can show a **Very Responsive to messages** badge on your Page. According to Meta's help page [about the Very Responsive badge](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/201893553741970), you need:

- a response rate of 90% or more, and
- a response time of under 15 minutes.

The same page says that instant replies and away messages do not count towards your response rate or response time, and that messages you move to Spam or Done, or that arrive while your Page is set to away, are not counted either.

Two honest notes:

1. **The bar has moved before.** When Facebook launched the badge in 2015 it asked for replies to 90% of messages with a median time under five minutes, as [TechCrunch reported at the time](https://techcrunch.com/2015/06/15/facebook-pages-can-now-show-how-quickly-they-respond-to-customers-messages/amp/). Check Meta's current page rather than trusting any blog, including this one.
2. **The badge is nice, not essential.** Under 15 minutes is hard for an owner who is on the tools all day. I would rather a Mansfield plumber replied well within the hour than rushed a poor answer in ten minutes to protect a badge. The customer cares about the reply, not the icon.

## Is there a deadline for replying to messages?

In the Facebook, Messenger and Instagram apps, you can reply to a message whenever you like.

Tools connected through Meta's platform work differently. Meta's [Messenger Platform and Instagram messaging policy](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy) gives businesses a standard messaging window that, for most user actions, lasts 24 hours from the customer's last message. There is a separate Human Agent tag that lets a person reply manually within 7 days, and conversations started from certain ads can stay open for up to 7 days.

What this means in practice: if you use a social inbox tool, answer within 24 hours wherever you can. After that, the tool may only be able to send a reply typed by a person, and after 7 days you may need to answer in the app itself. Another reason not to let messages sit.

## What does a slow reply cost? A worked example

This is an example with stated assumptions, not a statistic. Put your own numbers in.

**Assumptions** for a cleaning business in Leicester:

- 12 enquiries a week arrive through Facebook and Instagram messages and comments.
- When they reply within the hour, about 40% become a booking.
- When they reply the next day, about 20% become a booking, because many customers have already booked someone else.
- At the moment, half their enquiries get a next-day reply.
- The average first job is worth £120.

**Now:** 6 enquiries answered fast x 40% = 2.4 bookings. 6 answered next day x 20% = 1.2 bookings. Total 3.6 bookings a week.

**If all were answered within the hour:** 12 x 40% = 4.8 bookings a week.

**Difference:** 1.2 bookings a week x £120 = £144 a week, or about £624 a month (£144 x 52 ÷ 12). For a cleaner, many of those first jobs turn into regular clients, so the real figure is higher.

The conversion rates are my assumptions for the example. Look at your own messages from last month: which ones booked, and how quickly did you reply to each? That is your real answer.

## How can a small business reply faster?

You do not need to be glued to your phone. You need a few settings and a routine.

### 1. Set an instant reply

Meta Business Suite can send an [instant reply](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/395965998733706) to the first message someone sends you, on Facebook and Instagram. Use it to set expectations, not to pretend you have answered:

> Thanks for your message. We are out on jobs most of the day and reply to messages at lunchtime and after 5pm. If it's an emergency, please ring the number on our Page.

It will not help your badge (Meta does not count it), but it stops the customer wondering.

### 2. Set an away message for evenings and weekends

Away messages go out automatically when you are set to away, or on a schedule. Say when you will be back:

> We are closed until 8am Monday. We will reply first thing. For emergency call-outs this weekend, ring the number on our Page.

### 3. Write your common answers once

Most local businesses get the same ten questions: prices, areas covered, availability, opening hours, parking, how to book. Write a good answer to each and keep them as saved replies (Meta lists [saved replies](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/419420149025345) among its business messaging tools), so you paste and tweak rather than typing from scratch in a car park.

### 4. Check at fixed times

Instead of glancing at four apps whenever a notification pings, pick set times: before the first job, lunch, mid-afternoon, end of day. Four focused checks beat thirty distracted ones.

### 5. Put everything in one place

Checking Facebook, Messenger, Instagram and TikTok separately is the main reason messages get missed. Meta Business Suite puts Facebook and Instagram messages and comments together for free. A dedicated inbox adds TikTok comments and a team view. The options are set out in [one inbox for Facebook, Instagram and TikTok messages](/guides/social-media-inbox-small-business).

### 6. Share the load

If someone else in the business can answer messages (a partner, an office manager, a senior stylist), give them their own access rather than your password. A two-person rota halves the gaps.

### 7. Decide what needs you, and what does not

"What time do you open Saturday?" does not need the owner. "Can you price this extension?" does. Let whoever is on the rota answer the simple ones and flag the rest.

## How much time does replying take?

Faster is not the same as more time. A worked example for a salon in Nottingham:

- 25 messages and comments a week that need a reply.
- Typing a reply from scratch on a phone: about 3 minutes each, or 75 minutes a week.
- Pasting a saved answer and adjusting it: about 1 minute each, or 25 minutes a week.

That is 50 minutes a week saved, about 43 hours a year, from writing your answers down once. At £35 an hour that is about £1,500 of the owner's time a year (43 x £35), before you count any extra bookings from faster replies.
