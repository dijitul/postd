A small business needs a LinkedIn Company Page if it sells to other businesses, wins work through referrals and tenders, or is hiring. If your customers are local households (a café, a salon, a domestic plumber), LinkedIn is a low priority and your time is better spent on Google Business Profile and Facebook. If you do set one up, post once a week about real work, your people and what you know, and use your own personal profile alongside it. Below: who it suits, how to set it up properly, what to post with examples, and what it takes each week.

## Who actually needs a LinkedIn Company Page?

| Business | Worth it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Accountant, bookkeeper, solicitor | Yes | Clients are often business owners who check you out on LinkedIn |
| Commercial cleaner, facilities, security | Yes | Office managers and procurement teams use it |
| Builder or electrician doing commercial work | Yes, alongside Google | Main contractors and property managers look you up |
| Recruitment, training, IT support, consultancy | Yes | LinkedIn is where your buyers are |
| Anyone hiring | Yes, at least a basic Page | Candidates look for it |
| Domestic trades (plumber, roofer for homes) | Low priority | Homeowners search Google, not LinkedIn |
| Café, salon, groomer, takeaway | Usually no | Customers are on Google, Facebook and Instagram |

A domestic plumber in Mansfield does not need LinkedIn. An electrician in Nottingham who does fit-outs for shops and offices probably does, because the facilities manager who might hire them will look at it.

## How do you set up a LinkedIn Company Page?

You need a personal LinkedIn profile first. According to LinkedIn's [Page best practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/100826), you create the Page from your profile, you become its admin, and there is no separate password. Use your real name on your profile: LinkedIn may flag profiles that represent a business rather than a person.

1. From your profile, choose the option to create a Company Page.
2. **Name:** your trading name, exactly as it appears on Google and your website.
3. **Logo and cover image:** your real logo, and a cover photo of your team, van or work.
4. **About:** two or three plain sentences. What you do, for whom, where. Include your town and the areas you cover.
5. **Industry, size and location:** fill them in. They help people find you.
6. **Website:** your homepage, not a social profile.
7. **Second admin:** LinkedIn recommends adding a second super admin in case one person loses access or leaves. Do it on day one.
8. **Ask your team to list you as their employer.** It links their profiles to the Page and shows you are a real business with real people.

## What should a small business post on LinkedIn?

LinkedIn readers are at work. They want to know whether you are competent, reliable and easy to deal with. Six types of post do that well.

### 1. Finished jobs, in business terms

What the client needed, what you did, what changed. No hype.

Example for a commercial cleaner in Leicester:

> We have taken over the cleaning at a three-floor office in Leicester city centre this month. The brief: evenings only, no disruption, and a monthly deep clean of the kitchens and washrooms. Four of our team, five nights a week, with a supervisor checking every Friday. If your current contract renews in the new year, we would be happy to quote.

### 2. Know-how

Answer a question your business customers ask.

Example for an accountant in Derby:

> A question we get from new limited company clients every January: how much should I pay myself? There is no single answer, but most owner-managers we work with use a mix of a small salary and dividends, and the right split depends on your profits and other income. If you would like us to work through yours, our first meeting is free.

### 3. Your people

New starters, qualifications, long service. Business buyers like to know who will turn up.

> Congratulations to Tom, who has passed his 18th Edition wiring regulations exam this week. That makes all six of our electricians fully up to date. Tom joined us as an apprentice four years ago.

### 4. Credentials and compliance

Accreditations, insurance, health and safety, framework approvals. On LinkedIn, these are not boring, they are the deciding factor.

> We have renewed our SafeContractor accreditation for 2027. For facilities managers, that means our health and safety paperwork has been checked independently, so you do not have to start from scratch.

### 5. Reviews and testimonials

Quote a real Google review or a client's written feedback, word for word, with permission if you name the business.

### 6. Hiring

Job adverts, and what it is actually like to work for you.

> We are hiring a second van engineer, based in Mansfield. Full time, company van, no weekend call-outs. If you know a qualified engineer who is fed up of being rushed from job to job, please share this.

## Personal profile or Company Page?

Both, and they do different jobs.

- **Your personal profile** is where conversation happens. LinkedIn is built around people, and posts from the owner usually feel more natural than posts from a logo. Share the Company Page's posts there and add a line of your own.
- **The Company Page** is the steady, official record. It is what a buyer, a recruit or a main contractor checks when they look you up. It needs to look current: a post a week is enough.

## What should you avoid?

- **A Page with no posts.** An empty Page looks worse than none, because it suggests the business has stalled.
- **A personal profile pretending to be a business.** Use a Company Page for the business and your real name on your profile.
- **Copying Facebook posts word for word.** "Look at this lovely bathroom!!" reads oddly on LinkedIn. Say what the client needed and what you delivered.
- **Hashtag piles.** Two or three relevant tags at most.
- **Only posting job adverts.** Candidates also look at what else you post.

## Where to start

1. Decide honestly whether your buyers use LinkedIn. If not, focus on Google and Facebook.
2. If they do, set up the Page from your personal profile, add a second admin, and fill in every field.
3. Ask your team to list the business as their employer.
4. Post one finished job and one piece of know-how in your first fortnight.
5. Keep to one post a week, and share the good ones from your own profile.
