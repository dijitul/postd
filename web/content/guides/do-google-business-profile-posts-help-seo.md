Google Business Profile posts probably do not help your SEO much, if by SEO you mean ranking higher in the map results. Google's own explanation of local ranking does not mention posts, local search experts rank post frequency well down the list of factors, and the one public controlled test found no measurable effect. Posts do still earn their keep: they help the people who already see your profile decide to pick you, and the experts who rate them low for rankings rate them much higher for winning the click.

## What does Google say about local ranking?

Google publishes a short explanation of [how local results are ranked](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB). It names three factors:

- **Relevance:** how well your profile matches what someone is searching for.
- **Distance:** how far you are from the person searching.
- **Prominence:** how well known your business is, including links to your website and the number of reviews you have.

It then recommends a short list of things to do: verify your business, keep your information complete and accurate (address, hours, category, attributes), respond to reviews, and add photos and videos. Retailers can also add in-store products.

Posts are not on that list. Neither is any mention of activity or posting frequency. The page also says, plainly, "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

This does not prove posts have zero effect. Google keeps the details of its ranking systems vague on purpose. But it does tell you where Google itself points businesses, and posting is not one of those places.

## What do local search experts think?

The most widely used source on this is Whitespark's [Local Search Ranking Factors survey](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/). The 2026 edition, published on 6 November 2025, asked 47 local search specialists to rate hundreds of factors.

| Factor | Rank for local pack and Maps | Rank for local organic results |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency of Google posts or updates | 148th | 186th |
| Keywords in Google posts or updates | 160th | 177th |
| Quantity of Google posts or updates | 168th | 187th |

So the specialists put posts close to the bottom for rankings. The same survey tells a different story when it asks what persuades customers. In its list of conversion factors, post frequency comes 78th, quantity 91st and keywords in posts 95th, with much higher scores than in the ranking tables. And "keyword stuffing Google posts" appears among its negative factors and its suspension risk factors.

The caveat: this is expert opinion, not a measurement. It is still the most widely used survey in the field, and the specialists answering it work on local search every day.

## Has anyone tested it?

Yes, though not on a large scale. In July 2021, Joy Hawkins of Sterling Sky [published a test](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/do-google-posts-impact-ranking/) in which three inactive listings in small towns got one post a week for nine weeks, written around the keywords being tracked. 441 keywords were tracked for each listing.

The conclusion: adding posts "had no measurable impact on rankings in the local pack." One listing did not change, and the other two moved for reasons the author traced to other causes (a listing being filtered for sharing an address, and a jobs panel appearing and disappearing in the results).

The limits are obvious: three listings, nine weeks, five years ago. Someone commenting on the test argued nine weeks was too short. But it remains one of the few public tests that tried to isolate posts, and its result matches what the experts and Google's own page suggest.

## What about studies that say posting does help?

You will find studies and blog posts claiming that businesses who post rank higher. The most detailed recent one is from Localo, a Google Business Profile tool (and a competitor of ours, so weigh that as you like). Its [ranking factors study](https://localo.com/blog/top-local-seo-ranking-factors), first published in January 2025 and updated in 2026, looked at 16,098 profiles.

It found:

- Profiles that publish posts reached the top three positions 70.8% of the time, against 63.7% for those that did not.
- Profiles with 10 or more active posts reached the top three 73.0% of the time, against 66.0% for those with one to nine.
- Post length, posting pace, post type and putting category keywords in posts showed no meaningful relationship with rank.

Localo is clear that this is observational and shows correlation, not cause. The sample also leans towards strong performers, with 58.5% of profiles already in the top three.

That gap of seven points is real, but it has an obvious explanation. A business that posts regularly is usually also the one that answers reviews, uploads photos, keeps its hours right and has a decent website. Posting is a sign of an active business. It is not clear it is the cause of the ranking.

Notice too what the study did not find: no link between rankings and how often you post, how long posts are, or whether you put keywords in them. If posts were a strong ranking lever, you would expect those things to matter.

## Is there anything new in 2026?

One change worth knowing. In September 2026, Google began rolling out view counts for individual posts, after removing post-level views from its reporting in 2023, according to [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-posts-insights-views-41913.html) and other industry coverage. Each post in your profile dashboard now shows how many times it was viewed across Search and Maps. Google has not published exactly how a view is counted, and click counts for posts have not come back.

That does not change the ranking question, but it does give you a way to see which of your own posts get looked at. Over a few months, that tells you more about your customers than any industry survey.

## So what is the evidence, all together?

| Source | Type | Says posts help rankings? | Weight to give it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Google: how local results are ranked](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB) | Official guidance | Not mentioned | High, though deliberately general |
| [Whitespark 2026 survey](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/) | Expert opinion, 47 specialists | Low for rankings, higher for conversions | Medium |
| [Sterling Sky test, 2021](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/do-google-posts-impact-ranking/) | Small controlled test | No measurable effect | Low to medium |
| [Localo study, 2025 to 2026](https://localo.com/blog/top-local-seo-ranking-factors) | Correlation, 16,098 profiles | Posting profiles rank top three a bit more often | Low to medium, correlation only |

The honest summary: posts are at most a small factor for rankings, and nobody has shown they cause better rankings. If someone sells you a posting service on the promise of moving you up the map, ask for their evidence.

## Why post on Google at all, then?

Because rankings are only half the job. Getting into the map gets you seen. What you do with that moment decides whether the phone rings.

Picture someone in Leicester searching for a cleaner. Three businesses appear. All have four-and-a-half stars. One has a post from this week showing an end-of-tenancy clean in Clarendon Park, another from last week quoting a review, and a clear button. The other two last posted in the spring. The first one looks open, busy and careful. That is the job posts do, and it is why Whitespark's experts rate them higher for conversion than for ranking.

Posts also give you somewhere to put things customers need to know right now: holiday hours, a January offer, a new service, an event. Google archives posts older than six months unless they have a date range, according to [its help centre](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), so regular posting keeps that space filled.

## If you want better rankings, what should you do?

Spend the effort where Google points:

1. **Get the basics complete and accurate.** Category, services, hours (including [Christmas special hours](/guides/google-business-profile-christmas-hours)), service areas and description.
2. **Get more reviews, steadily.** Ask every customer. Never offer anything in return, which Google treats as fake engagement.
3. **Reply to every review.** Google names this directly.
4. **Add real photos and videos.** Also named directly.
5. **Have a website that matches your profile,** with a page for each main service and the areas you cover.

## Write for the reader, not for keywords

Since there is no good evidence keywords in posts help, and some evidence that stuffing them is a risk, write posts the way you would talk to a customer. Mention the job, the area and what you did, because a customer wants to know that. "Replaced a leaking cylinder in Forest Town" is naturally relevant without trying.
