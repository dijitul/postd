The best Christmas posts for a local business are useful first and festive second: your last booking dates, your opening hours, gift ideas you actually sell, and a thank you to the people who kept you going this year. Plan around a handful of fixed dates (Small Business Saturday on 5 December, Christmas Jumper Day on 10 December, the Royal Mail last posting days and the bank holidays) and two or three posts a week is plenty. Below is a December 2026 calendar, 20 post ideas by week, and copy-and-paste examples for trades, salons, cafés and shops.

## What are the key December 2026 dates?

| Date | What | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|
| Sat 5 Dec | [Small Business Saturday UK](/guides/small-business-saturday-ideas) | Everyone |
| Thu 10 Dec | [Christmas Jumper Day](https://www.savethechildren.org.uk/christmas-jumper-day) (Save the Children) | Teams, cafés, salons |
| Fri 18 Dec | Royal Mail 2nd Class last recommended posting day | Anyone posting gifts or orders |
| Sat 19 Dec | Royal Mail Tracked 48 last day | Online shops |
| Mon 21 Dec | Royal Mail 1st Class and Tracked 24 last day; shortest day | Online shops, cafés |
| Wed 23 Dec | Royal Mail Special Delivery last day | Online shops |
| Thu 24 Dec | Christmas Eve | Last opening, thank-you posts |
| Fri 25 Dec | Christmas Day (bank holiday) | Short scheduled greeting, or nothing |
| Mon 28 Dec | Boxing Day substitute bank holiday | Anyone open between Christmas and New Year |
| Thu 31 Dec | New Year's Eve | Hospitality, opening hours |
| Fri 1 Jan | New Year's Day (bank holiday) | Opening hours, January bookings |

The Royal Mail dates are the UK recommended last posting dates listed on the [Post Office's last posting dates page](https://www.postoffice.co.uk/last-posting-dates). Branches have their own final collection times, so check yours. The bank holidays are from [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/bank-holidays): Boxing Day falls on a Saturday this year, so the substitute day is Monday 28 December. In Scotland, Monday 4 January 2027 is also a bank holiday.

## What should you post in the first week of December?

The first week is about getting dates in people's heads before diaries fill up.

1. **Your last booking date before Christmas.** Salons, groomers, trades, mechanics, anyone with a diary.
2. **Christmas opening hours, early.** Post them now, and set them as special hours on Google too. Our guide to [Google Business Profile Christmas hours](/guides/google-business-profile-christmas-hours) walks through it.
3. **Small Business Saturday.** What you are doing on 5 December, or a shout-out to a neighbour.
4. **Gift cards.** If you sell them, say how to buy them and how long they last.
5. **The decorations going up.** A real photo of your shop, van or salon.

Example for a salon in Nottingham:

> Christmas appointments are filling up. Our last colour appointments before Christmas are on Wednesday 23 December, and cuts until 1pm on Christmas Eve. If you want a slot in the week before, book in the next few days. Gift cards are at the desk and on our website.

Example for a plumber in Mansfield:

> If you are hosting Christmas this year, now is the time to get the boiler checked, not on the 23rd. We are taking bookings up to Wednesday 23 December, then emergency call-outs only until 4 January. Book using the button below.

## What about the middle of December?

Mid-December is the busy stretch. Keep posts short and practical.

6. **Christmas Jumper Day on 10 December.** A team photo, and a donation if you are fundraising.
7. **Gift ideas from what you actually sell.** Three products under £20, with real photos and prices.
8. **Last posting dates.** If you post orders, tell customers your own cut-off, a day before Royal Mail's.
9. **A Christmas tip from your trade.** How to stop the outside tap freezing, how to keep a real tree fresh, how to make a blow-dry last for a party.
10. **A review from this year.** Quoted word for word, first name only.
11. **Behind the scenes in the rush.** The kitchen at 7am, the van loaded up, the order table.

Example for a gift shop in Leicester:

> Last orders for Christmas delivery: Thursday 17 December for standard post, Sunday 20 December for tracked. After that, you can still collect from the shop until 4pm on Christmas Eve. Free gift wrapping on everything, as always.

Example for an electrician:

> A Christmas tip from us: if you are plugging in more lights than usual, avoid daisy-chaining extension leads and keep an eye on any adaptor that feels warm. Indoor lights are not built for outdoor use either. Stay safe, and enjoy the lights.

Example for a café in Derby, Christmas Jumper Day:

> Jumpers on for Christmas Jumper Day. We are giving £1 from every hot chocolate today to Save the Children. Wear yours in and we will add a marshmallow.

## What should you post in the week of Christmas?

This is the week people check whether you are open, not the week they read long posts.

12. **Your hours, again.** A clear list: Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, Boxing Day, 28 December, New Year's Eve, New Year's Day.
13. **Last-minute availability.** "Two slots left on the 23rd." Only if true.
14. **Last day for collection.** For shops and bakeries with Christmas orders.
15. **Emergency cover.** Trades who do call-outs over Christmas should say exactly when and how.
16. **A thank you.** A short, personal post on Christmas Eve.

Example hours post:

> Our Christmas hours:
> Thursday 24 December: 9am to 2pm
> Friday 25 December to Monday 28 December: closed
> Tuesday 29 and Wednesday 30 December: 10am to 4pm
> Thursday 31 December: 10am to 2pm
> Friday 1 January: closed
> Normal hours from Saturday 2 January.

Example Christmas Eve thank you:

> That is us done for Christmas. Thank you to everyone who booked, bought, recommended us or left a review this year. We could not do it without you. Merry Christmas from all four of us, and see you on the 29th.

## And between Christmas and New Year?

The quiet week is useful. People are at home, scrolling and planning.

17. **Open between Christmas and New Year.** If you are, say so. It is a real reason to choose you.
18. **Your year in photos.** Four or five real photos from the year.
19. **January bookings.** The diary is open, and here is what January is good for.
20. **New Year's Eve hours** and a short "see you in 2027".

Example for a dog groomer:

> We are open 29 and 30 December if your dog needs a tidy after the Christmas muddy walks. January diary is open too, and our first free slots are on Monday 4 January.

## How many posts is that, and how long does it take?

A worked example. Twelve posts across December (three a week, plus hours and thank-you posts), on Google and Facebook. At 15 minutes each to write, find a photo and publish, that is 24 posts and 6 hours. If an owner's time is worth £40 an hour, that is £240 in the busiest month of the year.

You can cut that down:

- **Write the dated posts in one go.** Hours, last booking dates and the Christmas Eve thank you can all be written today and scheduled. That is roughly half the month.
- **Reuse the hours post.** Write it once, then post the same list in early December and again in Christmas week.
- **Let the everyday posts run themselves.** The other half (reviews, services, tips) does not need you. postd writes those from your own website and Google reviews, schedules them at sensible local times, and publishes to Google, Facebook, LinkedIn and X. You approve them, or let them go automatically, and spend your own 15 minutes on the hours post.

## What should you avoid at Christmas?

- **Stock festive graphics on every post.** They all look the same. Use your own photos.
- **Invented urgency.** "Only 3 left!" when there are thirty. UK consumer law covers urgency claims, and customers notice.
- **Forgetting Google hours.** A post saying "closed 25 to 28 December" does not change what Maps shows. Special hours do.
- **Going silent from 20 December to 5 January.** Schedule two or three posts so the page does not look abandoned.
- **Replying to nothing.** Christmas is when people message to ask if you are open. If you cannot reply for a few days, say so on your page.
