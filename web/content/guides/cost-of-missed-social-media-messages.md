A missed DM costs you whatever that customer would have spent, multiplied by how often it happens. The sum is simple: enquiries missed each week, times the share you would normally win, times your average job value. For a tradesperson missing three enquiries a week, winning one in four, with a £180 average job, that is about £135 a week or £7,000 a year. Add the hours spent checking four different apps, and slow replies are usually one of the most expensive habits in a small business.

There is no reliable UK statistic for how many messages small businesses miss, so this guide does not quote one. It gives you the sum, three worked examples with the assumptions stated, and a way to count your own.

## Why do small businesses miss messages?

Not because they do not care. The usual reasons are:

- **Too many places to check.** Facebook Page messages, Facebook comments, Instagram DMs, Instagram comments, TikTok comments, plus WhatsApp, email, Google and the phone.
- **Message requests hidden away.** On Instagram, messages from people who do not follow you can land in a requests folder that is easy to miss.
- **Comments on old posts.** A question under a post from three months ago does not feel urgent to anyone, so nobody sees it.
- **Being on the tools.** A plumber under a sink at 11:00 reads the message at 18:30, by which time the customer has booked someone else.
- **Several people, no system.** Everyone assumes someone else answered.
- **Holidays and evenings.** The Christmas message that sat unread until 4 January.

Customers rarely chase. They message the next business on the list.

## How do you work out what a missed message costs?

The basic sum:

> Missed enquiries a week x share you would normally win x average value = lost revenue a week

Then multiply by 52 for a year. Use your own numbers. Three examples follow, and every figure in them is an assumption for illustration, not data.

### A plumber in Mansfield

Assumptions:

- 12 enquiries a week by Facebook and Instagram message
- 3 a week are missed or answered more than a day later
- of enquiries answered promptly, 1 in 4 become a job
- average job value £180

Sum: 3 x 0.25 x £180 = **£135 a week**, or about **£7,000 a year**.

Even if you halve every assumption (1.5 missed a week, the same win rate, a £90 job) it is still £34 a week, or roughly £1,750 a year.

### A hair salon in Nottingham

A salon's sum works better with the value of a regular client than a single appointment.

Assumptions:

- 20 booking enquiries a week across Instagram and Facebook
- 2 a week go unanswered for more than a day
- 1 in 2 of those people would have booked
- average spend £55 a visit, and a new client who stays comes back 5 times a year
- 1 in 3 new clients becomes a regular

One missed enquiry is worth: 0.5 chance of booking x (£55 first visit, plus a 1 in 3 chance of 5 visits a year at £55, so about £92) = roughly £73.

Sum: 2 x £73 = **about £146 a week**, or roughly **£7,600 a year**.

### A café in Derby

A café's individual orders are small, so the costly messages are the bigger ones: group bookings, private hire and catering.

Assumptions:

- 2 group or catering enquiries a week by message
- 1 a month is missed
- 1 in 2 enquiries answered promptly would have gone ahead
- average group booking £150

Sum: 12 missed a year x 0.5 x £150 = **£900 a year** from one missed message a month.

## What does checking for messages cost in time?

The other cost is the owner's time spent looking. A worked example, with assumptions:

- 4 platforms to check: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp
- checked 8 times a day across the working day and evening
- about 2 minutes each time to open, scroll and see what is new, before replying to anything

That is 4 x 8 x 2 = 64 minutes a day, or about 7.5 hours a week over 7 days. At an owner's time worth £30 to £50 an hour, that is roughly **£225 to £375 a week** of looking, not replying. Your figure may be much lower, but it is worth timing yourself for a day.

## Is a slow reply as bad as no reply?

Often it is close. Local customers frequently message two or three businesses at once and book the first one that answers clearly. A reply the next afternoon to "can anyone come out today?" is effectively no reply.

The research on speed mostly comes from sales leads rather than social media. The best-known study, [The Short Life of Online Sales Leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) in Harvard Business Review (2011), looked at how fast US companies followed up web enquiries and concluded that most companies "are not responding nearly fast enough". It is old, American and not about Instagram, so treat it as a pointer. Your own messages are better evidence: look at the ones you answered late and see how many booked.

How fast you need to be depends on the message. We set out sensible targets for each type in [how fast should a business reply to Facebook and Instagram messages](/guides/how-fast-reply-to-facebook-messages).

## How do you count your own missed messages?

Ten minutes with a cup of tea:

1. Open each platform: Facebook Page inbox, Instagram DMs (including message requests), TikTok comments, and comments on your Facebook and Instagram posts.
2. Go back four weeks.
3. Count enquiries: anything asking about price, availability, booking or a service.
4. Mark each one: answered within a few hours, answered the next day or later, or never answered.
5. Divide the late and unanswered ones by four for a weekly figure.
6. Put that into the sum above with your own win rate and average value.

If the answer is close to zero, you are doing well and do not need to change anything. If it is not, you now know what fixing it is worth.
